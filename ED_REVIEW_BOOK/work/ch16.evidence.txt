The rest of his neurologic exam is normal. CT and MRI of the brain show no acute changes.
A: Abducens nerve palsy
EXPLANATION: This patient has a right abducens, or cranial nerve VI palsy. The abducens nerve is a pure motor nerve that supplies the lateral rectus muscle, controlling the abduction of the globe. Therefore a patient with a cranial nerve VI palsy would be unable to look laterally with the affected eye, resulting in diplopia. Patients often turn their head toward the affected side to help maintain binocularity. The most common cause of an isolated cranial VI nerve palsy is microvascular ischemia from diabetes or hypertension. Additional causes of cranial nerve VI palsies include intracavernous aneurysms, subarachnoid hemorrhage, giant cell arteritis, tumors, or infections.
