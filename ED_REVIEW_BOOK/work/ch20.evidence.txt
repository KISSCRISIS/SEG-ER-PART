EXPLANATION: The Parkland formula is the most widely used burn formula to calculate the amount of fluid required to adequately resuscitate a burn victim in the first 24 hours. Q: A 19-year-old man presents to the ED in respiratory distress. He has a penetrating wound to the left of his sternum where his partner stabbed him with a screwdriver during an altercation. EXPLANATION: Pericardial tamponade is a condition caused by the rapid accumulation of fluid within the pericardial space, leading to hypotension. Q: An 8-year-old boy presents by private vehicle after being impaled by a rod after a fall from a fence. He is struggling to breathe, with a sucking upper left chest wound. A: Put a three-sided occlusive dressing over the wound
EXPLANATION: Blast injuries, severe avulsion, and impalement are the most common causes of an open pneumothorax. During an open pneumothorax, spontaneous breathing causes air to be sucked from the room into the chest because of the decrease in intrathoracic pressure during inspiration. Not only does the lung collapse, but air trapping can result in a tension pneumothorax, shifting mediastinal structures away from the wound. Placing a three-sided, tight occlusive dressing over the wound prevents air from entering from the environment and prevents tension within the chest from developing and from worsening while the patient is still spontaneously breathing.
