## High-Yield Exam Review

- Multiple sexual partners is the most common risk factor for pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) and for tubo-ovarian abscess (TOA) [PEER-PH].
- Chlamydia trachomatis is the most common cause of PID and of cervicitis. Roughly 50% of PID is caused by C. trachomatis and Neisseria gonorrhoeae, 35% by bacterial vaginosis-associated pathogens, and 15% by respiratory and enteric organisms [PEER-PH].
- PID is diagnosed clinically: cervical motion tenderness, uterine tenderness, or adnexal tenderness in a sexually active patient with lower abdominal or pelvic pain when no other etiology is identified [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- 2021 CDC outpatient PID regimen: single-dose ceftriaxone 500 mg IM plus doxycycline 100 mg PO plus metronidazole 500 mg PO twice daily, for 14 days [PEER-PH].
- Cervicitis is treated empirically for both gonorrhoea and chlamydia without waiting for confirmation: ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once plus doxycycline 100 mg PO twice daily for 7 days [PEER-PH].
- Pregnancy is an indication for hospital admission in PID; so are fever, vomiting, abscess, sepsis, inability to tolerate oral therapy, and failure to improve within 72 hours of outpatient treatment [PEER-PH].
- An intrauterine device (IUD) is left in place in PID and does not change the antibiotic regimen [PEER-PH].
- Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome is right upper quadrant pain with normal liver function tests and a normal right upper quadrant ultrasound in a patient with PID findings [PEER-PH].
- Perihepatitis is far more common in adolescents — 27% in adolescents with PID versus 4% to 13.8% in adults [PEER-PH].
- TOA requires admission for at least the first 24 hours of intravenous antibiotics, then at least 14 days of broad-spectrum coverage [PEER-PH].
- Ectopic pregnancy is the third leading cause of maternal death; 90% of patients present with abdominal pain [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Any woman of reproductive age with unexplained syncope and hypotension is presumed to have an ectopic pregnancy until proven otherwise [ROSH].
- The discriminatory zone for transvaginal ultrasound is a beta-hCG of 1,500 to 2,000 IU/L [PEER-PH].
- A gestational sac appears at approximately 5 weeks with a beta-hCG of 1,500–2,000 IU/L; a yolk sac at 5–6 weeks with 2,000–2,500 IU/L; a fetal pole at approximately 5,000 IU/L [ROSH].
- Only an intrauterine fetal pole with a yolk sac confirms an intrauterine pregnancy; the double decidual sign raises the likelihood but does not confirm [ROSH].
- Pregnancy of unknown location: repeat the quantitative beta-hCG in 48 hours. In a normal early pregnancy beta-hCG rises by at least 50–66% over 48 hours [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Single-dose methotrexate is effective in 85% of ectopic pregnancies, and is used when beta-hCG is less than 5,000 mIU/mL. Fetal heart activity, hemodynamic instability, rupture on ultrasound, and kidney insufficiency are contraindications [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Painless third-trimester bleeding is placenta previa until excluded by ultrasound, and speculum and digital pelvic examination are avoided until placental location is known [ROSH].
- Painful bleeding with a tender, contracting uterus and abnormal fetal heart tones is placental abruption; prior abruption is the strongest risk factor, with an odds ratio of 25.8 [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Maternal cocaine use carries up to a 10% risk of abruption when used in the third trimester [PEER-PH].
- Uterine rupture is a complete division of all three uterine layers; prior cesarean section is the most common etiology and sudden fetal bradycardia is the single predictable fetal heart tracing finding [PEER-PH].
- Uterine physical examination distinguishes rupture from abruption: the ruptured uterus is flaccid and oddly shaped, whereas the abruption uterus contracts vigorously and continuously [PEER-PH].
- Eclampsia is treated with magnesium sulfate 4–6 g IV over 20 minutes followed by an infusion [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Severe hypertension in pregnancy is treated with labetalol, hydralazine, nifedipine, or nicardipine after confirming the blood pressure; magnesium sulfate is added for seizure prophylaxis [ROSH].
- HELLP syndrome spares the coagulation cascade: fibrinogen, PT, and PTT are typically normal while LDH, AST, and bilirubin rise and platelets fall [PEER-PH].
- Uterine atony is the most common cause of postpartum hemorrhage, responsible for 75–90% of cases; retained placenta is the most common cause of delayed postpartum hemorrhage [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Cesarean delivery is the strongest risk factor for postpartum endometritis, which presents on postpartum day 2 or 3 with fever, uterine tenderness, and foul-smelling lochia [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Staphylococcus aureus is the most common organism in mastitis; cephalexin or dicloxacillin is first-line for uncomplicated disease [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Umbilical cord prolapse is an indication for emergent cesarean section; the immediate action is manual elevation of the presenting fetal part [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Shoulder dystocia is managed first with the McRoberts maneuver — hyperflexion and abduction of the maternal hips with suprapubic pressure [PEER-PH].
- A copper IUD is the most effective emergency contraception, with a pregnancy risk of 0.1%, and is effective up to 5 days after unprotected intercourse [ROSH].

## Core Concepts

### The pelvic inflammatory disease spectrum
- PID is a spectrum of ascending infection of the upper female reproductive tract: salpingitis, endometritis, oophoritis, tubo-ovarian abscess, and pelvic peritonitis [ROSH].
- It is the most common serious infection in sexually active women aged 16–25 in the United States [ROSH].
- Infection may extend to neighbouring structures as periappendicitis, perihepatitis (Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome), and pelvic peritonitis [ROSH].
- Risk factors: multiple sexual partners, age 15–25, previous PID, and a partner with a sexually transmitted infection (STI) [PEER-PH].
- Most cases arise from sexually transmitted or bacterial vaginosis-related pathogens. A minority are caused by enteric or respiratory organisms including Escherichia coli, Bacteroides fragilis, group B streptococci, Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and Campylobacter species [ROSH].
- The consequences are reproductive: tubal-factor infertility from destruction of the ciliated epithelial cells of the fallopian tubes, ectopic pregnancy, pyosalpinx, hydrosalpinx, pyometra, adhesions, and TOA [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Because of these sequelae, the CDC recommends a low threshold to treat presumed PID [PEER-PH].
- Consider PID in sexually active transgender men, who may still have natal reproductive organs [PEER-PH].

### Cervicitis and genital ulcer disease
- Cervicitis is infection of the lower female genital tract. C. trachomatis is the most common cause, followed by N. gonorrhoeae, and coinfection is common, so both are treated empirically [PEER-PH].
- Risk factors for cervicitis: multiple sexual partners, prior STI, concurrent HIV infection, and age under 25 [ROSH].
- Findings include mucoid or mucopurulent discharge, post-coital bleeding, cervical friability, and cervical motion tenderness — the highest-sensitivity finding [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Genital herpes is usually caused by herpes simplex virus type 2 (HSV-2), though HSV-1 is increasingly implicated. Primary infection is the most severe; recurrences are common but milder and shorter [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Human papillomavirus (HPV) is detected in 99.7% of cervical cancers. Types 16 and 18 are the subtypes most associated with squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma of the cervix; types 6 and 11 cause condyloma acuminata [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Vertical transmission of genital herpes is associated with neonatal meningoencephalitis [ROSH].

### Vaginitis and vulvovaginitis
- The three most common causes of vaginitis are trichomoniasis, Candida vulvovaginitis, and bacterial vaginosis (BV) [ROSH].
- BV is the most common cause of abnormal vaginal discharge in the United States. It is not classified as an STI but may facilitate transmission or acquisition of other STIs, including HIV [ROSH].
- Candida vaginitis is the second most common cause of vaginitis, with Candida albicans accounting for about 90% of cases [ROSH].
- Trichomonas vaginalis is a flagellated protozoan and the most prevalent nonviral STI worldwide; only 10–20% of infected women develop symptoms [ROSH].
- Noninfectious causes are important: vaginal washes, retained tampons or condoms, contact irritants, and atrophic vaginitis in postmenopausal women [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- In postmenopausal women with bleeding, hypoestrogenism causes a thinned, friable vaginal endothelium prone to light bleeding [ROSH].

### Early pregnancy bleeding
- Spontaneous abortion is pregnancy loss before 20 weeks, or loss of a fetus weighing less than 500 g. It is the most common complication of early pregnancy and is most often caused by fetal chromosomal abnormalities [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Classification depends on the cervical os and on whether products have passed: threatened (closed os with bleeding), inevitable (open os with bleeding), incomplete (open os with fetal tissue in the os or vault), complete (closed os with products already passed), missed (fetal death with a closed os and retention of the pregnancy), and septic [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Ectopic pregnancy is implantation outside the uterine cavity. The ampullary fallopian tube is the most common site, but cervical, ovarian, intra-abdominal, and previous cesarean scar implantation all occur [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Pregnancy of unknown location is a distinct category: a positive pregnancy test with no intrauterine or extrauterine pregnancy identified on transvaginal ultrasound [PEER-PH][ROSH].

### Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy
- Preeclampsia is new hypertension after 20 weeks of gestation with proteinuria or other evidence of end-organ damage, and it may present up to 6 weeks postpartum [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Eclampsia is preeclampsia complicated by seizures and is the ultimate complication of pregnancy-induced hypertension [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- HELLP syndrome — hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelets — is a severe manifestation of preeclampsia and a peripartum emergency occurring between 20 weeks of gestation and 4 weeks postpartum [PEER-PH].
- Risk factors for preeclampsia include nulliparity, multifetal gestation, obesity, diabetes mellitus, and lower socioeconomic status [PEER-PH][ROSH].

### Third-trimester bleeding
- Placenta previa, placental abruption, and uterine rupture are the three cannot-miss causes. Previa is painless; abruption is painful with a contracting uterus; rupture is painful with a flaccid, abnormally shaped uterus or a fetal part palpable outside the uterus [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- The first imaging step is pelvic ultrasound to localise the placenta. Transvaginal ultrasound is superior to transabdominal and is considered safe [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Any instrumentation or digital examination in suspected previa or uterine rupture can provoke severe exsanguination and must be undertaken with extreme caution [ROSH].

### Postpartum complications
- The four Ts of postpartum hemorrhage: tone, trauma, tissue, and thrombin [ROSH].
- Primary hemorrhage occurs within 24 hours of delivery and is usually uterine atony. Delayed hemorrhage occurs from 24 hours to 6 weeks and is most often retained placenta [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Postpartum endometritis is the most common puerperal infection and usually develops on postpartum day 2 or 3 [ROSH].
- Amniotic fluid embolism is a rare, life-threatening complication with no single first-line treatment; care is supportive [PEER-PH].

### Intrapartum emergencies
- Umbilical cord prolapse, shoulder dystocia, and nuchal cord are the delivery emergencies most likely to be encountered in the emergency department [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- The seven cardinal movements of labour — engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, external rotation, expulsion — occur during the second stage [ROSH].

### Adnexal and vulvar emergencies
- Ovarian torsion threatens the viability of the ovary and the patient's fertility; a mobile ovarian mass such as a cyst is the most significant risk factor [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- A Bartholin cyst results from obstruction of the Bartholin gland ducts at the 4 and 8 o'clock positions relative to the vaginal opening; abscesses are usually polymicrobial [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome is a complication of assisted reproductive technology, estimated to complicate 3–20% of cycles [ROSH].
