## Diagnosis

### Blood gas and acid-base interpretation

- Read the gas in order: pH (acidemia below 7.35, alkalemia above 7.45), then pCO2 (respiratory, normal 35–45 mm Hg), then bicarbonate (metabolic, normal 21–27 mEq/L). [ROSH]
- Anion gap = sodium − (chloride + bicarbonate); normal is about 12 mEq/L. High-gap causes follow the MUDPILES mnemonic. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Winters formula: expected pCO2 = (1.5 × bicarbonate) + 8 ± 2. A measured pCO2 below the predicted range indicates an added primary respiratory alkalosis. [ROSH]
- Delta ratio = (measured anion gap − 12) / (24 − measured bicarbonate); a ratio above 2.0 indicates a mixed high-gap metabolic acidosis with metabolic alkalosis. [ROSH]

| Pattern | pH | pCO2 | Bicarbonate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metabolic acidosis | 7.33 | 30 mm Hg (compensatory fall) | 15 mEq/L |
| Metabolic alkalosis | above 7.45 | raised | raised |
| Acute respiratory alkalosis | 7.50 | 25 mm Hg | 23 mEq/L |
| Acute respiratory acidosis | low | above 45 mm Hg | normal to high |
| Chronic respiratory acidosis | near normal | high | high (compensated) |
| Mixed metabolic and respiratory acidosis | 7.25 | 65 mm Hg | 14 mEq/L |

- Alveolar hypoventilation from decreased respiratory rate, decreased tidal volume or increased dead space produces hypercapnia above 45 mm Hg; opioid toxicity acts through decreased respiratory drive. [ROSH]

### Adrenal insufficiency and cortisol excess

- Primary adrenal insufficiency: hyponatremia in about 90%, hyperkalemia in about 60%, plus hypoglycaemia and skin hyperpigmentation; adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) is elevated because the rest of the axis is intact. Hyperkalemia is what separates primary from secondary disease. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Consider adrenal crisis in vasopressor-refractory septic shock, after abrupt corticosteroid withdrawal, and in a child with known congenital adrenal hyperplasia who develops infection. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Cushing syndrome is screened with a 24-hour urinary cortisol or a low-dose dexamethasone suppression test: dexamethasone at night with a morning cortisol, where adequate suppression excludes the diagnosis. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- In children, excess cortisol suppresses growth hormone, testosterone and oestrogen, causing growth arrest and delayed puberty — unlike simple dietary obesity, in which children are tall and grow rapidly. [ROSH]
- Adrenal incidentalomas occur in up to 5% of the general population and 10% of older patients; nearly all are benign and nonfunctioning, and increased mass vascularity raises concern for malignancy. [ROSH]

### Diabetes: DKA versus HHS

| Feature | DKA | HHS |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose | usually above 250–300 mg/dL | above 600 mg/dL, often above 1000 mg/dL |
| pH | below 7.30 | above 7.30 |
| Bicarbonate and anion gap | low bicarbonate, elevated anion gap | normal bicarbonate and anion gap |
| Ketones | present | trace or absent |
| Serum sodium | low or normal | more likely elevated |
| Fluid deficit | moderate | 8–12 L average |
| Neurologic features | uncommon at presentation | common; follow hyperosmolarity |

- Neurologic features of HHS — obtundation, hemiparesis, seizures — follow the degree of hyperosmolarity and typically appear above a plasma osmolality of 330 mmol/kg. [PEER-PHYS]
- Euglycaemic DKA is defined by glucose above 70 mg/dL and below 250 mg/dL and is strongly associated with sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors such as empagliflozin and canagliflozin. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Diabetes can be diagnosed in the ED in a symptomatic patient with a random plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or more. [ROSH]

### Thyroid disease

- Thyroid storm is a clinical diagnosis supported by a low TSH with elevated free T4 and T3; in the Burch and Wartofsky score, central nervous system dysfunction carries the greatest diagnostic weight and coma scores highest at 30 points. [ROSH]
- Myxedema coma: hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension, altered mentation and hyponatremia; mortality approaches 30% with treatment and near 100% without it. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Nonthyroidal illness syndrome: low T3 with normal or slightly increased reverse T3 while T4 and TSH remain within normal limits. [ROSH]
- A thyroid nodule is the most common presentation of thyroid carcinoma; medullary thyroid carcinoma is associated with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A and 2B. [ROSH]

### Pituitary disease

- Pituitary apoplexy presents with abrupt headache and visual disturbance and mimics subarachnoid haemorrhage; pregnancy and a known adenoma are risk factors. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Visual field loss from a nonfunctioning adenoma, and galactorrhoea with amenorrhoea from a suprasellar adenoma, are the classic mass-effect presentations; bitemporal hemianopsia indicates chiasmal compression. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Arginine vasopressin deficiency shows a high serum osmolality with a low urine osmolality; a water deprivation test evaluates paediatric polydipsia. [ROSH]

### Pheochromocytoma

- Diagnosis is by plasma or urinary fractionated metanephrines. Headache is the most common symptom, present in 90% of symptomatic patients; the tumour may also present as an adrenal or renal mass. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]

### Electrolyte and vitamin diagnosis

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Peaked T waves at potassium 5.5–6.5 mEq/L | Hyperkalemia |
| P wave flattening, PR prolongation, loss of P waves at 6.5–7.0 mEq/L | Worsening hyperkalemia |
| Sine wave | Severe hyperkalemia |
| U waves with QT prolongation | Hypokalemia |
| Chvostek and Trousseau signs, QT prolongation | Hypocalcaemia |
| Hypocalcaemia with hyperphosphataemia | Hypoparathyroidism |
| Magnesium above 3 mg/dL; loss of deep tendon reflexes at 4–5 mg/dL; life-threatening above 5 mg/dL | Hypermagnesaemia |

- A calcium level above 14 mg/dL is life-threatening. Severe hypophosphataemia is a level below 1 mg/dL, and hyperkalemia may be spurious when the sample is haemolysed. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Wernicke encephalopathy is a clinical diagnosis requiring two of: dietary deficiency, oculomotor abnormality, cerebellar dysfunction, altered mental status or memory impairment. [ROSH]
- Pellagra shows the 3 Ds with a Casal necklace; scurvy shows perifollicular haemorrhages; vitamin B12 deficiency shows a macrocytic anaemia with MCV 108–115 fL and subacute combined degeneration with a positive Romberg sign. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Refeeding syndrome should be anticipated with lack of food access, advanced age, depression, anorexia nervosa and bariatric surgery. [ROSH]

## Management

### Adrenal crisis

1. Give hydrocortisone — it has both glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid activity and is available intravenously. [PEER-PHYS]
2. In the ambulatory steroid-dependent patient, sick-day rules increase prednisone from 5 mg to 10 mg orally for 3 days. [ROSH]
3. Treat coexisting infection and volume depletion. [PEER-PHYS]

### Diabetic ketoacidosis

1. Check the potassium before starting an insulin drip; delay insulin when the potassium is below 3.3 mEq/L. [PEER-PHYS]
2. Begin isotonic crystalloid; replace potassium once the level falls below 5.3–5.5 mEq/L.
3. Start insulin at 0.05–0.1 units/kg/hr; if glucose is not falling by 50–70 mg/dL/hr, reassess and escalate.
4. When glucose falls toward normal, change to D5 0.45% normal saline with insulin 0.02–0.05 units/kg/hr to clear ketones while avoiding hypoglycaemia.
5. In a child with a metabolic acidosis consistent with DKA, give D10 with 0.5% normal saline at 1.5 times maintenance.
6. Sodium bicarbonate is not routine; it shifts potassium into cells and can cause hypokalemia.
7. Avoid overaggressive correction — cerebral oedema is the most serious complication of paediatric DKA treatment.
8. Look for the precipitant: medication noncompliance is usual, with infection, pregnancy, myocardial infarction, hyperthyroidism, substance misuse and insulin pump failure. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]

### Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state

1. The first treatment step is isotonic crystalloid; the average fluid deficit is 8–12 L. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
2. Correct hyperglycaemia and hyperosmolarity gradually, monitoring osmolality rather than glucose alone. [PEER-PHYS]
3. Hunt for the precipitant — infection, particularly pneumonia and urinary tract infection, is the most common; thiazides are a pharmacological trigger. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]

### Hypoglycaemia

1. In a conscious patient, give a meal containing complex carbohydrate, fat and protein — a turkey and cheese sandwich contains 200–300 calories — because simple sugars correct the glucose only transiently. [PEER-PHYS]
2. In a lethargic child, give D10 2.5–5 mL/kg at 2–3 mL/min. [ROSH]
3. In a 1 kg preterm neonate, 5 mL of 10% dextrose is the correction volume. [ROSH]
4. Glucagon drives hepatic glycogenolysis through the cyclic adenosine monophosphate pathway and is useful in beta-blocker overdose; nausea is a dose-dependent side effect. [ROSH]
5. Sulfonylurea hypoglycaemia from glipizide or glyburide persists beyond 24 hours after overdose; observe for at least 24 hours. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
6. Diazoxide suppresses insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells and is used in insulinoma. [PEER-PHYS]
7. Check glucose before head CT in any suspected stroke — hypoglycaemia is a well-recognised mimic with focal deficits including hemiplegia. [PEER-PHYS]

### Thyroid storm and myxedema coma

1. Order of treatment in thyroid storm: beta-blocker (propranolol) first, then a thionamide, then iodine given at least 1 hour later; add hydrocortisone. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
2. Propranolol partially blocks peripheral conversion of T4 to T3; propylthiouracil both inhibits new hormone synthesis and decreases peripheral conversion; iodine inhibits release of stored hormone and must follow the thionamide because it is also substrate for hormone production. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
3. Corticosteroids decrease peripheral conversion of T4 to T3. Salicylates are contraindicated because they interfere with protein binding of T3 and T4 and increase free hormone. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
4. Myxedema coma is treated with intravenous levothyroxine, hydrocortisone and a normal saline bolus. Avoid amiodarone in hypothyroidism. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]

### Pheochromocytoma

1. Alpha blockade always precedes beta blockade. Phentolamine is first-line for hypertensive emergency; a beta-blocker given first causes unopposed alpha stimulation and worsens hypertension. [PEER-PHYS]
2. Glucocorticoids are contraindicated because they stimulate catecholamine release and can precipitate hypertensive crisis. [PEER-PHYS]

### Hypercalcaemia

1. Isotonic saline is the first step. Restoring intravascular volume alone usually lowers calcium by 1.6–2.4 mg/dL. [ROSH]
2. Add a loop diuretic only after volume is restored, in hypercalcaemic crisis with volume depletion. [ROSH]
3. Calcitonin acts within hours but tachyphylaxis develops at 48 hours; bisphosphonates such as zoledronic acid and pamidronate peak at about 2 days. [ROSH]
4. Emergent haemodialysis is indicated in hypercalcaemia with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction. [ROSH]

### Hyperkalemia

1. Intravenous calcium is given first to stabilise the myocardium; it does not change the serum potassium level. Calcium gluconate 1 g over 3 minutes is equivalent to calcium chloride given centrally, where 3 g of gluconate equals 1 g of chloride. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
2. Shift potassium intracellularly with insulin and dextrose.
3. Dialysis removes potassium and is required in renal failure. [ROSH]
4. Address the cause — rhabdomyolysis, haemolysed samples, adrenal insufficiency. [ROSH]

### Hyponatremia and hypernatremia

1. Hypertonic 3% saline 100 mL over 10 minutes, stopping once symptoms improve. [PEER-PHYS]
2. Avoid correction by more than 4–6 mEq/L acutely and not more than 8 mEq/L in the first 24 hours; the maximum correction is 12 mEq/L per 24 hours. [PEER-PHYS]
3. 3% saline at 25–100 mL/hr with furosemide 20–40 mg IV allows controlled correction. [PEER-PHYS]
4. Chronic hyponatremia beyond 48 hours carries the greatest risk of osmotic demyelination.
5. In hypernatremia above 150 mEq/L, consider salt poisoning and over-dilute formula in infants as causes.

### Pituitary and nutrition

1. Pituitary apoplexy is treated with steroids and an urgent neurosurgical consultation; high-dose intravenous dexamethasone is given. [PEER-PHYS]
2. Wernicke encephalopathy is treated with intravenous thiamine before or with glucose; diagnosis by response to thiamine supports the clinical suspicion. [ROSH]
3. Korsakoff syndrome recovers in about 21% of patients, and long-term supervision is often required. [PEER-PHYS]
4. Starvation ketosis is managed with fluids, thiamine, folic acid and a multivitamin. [ROSH]
5. Refeeding syndrome is managed by correcting hypophosphataemia, hypokalemia and hypomagnesaemia, which otherwise cause dysrhythmias, heart failure, pericardial effusions and cardiorespiratory failure. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
