## Diagnosis

### Bedside discriminators

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Dry skin, dry mucosa, urinary retention, reduced bowel sounds | Anticholinergic |
| Wet, warm, flushed skin with increased bowel sounds | Sympathomimetic |
| Clonus, lower-extremity hyperreflexia, myoclonus | Serotonin syndrome |
| Lead-pipe rigidity, hyporeflexia, no clonus | Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) |
| Miosis, bronchorrhea, lacrimation, fasciculations, wheezing | Cholinergic |
| Miosis, respiratory depression, absent bowel sounds | Opioid |
| Opisthotonos, awake seizure with preserved sensorium | Strychnine |
| Hot-and-cold temperature reversal | Ciguatera |
| Synesthesia, dilated pupils, piloerection | Lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD) |
| Talking to buildings, "waving shadows", clouded sensorium | Phencyclidine |
| Fluctuating consciousness with apparent apnea, midsize pupils | Gamma-hydroxybutyrate (GHB) |
| Cyanosis with pulse oximetry near 85% | Methemoglobinemia |
| Bitter almond odor | Cyanide |
| Garlic or metallic taste within 30–60 minutes | Arsenic |
| Optic disc hyperemia and retinal edema | Methanol |
| Visual loss, scotomata, red-green color blindness | Ethambutol |

### Exposure history that names the toxin

- Headache, nausea and dizziness in several household members or pets, or a patient found in a garage, at a faulty heater or with a running vehicle, points to carbon monoxide; fire victims have both carbon monoxide and cyanide exposure. [ROSH]
- Occupational insecticide exposure, or an unknown gas with miosis and copious secretions, indicates organophosphate or nerve agent. Blistering dermal burns with blood-filled endotracheal tubes indicate mustard; delayed dyspnea with diffuse crackles suggests phosgene. [ROSH]
- Wood preservatives with headache, drowsiness and a garlic taste indicate arsenic. Teething gel or recent mucosal injury with cyanosis indicates benzocaine-induced methemoglobinemia, as do dapsone, amyl nitrite, nitroprusside, phenazopyridine, quinine and sulfonamides. [ROSH]
- Immigration with treatment for a lung infection points to isoniazid, ethambutol or rifampin. A medication added within days points to serotonin syndrome; a newly started antipsychotic suggests NMS. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Grouper, barracuda or red snapper with ataxia suggests ciguatera. Salami with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor causes hypertensive crisis within about 60 minutes. Symptoms within 2 hours of mushroom ingestion predict a benign course; after 6 hours, a serious Amanita course. [ROSH]

### Electrocardiography

- Tricyclic antidepressants block sodium channels, widening the QRS beyond 100 ms with a dominant terminal R wave in lead aVR, tachycardia and a terminal rightward axis; class I antidysrhythmics do the same. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Digoxin gives scooped ST segments (Salvador Dali sign), PR prolongation and U waves; bidirectional ventricular tachycardia is classic. Diphenhydramine also widens the QRS, so monitor at least 6 hours even when asymptomatic. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Propranolol gives sinus bradycardia, first-degree atrioventricular block, QRS 127 ms, QTc 501 ms, seizure and hypotension; sotalol prolongs the QT and risks torsades de pointes. [PEER-PH]
- A wide-complex dysrhythmia with a wet rag or inhaled paint indicates a halogenated hydrocarbon, through myocardial sensitization to catecholamines. Lithium flattens and inverts T waves and prolongs the QT; organophosphates may prolong the QT and cause bradycardia. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Withdrawal

- Opioid withdrawal is piloerection, diarrhea, tachycardia, mydriasis, rhinorrhea and yawning; constipation and miosis indicate intoxication instead. The Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS) is validated for adults. [PEER-PH]

## Management

### Universal sequence

1. Support airway, breathing and circulation. For opioid toxicity the goal of naloxone is adequate respiratory function, not normal mental status. [ROSH]
2. For a suspected nerve agent, decontaminate before the patient enters the facility — remove clothing and wash with soap and water — and don personal protective equipment. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
3. Establish the time, the substance and the amount ingested. Poison Help Line: 1-800-222-1222. [PEER-PH]
4. Give activated charcoal to the alert patient with a toxin still in the stomach, most useful within about an hour; it is contraindicated with caustics and not useful for alcohols or metals. Do not intubate solely to give charcoal. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Time-critical pathways

1. **Cholinergic crisis** — decontaminate, atropine to dry secretions, pralidoxime second; physostigmine is contraindicated. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
2. **Sodium channel blockade** — sodium bicarbonate bolus and infusion for tricyclic-induced QRS widening; charcoal if alert. [ROSH]
3. **Calcium channel blocker** — fluids, calcium, vasopressors; norepinephrine when calcium and atropine fail; high-dose insulin first-line; lipid emulsion as rescue. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
4. **Beta-blocker** — high-dose glucagon first-line plus high-dose insulin with dextrose; bicarbonate for propranolol-induced sodium channel blockade. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
5. **Sulfonylurea** — dextrose and octreotide; monitor 12–24 hours because hypoglycemia recurs. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
6. **Digoxin** — digoxin immune Fab; in chronic toxicity treat by arrhythmia and clinical status, not level or potassium. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
7. **Isoniazid** — pyridoxine plus lorazepam terminates the seizure by restoring gamma-aminobutyric acid; intubate for aspiration risk. [ROSH]
8. **Cyanide and carbon monoxide** — hydroxocobalamin for cyanide; 100% oxygen by nonrebreather for carbon monoxide, with hyperbaric oxygen best within 6 hours. Indications: seizure, syncope, age > 35, loss of consciousness, abnormal cerebellar examination, carboxyhemoglobin > 25%, prolonged exposure. Serial carboxyhemoglobin measurements are unnecessary. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
9. **Salicylate** — correct fluid and acid-base deficits, bicarbonate to urine pH 7.5–8.0, charcoal, and dialysis for altered mentation, level ≥ 100 mg/dL, level > 90 mg/dL with renal impairment, hypoxemia or failure of standard therapy. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
10. **Acetaminophen** — charcoal within hours, a 4-hour level on the nomogram, then N-acetylcysteine; the intravenous form most often causes an anaphylactoid reaction. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
11. **Serotonin syndrome and neuroleptic malignant syndrome** — benzodiazepines, hydration, cooling and cyproheptadine for moderate to severe serotonin syndrome; stop the offending agent, give lorazepam, cool aggressively and give fluids for NMS. [ROSH]
12. **Sympathomimetics and hallucinogens** — benzodiazepines first; nitrates then phentolamine 1–5 mg intravenously for refractory cocaine hypertension; avoid beta-blockers and antipsychotics; measure core temperature and remove restraints early. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
13. **Opioid withdrawal** — clonidine after checking blood pressure and heart rate; buprenorphine is safer than methadone when QT-prolonging drugs are present. [PEER-PH]
14. **Caustic and hydrocarbon ingestion** — alkalis cause liquefaction necrosis with deep esophageal injury, acids coagulative necrosis sparing the esophagus; after a mouthful of bleach with no lesions, observe and give an oral challenge before discharge. For hydrocarbons, observe 6 hours then radiograph the chest, discharging the asymptomatic patient with a normal film. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Disposition

- Admit for inpatient chelation when lead poisoning is symptomatic at any level or the level exceeds 70; intensive care for encephalopathy. [PEER-PH]
- Admit for 24-hour observation after a single 5 mg glipizide tablet in a child, even with a normal glucose. Observe 4 hours after 40 tablets of 200 mg ibuprofen in a 70 kg adult, and on telemetry after citalopram overdose with a normal ECG. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Elemental mercury ingestion by a child with a normal examination can be discharged home; glow-stick contents are nontoxic. Gamma-hydroxybutyrate coma resolves over 3–4 hours, and patients can be discharged once the drug wears off. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
