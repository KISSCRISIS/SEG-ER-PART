## Diagnosis

### Triage and Acuity Assignment

- The Emergency Severity Index (ESI) is a five-level triage system based on acuity and resource needs. Level 1 requires immediate life-saving intervention. Level 2 is an ill-appearing patient with a potential high-risk diagnosis, confusion or disorientation, or severe pain and distress - a sudden severe headache rated 10/10 with no focal deficit qualifies. Levels 3 to 5 are assigned by the resources needed. [ROSH]
- Mass-casualty triage uses START: immediate (unable to follow simple commands) is the highest priority, dead (apnoeic despite an airway) the lowest, minor (ambulatory) is delayed, and delayed (normal vitals, follows commands) can wait. [PEER-PH]

### Recognising Bias-Driven and Anchoring Error

- Anchoring bias overemphasises particular history and examination findings, speeding care but increasing missed diagnoses. Repeated visits, demands for intravenous narcotics, homelessness, and illicit substance use can create a stereotype that ignores objective findings such as fever and an unstable gait, which warrant imaging and laboratory studies rather than discharge. [ROSH]
- Gender bias in acute coronary syndrome: women are less often diagnosed with myocardial infarction, less often catheterised, and given fewer cardiac medications despite identical features - attributing a woman's epigastric pain to anxiety despite an elevated troponin. The HEART score does not include gender. [PEER-PH]
- Implicit bias is subconscious; it lowers patient satisfaction and therefore adherence to medical recommendations. [PEER-PH]

### Recognising Impairment, Burnout, and Distress in Colleagues

| Finding | Suggests |
| Erratic behaviour, lateness, yelling at staff, drinking in a car after a lawsuit; guilt and sleeping difficulties | Second victim syndrome [PEER-PH] |
| Poor hygiene, arriving late, emotional lability, increased work-related errors | Impairment [ROSH] |
| Negative attitude towards patients, detachment, exhaustion, reduced accomplishment | Burnout - depersonalisation and emotional exhaustion [PEER-PH] |
| Difficulty sleeping, irritability, poor short-term memory, excessive fatigue on night shifts | Shift work sleep disorder [PEER-PH] |

### Assessment Instruments and Prognostic Tools

| Tool | Use |
| Identification of Seniors at Risk (ISAR) | Functional status of every geriatric ED patient [ROSH] |
| Kingston Caregiver Stress Scale | Caregiver stress and strain in dementia [PEER-PH] |
| Beers criteria | Potentially inappropriate medications in older adults - glyburide and glimepiride carry an increased risk of severe prolonged hypoglycaemia [ROSH] |
| ECOG performance status | Functional status before chemotherapy; ECOG 3 or higher (in bed more than 50% of waking hours) means the patient is too unwell to continue [PEER-PH] |

- Older patients account for almost 50% of hospital admissions, use twice as many laboratory and imaging resources, and are more likely to sustain an adverse drug event leading to unnecessary hospitalisation. [ROSH]
- Capacity to make a treatment decision can be assessed by any physician. [PEER-PH]
- Gender dysphoria (DSM-5) is distress from incongruence between birth sex and gender identity; transmasculine describes a person assigned female at birth with a masculine identity. [PEER-PH]

### Test Interpretation and Biostatistics

| Term | Definition |
| Sensitivity / specificity | Proportion of people with the disease who test positive / without the disease who test negative |
| Positive predictive value (PPV) | Proportion of positive results that are truly positive; depends on prevalence [PEER-PH] |
| Negative predictive value (NPV) | Probability that a patient with a negative result truly does not have the disease [ROSH] |

- Before a test is ordered, sensitivity and specificity are most useful; once the result is available, predictive values are more useful. Screening tests ideally have high sensitivity, sometimes at the expense of specificity. [ROSH]
- Of 32 children with a positive rapid influenza test, 24 truly had influenza - PPV 75%. A negative D-dimer is interpreted through the NPV, built from true negatives and false negatives. [ROSH]
- In a normal distribution, 1 standard deviation (SD) contains 68.2% of values, 2 SD contain 95.4%, and 3 SD contain 99.7%; a mean glucose of 95 mg/dL with an SD of 15 mg/dL therefore spans 65 to 125 mg/dL for 95% of values. [ROSH]
- Number needed to treat (NNT) is the inverse of the absolute risk reduction, rounded up, because a patient cannot be treated as a fraction. [ROSH]
- The Student t-test compares a continuous variable between two groups and assumes a normal distribution and equal variance; the Wilcoxon rank-sum (Mann-Whitney U) test does not, and chi-square and Fisher exact tests are used for categorical variables. [ROSH]

### Documentation and Record Review

- A macro is predetermined text inserted with a keystroke and must be reviewed and edited. An examination documenting a normal right leg in a patient with a prior below-knee amputation indicates an unedited macro, with legal and billing risk. [PEER-PH]
- An incidental abnormal result that is not communicated - severe lymphopenia with a white blood cell count of 1,000 in a patient discharged with diverticulitis - breaches the duty to treat when the patient is later found to have HIV/AIDS. [PEER-PH]

## Management

### Structured Handoff

1. Use a formal handoff tool for every transition; miscommunication during transitions underlies 80% of medical errors and faulty handoffs account for 24% of malpractice claims. [PEER-PH]
2. Deliver an ED I-PASS report: Expected disposition, Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situational awareness, Synthesis by receiver. State the anticipated disposition first - it is the most important element. [PEER-PH]
3. Prioritise the sickest patients, review pending results, and include code status; sign boarding patients out as active ED patients. Omit the full history and physical and the medication list. [PEER-PH]

### Death Notification and Bad News

1. Ask support staff to gather the family, then introduce yourself and the other staff present. [ROSH]
2. Establish what the family already knows - ask what they have been told so far - then warn that bad news is coming, educate them about the events, and inform them of the death. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
3. State unequivocally that the loved one is dead, using the word "dead" rather than a euphemism; use "breathing tube", never "intubated". [ROSH]
4. Pause for information processing, verify understanding, and stay for questions. Respond to emotion with NURSE (Name, Understand, Respect, Support, Explore) rather than facts or false reassurance. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
5. Invite the family into a pediatric resuscitation with a supportive staff member: AHA Class I, reduces PTSD and anxiety, and is not assigned to the code leader. [PEER-PH]

### Interpreter Use

1. Offer interpreter services to every patient with limited English proficiency, and brief the interpreter on the goals of the encounter before the interview. [PEER-PH]
2. Keep eye contact with the patient, one question at a time, in plain language. Position a spoken-language interpreter behind the patient and a sign-language interpreter in the patient's line of sight. [PEER-PH]
3. Never use a family member, and never a minor, as an interpreter except in a true life-or-death emergency with no formal interpreter available; ad hoc interpreters make twice as many errors. [PEER-PH]
4. Do not substitute an uncertified clinician in a stable patient even after a long wait; in a genuinely emergent presentation a non-certified speaker may obtain a basic history while a formal interpreter is arranged. [PEER-PH]

### Error Disclosure

1. Disclose the specific error even when no harm occurred - omitted antiseptic before lumbar puncture, or a platelet unit transfused to the wrong patient - describing potential effects, monitoring, and follow-up care, in person. [PEER-PH]
2. Apologise; open disclosure and apology decrease litigation risk. Report the error to the hospital quality reporting system and file an incident report. [PEER-PH]
3. Speak up regardless of hierarchy: pause and check the allergy list when a bupivacaine allergy is recalled before lidocaine is given, because the two amide anaesthetics cross-react. [PEER-PH]

### De-escalation and Conflict

| Situation | First move |
| Angry patient | Acknowledge the grievance, sit at the same height, respect personal space, shift to problem solving, offer small concessions; sedation or restraint if this fails [PEER-PH] |
| Colleague behaving unprofessionally | Express concerns directly and ask the person to clarify their motivations, focusing on the behaviour rather than the person [ROSH] |
| Impaired colleague with alcohol on the breath | Offer to take their patients and send them home [ROSH] |
| Nurse-physician conflict with resignations | Investigate the incident first; high turnover signals an unhealthy culture [PEER-PH] |
| Coverage crisis with one overwhelmed consultant | Collaboration - a resolution acceptable to both parties [ROSH] |

### Disposition: Observation, Against Medical Advice, and Discharge

- An ED observation unit extends emergency care for up to 24 hours for an unclear diagnosis needing limited further evaluation - syncope with a normal ECG and negative troponin, or low- to moderate-risk chest pain. It differs from a holding unit, which houses admitted patients waiting for beds. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Observation units deliver higher patient satisfaction scores, shorter stays, and decreased health care costs with comparable or better outcomes. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- About 2% of ED patients leave against medical advice (range 0.1-2.7%) and about 3% leave without being seen; those discharged against medical advice have a significantly increased 30-day return rate and greater need for emergent hospitalisation, and insurance still covers the visit. [PEER-PH]
- The best defence against liability for an AMA discharge is complete, real-time documentation of the discussion, risks, capacity assessment, and patient understanding; AMA forms support but do not replace documentation. [PEER-PH]
- Geriatric discharge: do not compress all instructions onto one sheet of paper. Engage the family, hand off to the primary care physician, use simple language, and review the instructions personally. [PEER-PH]

### Pain Management and Medication Safety

- Acute pain: screen for risk factors, educate about the benefits and risks of opioid therapy, maximise non-opioid and non-pharmacological therapy, and use the lowest dose of a short-acting opioid for the shortest course. [PEER-PH]
- Avoid combining an opioid with a sedative-hypnotic such as baclofen, which raises the risk of respiratory depression and arrest. [PEER-PH]
- In older adults, prefer non-opioid analgesics and titrate low: morphine 8 mg for gout precipitated delirium, a fall, and a femoral neck fracture. Fall-risk identifiers do not prevent falls. [PEER-PH]
- Store medications at home out of sight and out of reach, preferably locked; there are 60,000 child ED visits per year from medication exposure, and "one pill kills" applies to cardiac medications, antidepressants, imidazolines, clonidine, opioids, and sulfonylureas. [PEER-PH]

### Documentation, Coding, and Clinical Decision Support

- Critical care billing requires critical illness or injury with a high probability of imminent, life-threatening deterioration; no specific intervention is required and the minimum time is 30 minutes. Anaphylaxis requiring intramuscular epinephrine and frequent reassessment qualifies. [PEER-PH]
- Ventilator management is included in critical care time; separately billable procedures such as intubation and reductions are not. [PEER-PH]
- Clinical decision support is a rule-based alert combining patient information with medical evidence, such as a real-time reminder to give aspirin when troponin is positive. [PEER-PH]
- Order all needed laboratory and imaging studies simultaneously; sequential testing delays care. CMS Hospital Outpatient Quality Reporting Program measures include the rate of patients who left without being seen, median ED arrival-to-departure time, and head CT or MRI within 45 minutes for stroke; failure to report results in a 2% payment reduction. [PEER-PH]

### EMS Levels, Medical Direction, and Transport

| Level | Capability |
| Emergency medical responder | CPR and first aid |
| Emergency medical technician (EMT) | Basic life support |
| Advanced emergency medical technician (AEMT) | BLS plus intravenous access and supraglottic airways, but not rapid sequence intubation [PEER-PH] |
| Paramedic | Highest level of training and advanced practice: cardiac rhythm analysis, pharmacological treatment, advanced airways, and rapid sequence intubation with medical director approval [ROSH][PEER-PH] |

- Critical care transport teams are most commonly a paramedic and a nurse; advanced life support transport is EMT-paramedic; basic life support transport is two EMTs. [PEER-PH]
- Online medical direction is direct and real-time - an emergency physician ordering medication by radio to a paramedic. Offline medical direction is indirect: protocol development, quality improvement, education, and chart review. [PEER-PH]
- The medical director of a critical care transport team ensures the team is properly trained; the transferring physician selects the mode and monitoring, and the accepting physician selects the bed. [PEER-PH]
- Stable chest pain without STEMI transferred to a catheterisation laboratory needs advanced life support. A critically ill patient who may need intubation needs a paramedic, the only responder with intubation capability. [PEER-PH]
- Regionalisation transports time-critical diagnoses - trauma, STEMI, stroke - directly to designated facilities with the needed resources. [PEER-PH]

### EMTALA and Interfacility Transfer

- Any patient presenting to the ED must receive stabilizing treatment; if the patient cannot be stabilised, transfer to a facility that can provide the care is required. A life-threatening injury is an exception to parental consent: transfer the child before consent when the parents cannot be reached. [PEER-PH]
- A receiving hospital may decline a transfer only for lack of capability (missing specialty service) or capacity (no space or personnel) - a vascular surgeon in an 8-hour case, or a stroke centre at full capacity and diverting ambulances. Insurance status is never valid, and on-call status alone does not permit refusal. [PEER-PH]
- When an on-call consultant cannot be reached, escalate by contacting a supervisor before arranging transfer of a stable patient. [PEER-PH]
- Active labour is unstable: at a critical-access hospital without obstetric or neonatal services, deliver the infant and placenta first, then transfer both. [PEER-PH]
- STEMI: percutaneous coronary intervention is preferred if achievable within 2 hours; after aspirin and heparin, transfer a patient one hour from a PCI centre. Thrombolytics are used when PCI is not achievable within 2 hours. [PEER-PH]
- Staff credentialing may be waived only when the hospital Emergency Operations Plan has been activated; 72-hour oversight is required. [PEER-PH]

### Palliative and End-of-Life Management

- A do-not-resuscitate order does not mean "do nothing": a comfort-care-only patient with lobar pneumonia and dyspnoea receives an opioid plus a benzodiazepine, with a palliative medicine consultation. [PEER-PH]
- Refer to palliative medicine when a family considers a feeding tube for advanced dementia; artificial nutrition provides no health benefit in advanced dementia and hand-feeding is recommended. [PEER-PH]
- When a POLST documents no CPR and no intubation and is comfort-focused, and the family agrees, extubate in the emergency department and provide comfort care - "liberating from the ventilator", not "withdrawing care". [PEER-PH]
- Refer a patient with terminal cancer who wishes to die at home, avoid admission, and control symptoms to hospice. Eligibility requires a potential life expectancy of 6 months or less, and full code status does not disqualify a patient. [PEER-PH]
- A goals-of-care conversation starts with the patient's understanding and values; explore values before making recommendations. [PEER-PH]
- When a patient in hospice confirms they want an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator deactivated, place a magnet over the device and leave it in place until reprogramming; removing it returns the device to active status. [PEER-PH]
- Do not continue chemotherapy in a solid tumour with ECOG 3 or higher and prior non-beneficial treatment. [PEER-PH]

### Consent, Capacity, and Involuntary Care

- A sexually active 15-year-old with cervical motion tenderness, dysuria, and vaginal discharge can be treated for a sexually transmitted infection without parental consent or knowledge; all fifty states have provisions for expeditious treatment, and none precludes treatment. [ROSH]
- Arrange an emergency psychiatric hold under local state law for a patient who is gravely disabled - a danger to self or unable to self-care - and lacks capacity, such as a patient with bipolar disorder, grandiose delusions, flight of ideas, and a recent suicide attempt who wishes to leave. [PEER-PH]
- Involuntarily sedate and restrain as needed when an intoxicated patient with a head injury and a large scalp haematoma lacks capacity to refuse essential evaluation. [PEER-PH]

### Workplace Violence and Emergency Preparedness

- Nurses are the staff group most frequently victimised by workplace violence in the ED, followed by physicians, then other staff. [PEER-PH]
- Verbal threats are workplace violence under OSHA and constitute assault legally: call law enforcement to file a police report, which assesses criminal history and firearm status, and also file an incident report and notify administration. [PEER-PH]
- Active shooter response: run first, as far as possible; then hide, locking or barricading and staying quiet and concealed; fight only as a last resort. [PEER-PH]
- Tactical operations: for a casualty still under fire with controlled haemorrhage and a partially obstructed airway, the priority is extraction from the hot zone. [ROSH]
- Injury prevention counselling should match age and mechanism: safe sleep habits for an infant, helmet use for a school-age child after a skateboard fall, and firearms stored unloaded in a safe only adults can access with ammunition locked separately. Primary prevention intervenes before disease onset - the varicella vaccine is an example. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Quality Improvement, Teaching, and Supervision

- Kotter's 8-step model: 1 create urgency, 2 build a coalition, 3 form a vision, 4 communicate it, 5 remove obstacles, 6 secure short-term wins, 7 consolidate, 8 embed in culture; the first step is creating a sense of urgency. [PEER-PH]
- To build a culture of safety as a high-reliability organisation, develop a system to report and track errors and near misses; only 15% of errors come from individuals and 85% from flawed systems. [PEER-PH]
- Give feedback in real time and make it specific; general advice for the next shift is the least helpful form. Setting expectations before assigning a patient is essential for a new resident. [PEER-PH]
- A supervising physician is fully liable for the care delivered by a physician assistant regardless of personal evaluation; evaluate every patient with abnormal vital signs and discuss all cases. [PEER-PH]

### Contract, Insurance, and Financial Management

| Item | Key point |
| Occurrence policy | Covers the incident regardless of when the claim is filed - the broadest protection [PEER-PH] |
| Claims-made policy | Covers only if active at both the incident and the claim; tail coverage pays claims filed after the policy ends [PEER-PH] |
| Liability limits | `$1,000,000/$3,000,000` = per occurrence / per year in total [ROSH] |
| Own-occupation, specialty-specific disability | Best protection: pays if the physician cannot work in the specialty while allowing work outside it; a residual disability rider pays when income drops 15-20% [PEER-PH] |
| Group disability policy | Premiums from pre-tax earnings, so payments are fully taxable; not portable, no COLA, weaker definition of disability [PEER-PH] |

- Consult a contract lawyer with physician employment expertise at the beginning of the job search, before anything is in writing, and negotiate the malpractice insurance type and amount plus tail coverage if the policy is claims-made. Never sign immediately. [PEER-PH]
- A restrictive covenant (noncompete) limits where a physician may work after leaving, for example within a 5-mile radius of the hospital; ACEP opposes noncompetes. Independent contractors are more likely to be audited by the IRS, and locum tenens offers higher salaries. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Saving priority: fund the employer retirement plan to capture the full 401(k) or 403(b) match, then clear high-interest debt above 8%, then further retirement contributions. Save 15% to 25% of gross annual income. [PEER-PH]
- A Health Savings Account has a triple tax advantage - contributions pre-tax, growth tax-free, withdrawals for medical expenses untaxed - and requires a high-deductible plan; the 2025 individual maximum is $4,300. Public Service Loan Forgiveness requires 120 on-time payments plus full-time employment at a 501(c)(3) or government employer. [PEER-PH]
- Good Samaritan laws protect physicians providing care outside their clinical setting against ordinary negligence but not gross negligence. [PEER-PH]
- Under HIPAA, protected health information may be used without authorization for treatment, payment, and operations and may be shared for quality improvement evaluation; psychotherapy notes require written authorization except for treatment, certain legal matters, and protecting the public from a serious threat. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Liability requires duty, breach, harm, and causation; failing to communicate an incidental abnormal result breaches it. Willful misconduct is conscious disregard for another person's safety despite knowing harm is likely; negligence is failure to meet the standard of care. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Res ipsa loquitur ("the thing speaks for itself") may remove the need for direct factual evidence of negligence - a retained guidewire after central line placement. [PEER-PH]
- The Dr. Lorna Breen Health Care Provider Protection Act (2022) provides grants to support mental health and resiliency in healthcare workers. [PEER-PH]
