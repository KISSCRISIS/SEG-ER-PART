## Diagnosis

### Acute Kidney Injury

- Kidney injury is defined by a rise in serum creatinine >= 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, a rise to >= 1.5 times baseline within 7 days, or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 hours [ROSH].
- Prerenal pattern: hypotension, tachycardia, dry mucous membranes, gastrointestinal losses. A urine sodium of 10 mEq/L supports a prerenal cause [ROSH].
- Chronic kidney disease may declare itself through a complication rather than azotemia: pulmonary edema, hyperkalemia, severe hypertension, uremic pericarditis, or tamponade.

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Muddy granular casts | Acute tubular necrosis (amphotericin B, aminoglycosides, cisplatin, sepsis, ischaemia) [ROSH] |
| Eosinophiluria with pyuria, haematuria, mild proteinuria | Acute interstitial nephritis; rash, fever and eosinophilia in drug cases [ROSH] |
| Red cell casts with haematuria and proteinuria | Acute glomerulonephritis; red cell casts alone are highly specific [ROSH] |
| Hydronephrosis | Obstructive uropathy |
| Contrast exposure, creatinine rise > 25% of baseline within 3 days | Contrast-associated AKI [ROSH] |
| Weight gain > 5 lb (2.2 kg) above dry weight | Hypervolemia in end-stage kidney disease [PEER-PH] |

### Imaging

- Renal ultrasound is the preferred initial study in new-onset kidney failure: 90% sensitivity and specificity for hydronephrosis, and the "bear paw" sign when severe. It is the imaging of choice in pregnancy [PEER-PH].
- Computed tomography (CT) is the most sensitive modality in a child with classic nephrolithiasis symptoms; noncontrast CT confirms a ureteral stone [ROSH].

| Modality for nephrolithiasis | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|
| Noncontrast CT | 94-97% | 96-99% |
| Renal ultrasound | 63-85% | 79-100% |
| Abdominal x-ray | 29-58% | 69-74% |

Abdominal x-ray is the least sensitive and least specific [PEER-PH].

### Urinary Tract Infection

- Urinalysis provides objective evidence before treating pyelonephritis; imaging is not routine in uncomplicated cases [PEER-PH].
- Typical: leukocyte esterase, nitrites, >= 10 WBC/hpf; in men even 1 WBC/hpf is significant when bacteria are present.
- A symptomatic patient with > 102 CFU/mL on culture is infected [ROSH].
- Fever, chills, malaise, nausea, vomiting, back and flank pain with costovertebral angle tenderness separate pyelonephritis from cystitis; vaginal discharge instead points to vaginitis or cervicitis.
- Asymptomatic bacteriuria is not treated except in pregnancy, kidney transplant, and urologic instrumentation [ROSH].
- For suspected urosepsis, blood cultures and serum lactate belong to the CMS 3-hour bundle; urine culture is not required [PEER-PH].
- Older, immunocompromised, or comorbid patients may present with fever, rigors, altered mental status, malaise, or lethargy without localising urinary symptoms, delaying diagnosis [ROSH].

### Scrotal and Testicular Pain

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Absent cremasteric reflex, transverse lie, high-riding testis, abrupt severe pain with vomiting | Testicular torsion [ROSH] |
| Tender upper-pole nodule, cremasteric reflex present, milder gradual pain | Appendageal torsion of the appendix testis [ROSH] |
| Gradual onset, posterior epididymal tenderness, normal lie, Prehn sign | Epididymitis [ROSH] |
| Transilluminating fluctuant nontender swelling that enlarges with crying or Valsalva | Hydrocele |
| "Bag of worms" left-sided mass with inferior vena cava thrombus | Varicocele [ROSH] |
| Firm nontender mass that does not transilluminate | Testicular tumour [ROSH] |
| Parotitis then unilateral testicular swelling and pain | Mumps orchitis [PEER-PH] |

- Scrotal Doppler ultrasound is the gold standard for torsion (sensitivity 88-100%, specificity about 90%) but must not delay treatment [ROSH].
- Urinalysis shows pyuria in about 50% of epididymitis; test for gonorrhoea and chlamydia in sexually active patients and send a urine culture in those who are not [ROSH].
- A positive urine hCG supports a germ cell tumour [PEER-PH].

### Genital Ulcers and Discharge

| Lesion | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Single 1-2 cm painless ulcer, raised border, clean base, painless adenopathy | Primary syphilis (Treponema pallidum) [ROSH] |
| Multiple painful purulent-based ulcers with tender fluctuant bubo | Chancroid (Haemophilus ducreyi) [ROSH] |
| Painless ulcer healing in 2-3 days, then painful inguinal adenopathy with a groove | Lymphogranuloma venereum (C. trachomatis L1, L2, L3) [ROSH] |
| Multiple painful ulcers with dysuria, fever, headache, tender adenopathy | Genital herpes [ROSH] |

- Syphilis: rapid plasma reagin or Venereal Disease Research Laboratory testing confirmed by a treponemal immunoassay; results may not turn positive until up to 1 month after the chancre [ROSH].
- Darkfield microscopy visualises T. pallidum but its sensitivity is insufficient as a rule-out test [ROSH].
- Urethritis is a clinical diagnosis supported by nucleic acid amplification testing for N. gonorrhoeae and C. trachomatis on first-catch urine [ROSH].

### Foreskin, Priapism and Penile Findings

- Phimosis: inability to retract the foreskin proximal to the glans from adhesions that lyse over time; normal in young uncircumcised boys [ROSH].
- Paraphimosis: inability to reduce the retracted foreskin over the glans, with engorgement distal to the obstruction and proximal flaccidity; a urologic emergency [ROSH].
- Balanitis is inflammation of the glans; balanoposthitis adds foreskin involvement and is commoner in uncircumcised males. Candida is the usual cause, especially in diabetes, and poor hygiene is the leading contributor [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Cavernosal blood gas: ischemic blood is dark, hypoxemic, and acidemic; non-ischemic gas has normal pH and PO2 > 90 mm Hg [ROSH].
- Low-flow priapism is painful; high-flow priapism is painless, follows penile trauma or instrumentation, and often resolves spontaneously within days [ROSH].

### Inherited, Neoplastic and Other Findings

- Polycystic kidney disease: flank pain, haematuria, urinary frequency, hypertension or family history; multiple bilateral cysts on imaging confirm it, and magnetic resonance angiography of the head screens for the associated cerebral aneurysm [ROSH].
- Wilms tumour: painless abdominal mass in about 80% of children; renal ultrasound is the initial study [ROSH].
- Single thick-walled renal cortical mass with calcified septations on ultrasound suggests renal cell carcinoma [ROSH].
- Painless gross haematuria over age 65 years with prior pelvic irradiation for prostate cancer is concerning for bladder cancer [ROSH].
- Cloudy peritoneal dialysis effluent indicates peritonitis: WBC 150 cells/µL with 60% neutrophils, sent for cell count, differential, Gram stain, and culture [ROSH].
- Erythema overlying a known inguinal hernia suggests strangulation [PEER-PH].

## Management

### Time-Critical Pathways

1. Obstructive kidney failure or acute urinary retention: place a Foley catheter emergently; there is no contraindication in BPH [PEER-PH][ROSH].
2. Infected obstructed stone with hypotension and tachycardia: IV access, IV antibiotics such as ceftriaxone, and emergent urology consultation for drainage [PEER-PH].
3. Testicular torsion: parenteral analgesia, immediate urology consultation, and manual detorsion by lateral rotation using the "opening-a-book" technique if surgery is not immediate; ultrasound must not delay treatment [PEER-PH][ROSH].
4. Fournier gangrene: broad-spectrum antibiotics covering gram-negative, gram-positive, and anaerobic organisms, such as IV piperacillin-tazobactam with vancomycin, plus emergency surgical debridement [ROSH].
5. Strangulated hernia: emergency surgical consultation; do not attempt manual reduction when there is overlying erythema [PEER-PH].
6. Ischemic priapism: analgesia and anaesthesia (dorsal penile nerve block or circumferential ring block), then aspirate one corpus cavernosum with a needle at least 19 g and inject phenylephrine [ROSH].

### Emergent Renal Replacement Therapy

- AEIOU: Acidemia, Electrolytes (hyperkalemia > 6.5 mmol/L or any potassium-related ECG conduction abnormality), Ingestion, Overload, Uremia. Also severe fluid overload, refractory hypertension, and uremia [PEER-PH].
- In a dialysis patient, pulmonary edema is one of the most common ED presentations and an indication for emergent dialysis; these patients respond poorly to diuretics [ROSH].
- Hyperkalemia with peaked T waves and a wide QRS is treated with calcium gluconate [ROSH].
- Poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis with fluid overload failing loop diuretics requires hemodialysis to remove fluid and correct uremia or electrolyte imbalance [ROSH].
- Toxic alcohol ingestion: fomepizole (or ethanol if unavailable) inhibits alcohol dehydrogenase and temporises the patient until dialysis removes the toxic compounds [ROSH].
- Hemodialysis is ineffective for diltiazem overdose [ROSH].

### Dialysis Complications

| Problem | Management |
|---|---|
| Hypervolemic pulmonary edema in end-stage kidney disease | IV nitroglycerin, bilevel positive airway pressure, nephrology consultation; loop diuretics only with preserved residual kidney function [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Hypotension during hemodialysis | Reduce the filtration rate or give a small IV saline bolus [ROSH] |
| Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome | Supportive care and exclusion of serious causes; 5 mL of 23% sodium chloride has been used [ROSH] |
| Bleeding arteriovenous fistula | Firm finger pressure for 5-10 minutes, then confirm a thrill; if lost, consult vascular surgery immediately and do not irrigate or manipulate the access [ROSH] |
| Peritoneal dialysis peritonitis | Intraperitoneal vancomycin and cefepime; outpatient management is appropriate in the well patient [ROSH] |

- Excessive ultrafiltration is the most common cause of intradialytic hypotension [PEER-PH].

### Urinary Tract Infection Treatment

| Setting | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Uncomplicated cystitis | Nitrofurantoin, or oral trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for 5 days [ROSH] |
| Uncomplicated pyelonephritis, outpatient | Oral ciprofloxacin [ROSH] |
| Pregnancy | Cephalexin 500 mg PO four times daily for 10 days, or nitrofurantoin or amoxicillin for 10-14 days; ciprofloxacin is contraindicated. Asymptomatic bacteriuria is treated with antibiotics plus a urine culture [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Neonate | IV ampicillin and gentamicin; avoid ceftriaxone (hyperbilirubinemia, nephrotoxicity) [PEER-PH] |
| Febrile UTI in a young child | Oral cefpodoxime with close follow-up if non-toxic [ROSH] |
| Pyelonephritis in pregnancy or the immunocompromised host | Piperacillin-tazobactam, and admission [ROSH] |
| Recurrent dysuria with vaginal discharge | Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once plus doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days [PEER-PH] |
| Enterococcus faecalis isolate in a child | Amoxicillin [ROSH] |

- Untreated bacteriuria in pregnancy risks preterm labour, low birth weight, and pyelonephritis [PEER-PH].

### Pyelonephritis Complication

- Failure to improve within days of appropriate antibiotics suggests a renal corticomedullary or perinephric abscess. Small abscesses < 5 cm in stable patients may be treated with antibiotics alone; larger ones or instability require percutaneous drainage, and mortality with antibiotics alone for large abscesses can reach 33% [PEER-PH][ROSH].

### Nephrolithiasis

- Goals: make the diagnosis, relieve pain, assess kidney function, treat infection, and estimate the likelihood of spontaneous passage [ROSH].
- Stones <= 5 mm pass spontaneously in up to 90%; stones >= 10 mm are unlikely to pass without intervention [ROSH].
- Tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily is the preferred medical expulsive therapy for ureteral stones > 5 mm; alpha-blockers are preferred over calcium channel blockers [PEER-PH].
- Discharge with analgesia, a strainer, tamsulosin, and urology follow-up; stones > 5 mm or stones not passed within 4 weeks need urology review [ROSH].
- Admit with urology for an obstructing stone with hydronephrosis in a solitary functioning kidney, an 8 mm obstructing calculus with vomiting and elevated creatinine, or an infected obstructed system [ROSH].
- In pregnancy, ultrasound is the imaging of choice, narcotics are preferred for analgesia, and NSAIDs are contraindicated; infected obstructed nephrolithiasis requires admission with urology [PEER-PH].

### Scrotal and Genital Infection Treatment

| Scenario | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Epididymitis, sexually active or age < 35 years | Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once plus doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 10 days [ROSH] |
| Epididymitis after insertive anal intercourse | Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM plus levofloxacin 500 mg PO twice daily for 10 days (enteric cover) [PEER-PH] |
| Epididymitis in a non-sexually-active or older man | Levofloxacin [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Urethritis | Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once plus doxycycline 100 mg PO twice daily for 7 days [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Primary syphilis | Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM, single dose [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Chancroid | Single oral dose of azithromycin, or intramuscular ceftriaxone [ROSH] |
| Genital herpes | Acyclovir 400 mg three times daily for 10 days [ROSH] |
| Acute bacterial prostatitis | Ciprofloxacin or levofloxacin for 4-6 weeks; levofloxacin 750 mg IV daily if admitted; trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole is an alternative [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Balanitis or balanoposthitis | Saline cleansing twice daily plus a topical antifungal such as clotrimazole; oral fluconazole for severe refractory candidiasis; recurrent cases warrant a fingerstick blood glucose [PEER-PH][ROSH] |

- Ceftriaxone and doxycycline are combined because gonorrhoea and chlamydia commonly co-occur and are clinically indistinguishable [ROSH].
- Azithromycin is no longer first-line for urethritis because of resistance [PEER-PH].

### Other Management Points

- Hydrocele: ultrasonography when the diagnosis is uncertain; most paediatric hydroceles are self-limited [ROSH].
- Phimosis without pain, obstruction, or ischaemia: reassurance and hygiene in children, elective circumcision referral in adults [ROSH].
- Paraphimosis: reduce swelling with ice and compression, give parenteral or local anaesthesia, then reduce manually [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Sickle cell priapism: needle aspiration and saline irrigation of the corpus cavernosum with hydration and analgesia [ROSH].
- Untreated priapism most often results in impotence [PEER-PH].
- Inguinal hernia that is soft, nontender, and without erythema: manual reduction; incarcerated hernias need surgical reduction in the operating room [ROSH].
- Haemolytic uremic syndrome: supportive care with IV normal saline boluses for dehydration; hemodialysis is occasionally needed temporarily, and most patients recover fully [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis: fluid and salt restriction to control edema and prevent hypertension or volume overload [ROSH].
- Childhood nephrotic syndrome: corticosteroids with pediatric nephrology input, plus dietary restriction [ROSH].
- Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease: benazepril is most likely to slow progression [ROSH].
- Goodpasture syndrome: plasmapheresis, prednisone, and cyclophosphamide [ROSH].
