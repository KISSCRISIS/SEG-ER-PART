## Diagnosis

### First Step in Any Altered Mental Status

- Capillary blood glucose in every patient, before any other explanation is accepted, then vital signs including temperature, a targeted neurologic examination, and a search for focal signs, meningismus and trauma. [PEER-PH]

### Primary Psychiatric versus Medical Psychosis

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Abnormal vital signs, focal neurology, visual hallucinations | Medical or toxic cause |
| Onset over hours to days, confusion prominent | Medical or toxic cause |
| Onset of confusion after age 40 | Medical cause |
| Insidious onset, auditory hallucinations, disorganized thought and behaviour, age 15–25 | Primary psychiatric disorder |
| Command hallucinations that persist despite sobriety | Organic psychosis |
| Hallucinations that resolve with sobriety | Substance-induced psychosis |
| Young woman, viral-like prodrome, seizures | Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis [PEER-CS] |

- Cocaine-induced psychosis is more likely with younger age at first use, male sex, prolonged use, high doses and pre-existing mental illness. [PEER-PH]
- Violence risk rises with auditory hallucinations, prior violence, extreme excitement, systematization of delusions and long illness duration. [ROSH]

### Psychotic and Mood Disorder Criteria

| Disorder | Diagnostic requirement |
|---|---|
| Schizophrenia | Symptoms at least 6 months, including delusions or disorganized speech plus hallucinations or negative symptoms [ROSH] |
| Delusional disorder | Delusion at least 1 month, not medically explained, criterion A for schizophrenia not met [PEER-CS] |
| Major depressive disorder | Five or more symptoms nearly every day for at least 2 consecutive weeks, one being depressed mood or anhedonia [ROSH] |

### Anxiety and Somatic Presentations

- Anxiety is a diagnosis of exclusion; the first test is a serum glucose, because hypoglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis both present with anxiety-like symptoms. [PEER-CS]
- Panic disorder is recurrent panic attacks with a normal examination and normal vital signs; agoraphobia adds fear of being alone in public places where escape would be difficult, and up to 75% of those patients have panic attacks there. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Illness anxiety disorder is persistent unrealistic worry about illness despite repeated negative testing; about 70% have comorbid generalized anxiety disorder. [ROSH]
- Functional neurologic symptom disorder is a diagnosis of exclusion for involuntary loss of neurologic function and may show la belle indifference; consciously produced symptoms for an obvious external reward indicate malingering, while intentional production without external reward indicates factitious disorder. [PEER-CS][PEER-PH]

### Substance Use: Recognition

| Presentation | Features |
|---|---|
| Alcohol withdrawal | Tremor, autonomic hyperactivity, hallucinations, seizures, delirium tremens |
| Opioid intoxication | Miosis, respiratory depression, depressed mental status |
| Opioid withdrawal | Mydriasis, lacrimation, rhinorrhoea, piloerection, diaphoresis, myalgias, cramping, diarrhoea, no seizures [PEER-CS] |
| Benzodiazepine withdrawal | Life-threatening; seizures and delirium |
| Sympathomimetic toxicity | Hypertension, tachycardia, hyperthermia, mydriasis, agitation, psychosis |
| Phencyclidine toxicity | Disinhibition, hallucinations, seizures, violent agitation [PEER-PH] |

- Alcohol use disorder is defined by eleven criteria and two or more are diagnostic; continuing to drink after alcohol-related injury is hazardous use. [PEER-PH]
- Risky drinking predictive of later alcohol use disorder and dependence is 15 or more standard drinks per week in men. [ROSH]
- Opioid use disorder requires two or more specific behaviours in 12 months causing impairment or distress, including tolerance and withdrawal. [PEER-PH]
- Alcoholic cardiomyopathy is a dilated cardiomyopathy with reduced ejection fraction in long-term severe alcohol abuse. [PEER-PH]

### Eating Disorders: Recognition

| Feature | Anorexia nervosa | Bulimia nervosa |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | Abnormally low | Usually normal |
| Core feature | Distorted body image, fear of weight gain | Binge eating with loss of control plus compensatory behaviour |
| Subtypes | Restrictive; binge-eating/purging | Purging; non-purging |

- Bulimia nervosa typically begins at 13–18 years and is three times more common in women; anorexia nervosa is associated with osteoporosis, thinning hair, hypothyroidism with bradycardia, and muscle wasting. Between 10% and 25% of eating disorder cases occur in men. [ROSH][PEER-CS]

### Delirium versus Dementia

| Feature | Delirium | Dementia |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Abrupt, hours to days | Insidious, months to years |
| Course | Fluctuates, worse at night | Progressive, does not fluctuate |
| Attention | Impaired | Preserved until late |
| Reversibility | Reversible | Progressive |

- Delirium precipitants: infection, dehydration, electrolyte derangement, intoxication, trauma, benzodiazepines and anticholinergics, unfamiliar settings, and a lost hearing aid or glasses. [ROSH]
- Alzheimer disease: progressive memory loss from loss of cholinergic innervation. Lewy body dementia: visual hallucinations, parkinsonian features, fluctuating cognition. Frontotemporal dementia: disinhibition, apathy, hyperorality. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Abuse, Neglect and Exploitation

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Bruising in a child under 6 months, or any bruise in a non-mobile child | Intentional trauma [ROSH] |
| Bruising to torso, ears or neck (TEN-4-FACESp) in a child under 4 years | Concerning for abuse [ROSH] |
| Metaphyseal corner fractures, posterior rib fractures, any fracture in a non-ambulatory child | Abuse [ROSH] |
| Delay in care, changing story, explanation inconsistent with the child's age | Abuse [ROSH] |
| Central injury pattern, facial injury, psychological aggression | Intimate partner violence [PEER-CS][PEER-PH] |
| Poor hygiene, dehydration, missed appointments, unmet medical needs | Neglect — the most common form of maltreatment [ROSH] |
| Branding tattoos showing ownership, restricted movement, scripted answers | Human trafficking [ROSH][PEER-CS] |

### Sexual Assault Examination

- Offer emergency contraception: ulipristal is effective up to 120 hours and levonorgestrel up to 72 hours after the assault. [PEER-CS]
- Offer HIV post-exposure prophylaxis within 72 hours of exposure, continued for 28 days; it reduces transmission by about 81%. [PEER-CS]
- Prophylaxis against sexually transmitted infection: ceftriaxone 250 mg intramuscularly, azithromycin 1 g orally, and metronidazole. [PEER-CS]
- Document injuries and collect forensic evidence with the patient's consent, then address safety before discharge.

## Management

### The Agitated Patient — Sequence

1. Ensure scene safety and adequate staffing before approaching the patient.
2. Attempt verbal de-escalation and modify the environment; approaching a psychotic patient first with restraints and sedation can escalate behaviour. [PEER-CS]
3. If de-escalation fails, give chemical sedation; add physical restraint only as a last resort.
4. Treat the underlying cause once the patient is safe: glucose, hypoxia, intoxication, pain, infection.

### Chemical Sedation Choices

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| Midazolam intramuscularly | Most rapid onset of the intramuscular options [PEER-PH] |
| Diazepam | Preferred benzodiazepine for sympathomimetic toxicity; mainstay for phencyclidine intoxication and its seizures [PEER-CS][PEER-PH] |
| Haloperidol | Widely used for moderate to severe agitation; risk of QT prolongation and dystonia |
| Risperidone | Does not significantly prolong the QT; preferred in older adults [PEER-CS] |
| Diphenhydramine | Treats acute dystonia from antipsychotics [PEER-CS] |
| Oral diphenhydramine | Preferred first drug for agitation in pregnancy after de-escalation [ROSH] |

### Antipsychotic Adverse Effects

- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome: altered mental status, hyperthermia, dysautonomia, bradyreflexia and rigidity days to weeks after starting or increasing an antipsychotic. Stop the drug and treat supportively. [PEER-CS]
- Tardive dyskinesia: painless repetitive orofacial movements after months to years of therapy; risk rises with older age and longer treatment. Extrapyramidal symptoms come from dopamine D2 blockade in the basal ganglia and high-potency first-generation agents are the usual cause, with diphenhydramine used for acute dystonia. Olanzapine and clozapine carry the highest metabolic risk, and clozapine is reserved for refractory schizophrenia because of agranulocytosis. [PEER-CS]

### Suicide Risk Assessment

1. Screen directly: ideation, plan, intent, means, prior attempts, and access to firearms.
2. Score the SAD PERSONS scale: 0–4 low, 5–6 medium, 7–10 high. [PEER-CS]
3. Previous attempt is the strongest predictor of completed suicide; the highest rate is in men older than 75 years. [PEER-PH]
4. A high-risk patient with an active plan and firearm access must be kept involuntarily for psychiatric evaluation. [PEER-CS]
5. Do not leave the patient unobserved; remove access to means.

### Alcohol Withdrawal

1. Check glucose, and give thiamine before or with glucose; correct magnesium, potassium and phosphate, because hypomagnesaemia makes thiamine replacement ineffective. [PEER-PH]
2. Sedate with a benzodiazepine or phenobarbital. Lorazepam 2 mg intravenously reduced recurrent withdrawal seizures from 24% to 3% over a 6-hour observation period. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
3. Use symptom-triggered dosing rather than fixed dosing: it lowers the total dose, shortens treatment and shortens length of stay. [PEER-CS]
4. Monitor for delirium tremens and for alcoholic cardiomyopathy in long-term heavy drinkers. [PEER-PH]

### Opioid Overdose and Opioid Use Disorder

- Opioid intoxication with respiratory depression is treated by supporting ventilation and reversing with naloxone.
- Buprenorphine is a partial mu-opioid agonist with a ceiling effect on sedation, but because its receptor affinity is roughly 1,000 times that of full agonists it can precipitate withdrawal in a patient still receiving methadone. Methadone prolongs the QT interval by interfering with extracellular potassium, so monitor the ECG during maintenance. [PEER-CS]
- Opioid withdrawal is not life-threatening and can be managed with supportive care and symptom control; benzodiazepine withdrawal is life-threatening. [PEER-CS]

### Medications for Alcohol Use Disorder

- Naltrexone, acamprosate and disulfiram are the only FDA-approved medications for alcohol use disorder. [PEER-PH]
- Naltrexone is a mu-opioid antagonist that blocks the dopamine-mediated reward of drinking and reduces craving; acamprosate is an NMDA receptor antagonist acting on glutamate and supports abstinence. [PEER-PH]
- Disulfiram inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase, so acetaldehyde accumulates after any alcohol exposure and produces flushing, nausea, vomiting, tachycardia and hypotension.

### Eating Disorders — Medical Stabilisation

1. Assess for haemodynamic instability, bradycardia, hypotension, orthostatic changes and electrolyte derangement.
2. Correct hypokalaemia and the hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis caused by purging. [PEER-CS]
3. Refeed slowly and monitor phosphate, potassium and magnesium; refeeding syndrome with hypophosphataemia is the danger when a normal diet is restarted too quickly. [ROSH]
4. Watch for a prolonged QT interval from loss of cardiac muscle mass and increased vagal tone in starvation.
5. Arrange psychiatric follow-up; bupropion is avoided in patients with eating disorders. [ROSH]

### Delirium and Dementia

- Treat the precipitant: infection, dehydration, electrolyte derangement, pain, urinary retention, faecal impaction, and offending drugs.
- Use non-pharmacologic measures first: reorientation, lighting, hearing aids and glasses, family presence, sleep protection. Avoid benzodiazepines and anticholinergics, which precipitate and prolong delirium. [ROSH]
- Antipsychotics may be used cautiously at the lowest effective dose for distressing hallucinations or agitation.

### Abuse, Neglect and Exploitation — Reporting

1. Treat immediate medical and safety needs first; separate the child from the suspected abuser only through the proper authority.
2. Report suspected child abuse to child protective services; suspected elder mistreatment goes to state Adult Protective Services. [ROSH]
3. Screen for intimate partner violence privately, and provide resources and a safety plan; the face is the most common site of injury, and recognising the three forms of human trafficking and the requirement of coercion calls for a trauma-informed interview and social work involvement. [PEER-CS][PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Grief, Trauma and Physician Wellness

- Distinguish grief from PTSD by the dream content: sad dreams that trigger sadness suggest grief, fearful replaying nightmares suggest PTSD. Complicated grief prevents return to normal life for more than 6 to 12 months and warrants referral; violent death is a risk factor. [PEER-PH]
- Acute stress disorder resolves within 1 month; symptoms persisting beyond it define PTSD. [PEER-PH]
- Second victim syndrome describes the emotional effect of a patient safety event on the responsible physician; residents are at higher risk of depression and suicide than the general population. [PEER-PH]

### Poisoning Antidotes in the Agitated or Psychobehavioral Patient

| Toxin | Antidote or treatment |
|---|---|
| Carbon monoxide | High-flow oxygen; hyperbaric oxygen for carboxyhaemoglobin above 25%, or above 20% in pregnancy [PEER-CS] |
| Salicylate | Sodium bicarbonate; mixed respiratory alkalosis with metabolic acidosis is the classic pattern [PEER-CS] |
| Iron | Deferoxamine, which chelates ferric iron; consider when serum iron exceeds 500 mcg/dL [PEER-CS] |
| Methanol, ethylene glycol, isopropanol | High anion gap metabolic acidosis with an osmolar gap; methanol and ethylene glycol are metabolised to formic and oxalic acid respectively [PEER-CS] |
| Isoniazid | Functional GABA deficiency from blocked pyridoxine activation; treat with pyridoxine [PEER-CS] |
| Factitious hypoglycaemia | Insulin or sulfonylurea use; a C-peptide below 0.2 ng/mL indicates exogenous insulin [PEER-CS] |
