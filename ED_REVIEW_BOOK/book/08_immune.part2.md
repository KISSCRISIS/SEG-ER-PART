## Pathophysiology

### Mediator cascade in anaphylaxis

- Antigen binds IgE already fixed to the surface of mast cells and basophils, triggering degranulation and release of histamine, tryptase and tumor necrosis factor. [ROSH]
- Histamine is the most important mediator and is responsible for most of the symptoms of anaphylaxis: tachycardia, flushing and hives. [ROSH]
- Anaphylaxis may also be immune complex mediated or complement mediated; drug-triggered reactions in particular tend to follow these pathways rather than direct IgE binding. [ROSH]
- Nonimmunologic anaphylaxis occurs through direct mast cell and basophil degranulation in the absence of immunoglobulins but produces an identical clinical syndrome. [ROSH]
- Epinephrine acts at adrenergic receptors: beta-2 agonism stabilizes mast cells and produces bronchodilation, alpha-agonism produces vasoconstriction. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Why anaphylactic shock becomes refractory

- Increased vascular permeability in anaphylaxis causes rapid and sustained fluid shift into the extravascular space — third spacing. [PEER-PH]
- The combination of third spacing and loss of vasomotor tone produces significant hypotension that persists despite epinephrine, antihistamines, H2 blockers and steroids. [PEER-PH]
- Aggressive crystalloid resuscitation must therefore be instituted early rather than relying on vasoactive drugs alone. [PEER-PH]
- In a patient taking a beta-adrenergic blocking agent such as atenolol, the beneficial effects of epinephrine are blunted at the receptor. [PEER-PH]
- Glucagon bypasses the blocked receptor by stimulating cyclic adenosine monophosphate, reversing beta-receptor blockade. [PEER-PH]

### Angioedema: two distinct mechanisms

- Mast cell–mediated angioedema belongs to the allergic spectrum; bradykinin-mediated angioedema arises from ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, aliskiren, thrombolytics and estrogens, or from C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency or dysfunction, and hereditary attacks may be gradual or triggered by minor trauma. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- ACE-inhibitor angioedema is caused by decreased metabolism of bradykinin, with a bradykinin- and substance P–mediated mechanism; it is not histamine mediated, and C1 inhibitor deficiency leads to excess bradykinin. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Angioedema represents local extravasation of fluid into interstitial tissues across a permeable vascular bed; because the lips, mouth, uvula, larynx and pharynx are involved, progression to airway loss can be rapid and intubation may be very challenging. [ROSH]

### Systemic lupus erythematosus

- SLE is an autoimmune disease considered a type III hypersensitivity reaction in which cells and virtually every organ may be damaged by tissue-specific autoantibodies and immune complexes. [ROSH]
- Ultraviolet light triggers translocation of nuclear proteins to the epithelial cell surface, which activates lymphocytes and produces the rash or sunburn of photosensitivity. [ROSH]
- A subtype of lupus is drug-induced; commonly implicated drugs are procainamide, hydralazine and isoniazid. [ROSH]
- Approximately 90% of patients with SLE are women of reproductive age, and there is a significant genetic predisposition. [ROSH]
- The inflammatory process causes accelerated atherosclerosis and a hypercoagulable state, predisposing to atherosclerotic and thromboembolic disease; early coronary artery disease is a major cause of morbidity and mortality. [ROSH]
- SLE can involve antiphospholipid antibodies, which increase hypercoagulability and lead to complications such as recurrent pregnancy loss. [ROSH]
- Organ inflammation leads to nephritis, thrombosis, carditis, pneumonitis, pulmonary hypertension and stroke. [ROSH]

### Rheumatoid arthritis

- RA is a chronic inflammatory disease leading to deformity and destruction of joints through erosion of cartilage and bone. [PEER-PH]
- Both genetic and environmental factors contribute, and the exact cause is unknown; women are affected two to three times more often than men, with peak incidence in the 40s and 50s. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Joint involvement is symmetrical and additive, affecting the metacarpophalangeal joints, proximal interphalangeal joints, wrists and metatarsophalangeal joints, with sparing of the distal interphalangeal joints. [ROSH]
- Chronic rheumatic disease damages the transverse ligament and erodes the odontoid process, producing instability of the atlantoaxial joint that may sublux spontaneously or after trivial injury. [ROSH]
- Extra-articular inflammation may produce hepatitis, scleritis, myocarditis, pericarditis and pleuritis. Risk factors for atlantoaxial subluxation are male sex, positive anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibody, long disease duration and higher inflammatory marker levels. [ROSH]
- Felty syndrome is a rare sequel of RA with a classic triad of rheumatoid arthritis, neutropenia and splenomegaly; inflammation and an imbalance between granulocyte production and removal contribute to the neutropenia. [PEER-PH]

### Systemic sclerosis (scleroderma)

- Scleroderma is a systemic connective tissue disease caused by pathologic formation or production of collagen, with excessive accumulation of collagen and matrix in various tissues. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Widespread vascular involvement accompanies the fibrotic process and underlies both Raynaud phenomenon and renal crisis. [ROSH]
- Skin involvement is the most clinically apparent feature; early nonpitting edema of the extremities and pruritus progress to skin tightness severe enough to cause contractures, commonly of the hands, and digital ulcers. [ROSH]
- Gastrointestinal dysmotility causes severe reflux disease and small bowel bacterial overgrowth from stasis, producing chronic diarrhea; pulmonary fibrosis, pulmonary hypertension and restrictive cardiomyopathy are the major internal organ manifestations. [PEER-PH]
- Scleroderma renal crisis is a kidney emergency characterised by rapid decline in kidney function with hypertensive encephalopathy and, to a lesser extent, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia; it usually develops within the first 4 years, is more common in diffuse disease, and glucocorticoid use has been implicated as a precipitating factor. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Kidney disease is the most common cause of death in systemic sclerosis, and renal crisis affects up to 20% of patients. [ROSH]

### Raynaud phenomenon

- Raynaud phenomenon is an exaggeration of normal vasoconstriction of the digital arteries in response to cold or emotional stress; the classic sequence is pallor from vasoconstriction, then cyanosis from tissue hypoxia, then rubor from reperfusion hyperemia, and the color changes last 15–20 minutes and resolve with rewarming. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Primary (idiopathic) Raynaud disease is not associated with other conditions; secondary Raynaud syndrome occurs with connective tissue diseases such as scleroderma and systemic lupus erythematosus, in which digital ulceration and gangrene suggest systemic sclerosis. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Sarcoidosis and calcium metabolism

- Sarcoidosis is characterised by noncaseating granulomas composed of helper T cells and other inflammatory cells such as macrophages; the underlying mechanism remains undetermined. [ROSH]
- Granulomas produce 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol) in an uncontrolled manner. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- The resulting hypercalcemia is believed to occur through increased intestinal absorption of calcium due to high serum calcitriol concentrations and decreased fecal excretion; calcitriol also drives bone resorption. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Elevated calcium suppresses parathyroid hormone production by negative feedback. [ROSH]
- Hypercalciuria accompanies the hypercalcemia and predisposes to nephrolithiasis. [ROSH]

### Pathogenesis of the vasculitides

- Polyarteritis nodosa produces segmental, transmural inflammation of medium-sized arteries leading to intimal narrowing, thrombosis, end-organ ischemia and aneurysmal dilation. [ROSH]
- Unlike many other vasculitides, PAN is not associated with a specific autoantibody; hepatitis B virus infection and intravenous drug use are risk associations. [ROSH]
- Takayasu arteritis is a chronic large-vessel granulomatous vasculitis of the aorta and its major branches, including the subclavian and carotid arteries. [ROSH]
- Large artery inflammation and damage lead to decreased upper extremity pulses, unequal blood pressures between arms, tenderness over the carotid arteries, bruits, hypertension, retinopathy and limb claudication. [ROSH]
- Giant cell arteritis affects the superficial branches of the carotid artery, especially the superficial temporal artery, and threatens vision through ischemia of the eye. [PEER-PH]
- IgA vasculitis is a small-vessel vasculitis caused by deposition of IgA immune complexes; the etiology of the deposition is unclear but there is an association with a preceding upper respiratory infection. [PEER-PH]
- Kawasaki disease is an acute vasculitis with a predilection for the coronary arteries; the immunologic and inflammatory cascade is thought to weaken vessel walls and predispose them to aneurysm formation. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Post-streptococcal and post-infectious mechanisms

- Acute rheumatic fever is a type II hypersensitivity inflammatory reaction resulting from molecular cross-reactivity between *Streptococcus* and host cell proteins, with antibody formation; antibodies cross-react with tissues around the body, most notably heart, joints and skin. [ROSH]
- Acute rheumatic fever is a delayed sequela occurring 1–5 weeks after pharyngeal infection with group A *Streptococcus* (*Streptococcus pyogenes*). [PEER-PH]
- Poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis is a glomerular immune complex disease induced by specific nephritogenic strains of group A beta-hemolytic *Streptococcus*; streptococcal antigens deposit within the glomerulus and immune complexes form there, causing local damage and impaired filtration. [ROSH]
- Skin infection with *Streptococcus* leads to a later onset of poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis than pharyngeal infection. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The immune response toward streptococcal antigens increases production of antibodies and complement factors that deposit in the kidney, producing acute kidney injury. [ROSH]
- Reactive arthritis is a sterile reactive inflammatory arthritis associated with the HLA-B27 genetic marker, occurring in a susceptible host after infection with *Chlamydia trachomatis*, *Ureaplasma urealyticum*, *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Yersinia*, *Campylobacter*, *Escherichia coli* or *Clostridioides difficile*. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- MIS-C is a postinfectious overactive inflammatory response occurring 2–6 weeks after SARS-CoV-2 infection, almost exclusively in children who did not develop COVID-19 pneumonia and are in a period of viral recovery; it can produce cardiovascular involvement and cytopenias. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Transplant rejection and graft-versus-host disease

- Hyperacute rejection is a rare event occurring minutes to hours after transplantation, caused by large amounts of preformed antibodies such as anti-ABO antibodies or human leukocyte antigen mismatch. [ROSH]
- Acute rejection occurs between the time of transplant and 6 months afterwards and is caused by a recipient T-cell–mediated response. [PEER-PH]
- The acute rejection mechanism is a CD8+ T-cell-mediated process against donor tissue, with leukocyte infiltration of graft vessels impairing perfusion; it is a type IV hypersensitivity reaction analogous to a tuberculosis skin test or contact dermatitis. [ROSH]
- This delayed T-cell attack is directed against foreign major histocompatibility complexes in the donated organ and results in microvascular lesions. [ROSH]
- Chronic rejection is more antibody mediated and occurs over months to years; a CD4+ T-cell-mediated process causes proliferation of the vascular intima and fibrosis of renal vessels with marked reduction in lumen size. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Chronic rejection involves a combination of type II and type IV hypersensitivity reactions against donor tissue, producing intimal thickening, fibrosis, systemic hypertension and graft ischemia. [ROSH]
- Chronic allograft nephropathy is a pathologic process characterised by interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy that does not identify a particular underlying pathogenesis; the term chronic rejection represents an etiology but not all cases of chronic allograft nephropathy are due to alloimmune rejection. [PEER-PH]
- Risk factors for chronic allograft nephropathy are prior episodes of acute rejection, delayed graft function after transplantation, and concomitant hypertension and hyperlipidemia. [PEER-PH]
- Risk factors for acute rejection include presensitization, human leukocyte antigen mismatches, blood group incompatibility, and recipient age under 18 years; deceased donor allografts carry a slightly higher risk than living donor grafts. [ROSH]
- Graft-versus-host disease occurs when transplanted donor cells recognise recipient tissue as foreign and incite a T-cell immune response; human leukocyte antigen mismatching is the primary risk factor, with incidence ranging from 20–40% with matched sibling donors to more than 50% with unrelated donors. [ROSH]
- Bronchiolitis obliterans is generally regarded as a manifestation of chronic lung transplant rejection: submucosal lymphocytic inflammation disrupts small airway epithelium, followed by ingrowth of fibromyxoid granulation tissue that obliterates the airway lumen. [ROSH]

### Cutaneous drug reaction mechanisms

- Fixed drug eruption is believed to be associated with CD8+ T-cell activity, producing a lesion that recurs in the same area on re-exposure to the offending agent. [ROSH]
- Allergic contact dermatitis is a T cell–mediated delayed hypersensitivity reaction, whereas irritant contact dermatitis presents similarly but is not immune mediated. [ROSH]
- Type IV hypersensitivity is cell-mediated rather than humoral and is mediated by CD4+ T cells. [ROSH]
- Serum sickness is a type III hypersensitivity reaction in which IgM-containing immune complexes deposit in membranes, fix complement and cause tissue damage. [ROSH]
- Immunoglobulin M antibodies develop about 7–14 days after exposure to animal serum, form complexes with remaining animal serum, deposit in tissues, activate complement and cause mast cell degranulation. [ROSH]
- Serum sickness-like reactions are more commonly seen in children and occur after administration of various medications such as amoxicillin, cefaclor and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, despite being thought to be caused by a different mechanism from true serum sickness. [ROSH]
- Amoxicillin is an aminopenicillin; patients with anaphylaxis to penicillins are at increased risk with cephalosporins, and first-generation agents such as cephalexin share similar R1 side chains and carry the greatest cross-reactivity risk, whereas later-generation cephalosporins carry very low risk. [PEER-PH]
