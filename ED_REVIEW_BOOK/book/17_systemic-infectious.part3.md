## Diagnosis

### Recognising Sepsis and Septic Shock

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Temperature >38°C or <36°C, HR >90 bpm, RR >20/min or PaCO2 <32 mm Hg, WBC >12,000 or <4,000/mcL or >10% bands | SIRS — two or more elements required |
| Altered mental status (GCS ≤14), RR ≥22/min, SBP ≤100 mm Hg | qSOFA — two or more points |
| Hypotension refractory to fluids with lactate >2 mmol/L | Septic shock |

- SIRS is met by non-infectious insults too — heat stroke, pulmonary embolism, and recent surgery. [PEER-PH]
- Procalcitonin is more sensitive and specific for bacterial infection than CRP or leukocyte count, but metastatic and neuroendocrine tumours, medullary thyroid cancer, trauma, surgery, pancreatitis, and non-septic shock all raise it. [PEER-PH]

### Fever in the Neonate and Young Infant

- The 2021 American Academy of Pediatrics framework covers infants 8–60 days old with a rectal temperature of 38°C or greater, stratified into 8–21, 22–28, and 29–60 days because risk falls with age. [PEER-PH]
- Well-appearing febrile infants 29–90 days old who are term, appropriately immunised, feeding, and with normal vital signs need only urinalysis and urine culture — urinary tract infection is the most common bacterial infection at that age. [PEER-PH]
- Risk markers in the 8–21 day group are procalcitonin >0.5 ng/mL, CRP >20 mg/L, and absolute neutrophil count >4,000/mcL with procalcitonin available or >5,200/mcL without it. [ROSH]
- Rectal measurement is the most reliable route; axillary, temporal, and tympanic readings count as positive only above 38°C, and half of patients with tactile fever have a true fever. [ROSH]

### Bacterial Meningitis and CNS Infection

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Fever, headache, altered mental status, nuchal rigidity | Bacterial meningitis — the complete triad is uncommon |
| 1–2 mm petechiae, ecchymoses, purpura fulminans | Neisseria meningitidis |
| CSF WBC >500/mL with neutrophil predominance, high protein, low glucose, gram-negative diplococci | Meningococcal meningitis |
| Opening pressure >55 cm H2O, encapsulated budding yeast on India ink | Cryptococcal meningitis |

- Neutrophilic pleocytosis is not unique to meningococcal disease; interpret it with CSF protein, CSF glucose, and the clinical picture. [PEER-PH]
- A rash is absent in up to 20% of Rocky Mountain spotted fever, and CSF pleocytosis indicating aseptic meningitis is a common early finding. [PEER-PH]
- Cryptococcal meningitis is indolent, taking 1–2 weeks to become severe; meningismus appears in only one-quarter to one-third, so fever with lethargy or confusion is more typical. [ROSH]

### Skin, Soft Tissue, and Toxin-Mediated Disease

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Fever, hypotension, diffuse macular rash on palms and soles, progression over 48 hours | Toxic shock syndrome |
| Erythroderma, hypotension, crepitus, pain out of proportion | Necrotizing soft tissue infection |
| Haemorrhagic bullae after shucking oysters, or after handling raw shellfish in cirrhosis | Vibrio vulnificus |

- Crepitus reflects gas-forming organisms such as Clostridium perfringens; group A Streptococcus and Enterobacteriaceae are also implicated, and polymicrobial infection is usual. [PEER-PH]

### Tick-Borne and Rickettsial Illness

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Fever and rash with tick bite; rash follows fever by 2–4 days, spreading centripetally from wrists and ankles | Rocky Mountain spotted fever |
| Fever, headache, myalgias, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, transaminitis, morulae in monocytes | Ehrlichiosis |
| Malarial-like parasites on smear after an Ixodes bite, no foreign travel | Babesiosis |
| Erythema migrans expanding with central clearing | Lyme disease |

- RMSF is most commonly detected in June and July, and the vectors are Dermacentor and Rhipicephalus species. [ROSH]
- Erythema migrans is a clinical diagnosis — no laboratory testing is required, and testing is often negative early. Fever, headache, photophobia, and altered mental status weeks later in an unvaccinated patient indicates neuroborreliosis. [ROSH]

### Tuberculosis

- The tuberculin skin test is read 48–72 hours after placement, measuring induration and not erythema: 5 mm or greater is positive in HIV infection, 10 mm or greater in diabetes and other high-risk conditions, and 15 mm in an immunocompetent patient with no risk factors. [PEER-PH]
- A healthy employee with unprotected respiratory exposure is tested at exposure and again in 8–10 weeks if the first test is negative; culture of a bodily secretion or fluid is the best test to rule out tuberculosis in a patient with HIV and haemoptysis. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Malaria and Travel-Associated Fever

- Malaria is confirmed on thick and thin smears: thick smears screen for parasites, thin smears identify the species, and malaria cannot be excluded until three sets are negative 12 hours apart. [PEER-PH]
- Relapse without re-exposure after apparently completed treatment indicates Plasmodium vivax; malaria also produces haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, low haptoglobin, and abnormal renal and liver function. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The classic tertian, quartan, and quotidian periodicity is rarely seen clinically, so any fever with recent travel to an endemic region warrants screening regardless of pattern. [PEER-PH]

## Management

### Sepsis and Septic Shock — Sequence

1. Obtain blood cultures, lactate, and other early laboratory testing before antibiotics, provided this does not delay treatment.
2. Give 30 mL/kg of crystalloid within the first 3 hours for hypotension (systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg or MAP <65 mm Hg) or elevated lactate. [PEER-PH]
3. Prefer lactated Ringer or another balanced crystalloid over normal saline when multiple litres are anticipated, and reassess volume status dynamically rather than by static numbers. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
4. Start norepinephrine within 6 hours if hypotension persists after adequate fluid resuscitation; titrate to a MAP greater than 65 mm Hg. [PEER-PH]
5. Add vasopressin, then epinephrine, with phenylephrine as the last-line vasopressor. [ROSH]
6. Add stress-dose glucocorticoids only for shock persisting despite fluids and vasopressors; steroids act permissively on blood pressure and upregulate vascular alpha-1 receptors. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
7. Achieve source control — drain an abscess, remove an infected line, debride necrotic tissue.
8. Transfuse packed red blood cells at a haemoglobin less than 7 g/dL in an otherwise healthy adult; use a higher goal with active bleeding or ischaemic heart disease. [PEER-PH]

### Empiric Antimicrobial Regimens

| Scenario | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Sepsis, no Pseudomonas or multidrug-resistant risk | Ceftriaxone plus vancomycin |
| Sepsis with Pseudomonas or multidrug-resistant risk | Piperacillin-tazobactam, cefepime, or meropenem plus vancomycin |
| ESBL-producing organism | Imipenem or meropenem |
| Bacterial meningitis, adult | Ceftriaxone plus vancomycin, with dexamethasone |
| Febrile neonate 8–21 days | Ampicillin plus gentamicin, or ampicillin plus ceftazidime |
| Neonatal omphalitis with maternal chorioamnionitis | Gentamicin, metronidazole, and vancomycin |
| Necrotizing soft tissue infection of the hand | Meropenem, vancomycin, and clindamycin intravenously plus emergent debridement |
| Fournier gangrene | Piperacillin-tazobactam, vancomycin, and clindamycin |
| Toxic shock syndrome | Vancomycin and clindamycin intravenously, or linezolid and clindamycin |
| Fulminant Clostridioides difficile colitis | Oral vancomycin, adding intravenous metronidazole in fulminant disease |
| Cryptococcal meningitis | Amphotericin B plus flucytosine |
| Neurotoxoplasmosis | Pyrimethamine, sulfadiazine, and leucovorin |
| Pneumocystis pneumonia | Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for 21 days |

- Broad-spectrum coverage must include activity against MRSA and common gram-negative organisms; carbapenems outperform piperacillin-tazobactam and cefepime for sepsis and bacteraemia caused by ESBL organisms. [PEER-PH]
- Steroids are added to Pneumocystis pneumonia treatment when PaO2 is less than 70 mm Hg, the alveolar-arterial gradient is 35 or more, or room-air SpO2 is less than 92%; prednisone 40 mg orally twice daily with a 3-week taper is a standard regimen. [PEER-PH]
- Leucovorin accompanies pyrimethamine to prevent megaloblastic anaemia and pancytopenia; cytomegalovirus retinitis is treated with ganciclovir. [ROSH]

### Time-Critical Syndrome Management

1. **Botulism** — secure the airway; intubation and mechanical ventilation take priority because death is due to hypoventilation and respiratory failure. Give botulinum antitoxin on clinical suspicion without waiting for confirmatory testing, which takes 4–6 days; the state or local health department coordinates obtaining antitoxin from the CDC. Infant botulism is treated with botulism immune globulin 0.5 mL/kg/hr. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
2. **Tetanus** — control spasms with intravenous benzodiazepines such as diazepam or lorazepam, consider baclofen, and intubate with paralysis if spasms persist. Give tetanus immune globulin to neutralise circulating toxin, and arrange surgical debridement plus antibiotics to remove the devitalised tissue in which spores germinate. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
3. **Toxic shock syndrome** — rapid volume resuscitation, antibiotics covering both Staphylococcus aureus and group A Streptococcus, and clindamycin to inhibit exotoxin production. Remove the source, such as a retained vaginal foreign body or nasal packing. [ROSH]
4. **Necrotizing soft tissue infection** — broad-spectrum antibiotics with clindamycin for toxin suppression and immediate surgical consultation; delay increases morbidity and mortality. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
5. **Rocky Mountain spotted fever** — doxycycline in all age groups, started within 5 days of symptom onset without waiting for confirmatory testing. [PEER-PH]
6. **Dengue with warning signs** — intravenous fluid resuscitation, analgesia, close haemodynamic monitoring, and acetaminophen for fever; avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and aspirin because they may worsen bleeding. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
7. **Suspected viral haemorrhagic fever** — isolate the patient, notify infection control, and use full barrier protection: fluid-resistant gown, double gloves, boot covers, apron, and a powered air-purifying respirator or fit-tested N95 with face shield. [ROSH]
8. **Suspected pulmonary tuberculosis** — airborne precautions in a negative-pressure room, with an N95 or powered air-purifying respirator for staff. [PEER-PH]

### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis and Targeted Therapy

| Exposure or disease | Action |
|---|---|
| Bat in the room, bite cannot be excluded | Rabies immune globulin 20 IU/kg infiltrated in and around the wound with the remainder in the ipsilateral deltoid, plus rabies vaccine 1 mL IM in the contralateral deltoid, repeated on days 3, 7, and 14 |
| Meningococcal meningitis close contact | Ciprofloxacin, rifampin, or ceftriaxone |
| Tularemia aerosol exposure, asymptomatic | Ciprofloxacin 500 mg orally twice daily for 14 days |
| Unvaccinated complicated wound | Tetanus immune globulin plus tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine, with referral to complete the series |
| Tick attached about 48 hours, no rash | Doxycycline 200 mg orally as a single dose |
| Erythema migrans | Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 14 days |
| Neuroborreliosis or Lyme encephalitis | Ceftriaxone 2 g intravenously |
| Primary syphilis | Penicillin G benzathine 2.4 million units intramuscularly once |
| Ocular or neurosyphilis | Penicillin G 3–4 million units intravenously every 4 hours for 10–14 days |
| Severe measles requiring hospitalisation | Vitamin A 50,000 IU under 6 months, 100,000 IU at 6–11 months, 200,000 IU at 12 months and older, with a second dose the next day |

- Meningococcal prophylaxis is for prolonged close contact, intimate contact, or health care workers exposed directly to oral secretions; brief contact without secretions does not require it. [PEER-PH]
- High-risk wounds are more than 6 hours old, contaminated, ischaemic, or infected; complicated wounds include puncture wounds, avulsions, missile or crush injuries, burns, frostbite, and wounds contaminated with dirt, faeces, or saliva. The preferred tetanus formulation is tetanus, diphtheria toxoid, and acellular pertussis. [PEER-PH]
- Amoxicillin and cefuroxime have efficacy equal to doxycycline for erythema migrans, and oral amoxicillin is used in pregnancy. Intramuscular penicillin G benzathine does not produce measurable drug levels in cerebrospinal fluid, which is why neurosyphilis requires intravenous therapy. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Pregnant patients with varicella pneumonia are admitted for intravenous acyclovir; Ramsay Hunt syndrome is treated with prednisone and acyclovir. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Influenza is treated with oseltamivir, given as an oral liquid to infants; there is no intravenous or intramuscular form. Treat within 48 hours of onset where possible, but treat hospitalised patients and those with comorbidities regardless of the time of onset. Zanamivir is an alternative. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Mild COVID-19 without hypoxia is managed as an outpatient with supportive care; hypoxic disease requires admission for corticosteroids and remdesivir. Nirmatrelvir-ritonavir interacts with other drugs through cytochrome P450 inhibition. [PEER-PH]
