## Pathophysiology

### Aqueous humor dynamics and the acute glaucoma attack

- Aqueous humor is produced by the ciliary body, flows through the pupil into the anterior chamber, crosses the anterior chamber angle between cornea and iris, and leaves through the trabecular meshwork into the canal of Schlemm. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Intraocular pressure (IOP) reflects the balance between aqueous production and outflow; normal is 10–21 mm Hg. [PEER-PH]
- In primary angle-closure glaucoma the lens sits too close to the iris and impairs flow through the pupil; anterior movement of the iris then closes the angle and obstructs the canal of Schlemm. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The trigger is pupillary dilation — dim light, emotional stress, parasympatholytic and sympathomimetic drugs — which is why attacks begin in the evening or on entering a dark cinema. [PEER-PH]
- IOP rises abruptly, typically above 40 mm Hg, and may exceed 60–80 mm Hg in an acute attack. [PEER-PH]
- Ischemia of the iris caused by that pressure spike produces the characteristic mid-sized, minimally reactive pupil of 4–6 mm. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Corneal edema from the same pressure rise produces the hazy cornea, the blurred vision and the halos around lights. [PEER-PH]
- Risk is higher over age 60, with a family history of the condition and in hyperopia. [PEER-PH]
- Definitive treatment is laser iridotomy; emergency measures lower the pressure only temporarily. [PEER-PH]

### Optic nerve: demyelination versus ischemia

- Optic neuritis is inflammation of the optic nerve causing monocular vision loss; the most common cause is demyelination, and multiple sclerosis (MS) is the disease most often responsible. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The pathology mirrors MS: perivascular cuffing, edema of the myelinated nerve sheaths and myelin breakdown. [ROSH]
- Pain with extraocular movements is present in the vast majority of patients; dyschromatopsia, red color in particular, and a central scotoma are characteristic. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Optic neuritis is the presenting feature in about one-fifth of patients eventually diagnosed with MS. [ROSH]
- With a normal brain MRI, 22% of patients develop MS within 10 years of optic neuritis; with one or more cerebral white-matter lesions, 56% do. [PEER-PH]
- Oral steroids increase the 2-year and 10-year recurrence risk compared with intravenous steroids, so oral prednisone is contraindicated without prior intravenous therapy. [PEER-PH]
- Recovery is excellent: vision improves over 3–5 weeks and 90% of patients reach 20/40 or better at 1 year, though subtle color deficits and a mild relative afferent pupillary defect may persist. [PEER-PH]
- Heat or exercise can worsen symptoms — the Uhthoff phenomenon. [ROSH]

### Retinal vascular occlusion and detachment

- The central retinal artery supplies the inner retina; the outer retina, including the thin macula, is supplied by the choroidal circulation from the ciliary arteries. [PEER-PH]
- Arterial occlusion makes the inner retina pale and edematous while the translucent macula continues to show the choroidal circulation beneath it — the cherry-red spot. [PEER-PH]
- The ophthalmic artery is the first intracranial branch of the internal carotid artery and gives off the central retinal artery, so carotid plaque rupture or embolus can occlude the retinal circulation, and the same embolus can cause a cerebral infarct. [PEER-PH]
- Carotid atherosclerosis is the most common overall cause, followed by cardiogenic embolism. [PEER-PH]
- Amaurosis fugax — transient, painless, monocular visual loss that resolves spontaneously — may precede CRAO. [PEER-CS]
- Central retinal vein occlusion causes venous stasis with a blurred disc margin, dilated tortuous veins and diffuse retinal hemorrhages, described as "blood and thunder." [PEER-PH]
- Retinal detachment is separation of the neurosensory retina from the pigmented retinal epithelium, usually preceded by posterior vitreous detachment and a retinal tear that gradually enlarges. [PEER-CS]
- Age-related vitreous degeneration causes collagen fibril aggregation, liquefaction and weakened vitreoretinal adhesion; traction then tears the retina at points of strong adhesion. [ROSH]
- Risk factors for detachment include trauma, aging, diabetic retinopathy and recent eye surgery. [ROSH]

### Hemorrhage into the eye and its consequences

- Hyphema is blood or clot in the anterior chamber from damage to iris root vessels after blunt injury. [PEER-PH]
- Trauma is the most common cause, but hyphema also occurs spontaneously in hemophilia and sickle cell disease and in patients taking anticoagulants. [PEER-PH]
- Increased IOP occurs in one-third of hyphema patients through occlusion of the trabecular meshwork, and the larger the hyphema the greater the risk. [PEER-PH]
- Rebleeding is the most common complication of untreated traumatic hyphema; it occurs 3–5 days after the original insult in up to 30% of cases. [PEER-PH]
- In sickle cell disease, sickled erythrocytes are less pliable and have greater difficulty exiting the trabecular meshwork, so IOP rises disproportionately. [PEER-CS]
- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors lower aqueous pH in the anterior chamber, which promotes sickling, clogs the trabecular meshwork and can raise IOP further — hence their contraindication in sickle cell disease. [PEER-PH]
- In hemophilia A, factor VIII deficiency makes ocular bleeding a severe event; factor VIII is given to achieve a 100% rise in factor level. [PEER-PH]
- Orbital compartment syndrome occurs when a retrobulbar hematoma raises pressure within the fixed orbit, with IOP above 30 mm Hg and severe visual loss. [PEER-CS]

### The vestibular system: peripheral versus central

- Vertigo is an illusion of motion, classically described as the room spinning and exacerbated by head movement or position change. [ROSH]
- BPPV arises when otoliths from the utricle are displaced into the posterior semicircular canal; rapid head turning moves them within the canal and produces the illusion of rotation, and they settle within 3–5 seconds. [ROSH]
- Ménière disease is idiopathic endolymphatic hydrops — excessive volume and disturbed flow of endolymph within the inner ear. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The labyrinth serves hearing (cochlea) and balance (semicircular canals) and transmits through the eighth cranial nerve. [PEER-PH]
- Vestibular neuritis affects only the vestibular branch, so hearing is preserved; labyrinthitis affects the whole labyrinthine organ and adds hearing loss or tinnitus. [PEER-PH]
- Labyrinthitis may be serous, from an inflammatory response to a nearby viral infection, or suppurative, from otitis media, mastoiditis or meningitis. [ROSH]
- Suppurative labyrinthitis is the most serious complication of acute otitis media because the labyrinth lies in close proximity to the brain. [PEER-PH]
- In labyrinthitis the nystagmus is unidirectional and horizontal and intensifies on gaze away from the affected ear; cerebellar stroke causes direction-changing, vertical or pure torsional nystagmus. [PEER-PH]
- About one-quarter of cases of acute vestibular syndrome are due to a central cause, so the HINTS examination is used to separate the two. [PEER-PH]

### Why head and neck infection obstructs the airway

- Ludwig angina is a polymicrobial infection of the submental, sublingual and submandibular spaces; more than two-thirds of cases begin as a periapical abscess of the mandibular molars that breaks through the cortical plate of the mandible. [PEER-PH]
- The swelling displaces the tongue posteriorly, and the edema progresses in a non-linear fashion, so asphyxiation can be sudden. [PEER-PH]
- Late signs of airway compromise are stridor, inability to manage secretions and cyanosis; trismus is usually absent early and appears as the infection spreads to the parapharyngeal regions. [PEER-PH]
- Orbital cellulitis arises deep to the orbital septum, most often by local extension from the ethmoid sinus, and can seed the cavernous sinus. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Cavernous sinus thrombosis is usually a late complication of acute infectious sinusitis, particularly sphenoid or ethmoid sinusitis, and less often follows bacteremia or infection of the maxillary teeth. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The internal carotid artery and cranial nerves III, IV, VI and portions of V traverse the cavernous sinus, which explains the ophthalmoplegia, gaze palsy and facial sensory loss. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Orbital cellulitis can produce subperiosteal abscess, orbital abscess (the most common), cavernous sinus thrombosis and brain abscess, with vision loss in up to 11% of cases. [PEER-PH]
- Mastoiditis follows obstruction of the aditus ad antrum, so the mastoid air cells fill with fluid and become inflamed and the infection can erode into the temporal bone. [PEER-PH]
- Intracranial complications of mastoiditis — intracranial abscess, lateral cerebral sinus thrombosis, meningitis and acute petrositis — occur in 4% to 7% of cases. [PEER-CS]
- Basilar skull fractures commonly involve the petrous ridge of the temporal bone, so hearing loss is a common presenting symptom and may be conductive or sensorineural depending on the fracture location. [PEER-CS]
- Dural tears from basilar skull fracture give rise to cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leaks in 12% to 30% of cases; beta-2 transferrin is found exclusively in CSF, perilymph and aqueous humor and identifies the fluid. [PEER-CS]

### Bradykinin, histamine and swelling of the face

- Anaphylaxis is a type I hypersensitivity reaction involving two or more organ systems; in most patients symptoms peak within 30 to 60 minutes of onset. [PEER-CS]
- Angioedema from angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors is mediated by bradykinin accumulation, which increases vascular permeability and vasodilation. It is typically nonpitting and lacks urticaria or rash. [PEER-CS]
- ACE inhibitor angioedema accounts for up to 30% of angioedema-related emergency department visits. [PEER-CS]
- Hereditary angioedema is autosomal dominant, caused by mutations in the C1 esterase inhibitor gene; deficient or dysfunctional C1-INH allows overproduction of bradykinin. [PEER-CS]
- Hereditary angioedema often begins as minor swelling in early childhood, with puberty triggering more severe attacks; the upper respiratory tract is commonly affected, as are the abdominal organs and extremities. [PEER-CS]
- Histamine-mediated allergic angioedema, by contrast, is accompanied by urticaria and pruritus, the features absent in bradykinin-mediated disease. [PEER-CS]

### Immune-mediated disease of the eye and mouth

- Anterior uveitis is associated with the seronegative spondyloarthropathies; ankylosing spondylitis carries a uveitis frequency of approximately 25% to 30%, and 50% of patients with recurrent iritis have a spondyloarthropathy. [PEER-PH]
- Behçet syndrome is a vasculitis with recurrent oral ulceration in almost 100% of cases, plus genital ulcers and uveitis; it may also involve the gastrointestinal tract, nervous system, joints and vessels. [PEER-PH]
- Aphthous ulcers are multifactorial, noninfectious inflammatory lesions of nonkeratinized oral tissue; herpes labialis prefers keratinized tissue outside the lips and around the mouth. [PEER-PH]
- Oral leukoplakia is a premalignant white patch that cannot be scraped off, unlike the plaque of oral candidiasis, which scrapes away. [ROSH]
- Kaposi sarcoma is a vascular neoplasm related to human herpesvirus 8 and is an AIDS-defining illness; it affects the oral cavity in one-third of patients, most often the palate and gingiva. [ROSH]
- Oral squamous cell carcinoma typically arises on the lateral surface of the tongue, and the first site of spread is the cervical lymph nodes. [PEER-CS]
