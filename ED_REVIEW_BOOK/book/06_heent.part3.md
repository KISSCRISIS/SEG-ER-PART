## Diagnosis

### Monocular vision loss

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Painless loss, pale retina, cherry-red macula, afferent pupillary defect | Central retinal artery occlusion (CRAO) |
| Painless loss, blurred disc margin, dilated tortuous veins, hemorrhages | Central retinal vein occlusion |
| Painless loss with flashes, floaters and a curtain after eye surgery | Retinal detachment |
| Painful loss worse on eye movement, loss of red color vision | Optic neuritis |
| Painful loss with halos, mid-dilated pupil, hazy cornea | Acute angle-closure glaucoma |
| Painful loss with hypopyon after cataract surgery | Endophthalmitis |
| Painless loss over 50 with jaw claudication, headache, raised ESR | Giant cell arteritis |

- Ophthalmoscopy is frequently normal in retinal detachment because most tears are peripheral; ultrasound shows a floating hyperechoic membrane attached to the optic disc. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Bilateral disc edema from raised intracranial pressure is papilledema; idiopathic intracranial hypertension adds transient visual obscurations and a sixth-nerve palsy. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

### The red eye

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Cells and flare, ciliary flush, constricted poorly reactive pupil, normal intraocular pressure | Iritis (anterior uveitis) |
| Layering of white cells in the anterior chamber (hypopyon) | Endophthalmitis or severe infectious keratitis |
| Dendritic ulcer or corneal vesicles on fluorescein staining | Herpes keratitis |
| Corneal defect with fluorescein uptake in a contact lens wearer | Infectious keratitis |
| Superficial punctate lesions 6–12 hours after ultraviolet exposure | Radiation keratitis |

- Relief of pain with topical anesthetic drops is a hallmark of corneal abrasion; fluorescein with cobalt blue light enhances the defect, and the lid is everted in every abrasion. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- A rust ring is metallic sediment that forms within hours of an iron foreign body and reaccumulates within 24 hours unless the foreign body is removed. Viral conjunctivitis is the most common acute red eye and the most common infectious conjunctivitis in adults, and it stays contagious while discharge persists; neonatal conjunctivitis at 2–5 days of life with copious purulent discharge and chemosis suggests gonococcal infection with gram-negative diplococci on Gram stain. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### The dizzy patient

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Seconds of spinning provoked by head turning, under a minute, positive Dix-Hallpike | Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo |
| Recurrent vertigo lasting 20 minutes to hours with tinnitus, aural fullness and hearing loss | Ménière disease |
| Vertigo with hearing loss or tinnitus after a viral illness or rhinosinusitis | Labyrinthitis |
| Vertigo without hearing loss, abnormal head impulse with corrective saccades | Vestibular neuritis |
| Direction-changing, vertical or nonfatigable nystagmus with a normal head impulse test | Central cause, including cerebellar stroke |

- Sensorineural hearing loss lateralizes the Weber to the unaffected ear with air conduction greater than bone conduction on Rinne; conductive loss lateralizes to the affected ear. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

### The painful or draining ear

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Itching progressing to pain, canal erythema and edema, pain on moving the pinna | Otitis externa |
| Severe pain, profuse otorrhea, canal granulation tissue, diabetes, cranial nerve VII palsy | Necrotizing (malignant) otitis externa |
| Painful red swollen auricle sparing the lobule after piercing | Perichondritis |
| Bulging, erythematous tympanic membrane with impaired mobility | Acute otitis media |
| Postauricular erythema, bony tenderness and protrusion of the auricle | Mastoiditis |
| Blood behind the tympanic membrane after trauma | Hemotympanum suggesting basilar skull fracture |
| Bloody otorrhea and hearing loss after a slap or cotton-tipped applicator | Traumatic tympanic membrane perforation |

- Acute otitis media is supported by moderate to severe bulging of the tympanic membrane, new otorrhea not from otitis externa, or mild bulging with recent ear pain or intense erythema. [PEER-PH]
- CT of the temporal bone without contrast is indicated in traumatic perforation with focal neurologic deficit, signs of basilar skull fracture, significant hearing loss or a possible retained foreign body; suspected cerebrospinal fluid leak is confirmed by beta-2 transferrin immunoassay. [PEER-CS]

### Nose, face and neck

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Unilateral purulent nasal discharge in a toddler failing antibiotics | Nasal foreign body |
| Disc-shaped radiopaque object in the nose | Button battery |
| Bilateral congestion, sneezing, itchy watery eyes, atopy, no sinus tenderness | Allergic rhinitis |
| Symptoms beyond 10 days, or worsening after initial improvement, with fever and purulent discharge | Bacterial rhinosinusitis |
| Bleeding not controlled by anterior pressure with blood in the posterior oropharynx | Posterior epistaxis from the sphenopalatine artery |
| Cystic mass anterior to the sternocleidomastoid in a child under 5 | Branchial cleft cyst |
| Progressive neck pain, raised ESR, recent urinary infection | Spondylodiscitis (discitis or vertebral osteomyelitis) |
| Neck and bilateral arm swelling with a lung mass and stridor | Superior vena cava syndrome |

### Sore throat and oral lesions

- Rapid antigen test for group A beta-hemolytic Streptococcus; treat if positive and culture when negative, while cough, runny nose and conjunctivitis point to viral pharyngitis. Uvular deviation with trismus and a muffled "hot potato" voice indicates peritonsillar abscess. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- A white plaque that scrapes off is oral candidiasis and a patch that cannot be scraped off is leukoplakia, while a fixed white plaque on the tongue is the finding most strongly associated with oral cancer. A gray pseudomembrane over blunted, ulcerated interdental papillae that bleeds when scraped is acute necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis. [ROSH]

## Management

### Sight-threatening ocular emergencies

1. Chemical injury: irrigate at once with normal saline or any drinkable fluid, without delaying to check acuity, until the ocular pH is 7.0–7.4. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
2. Acute angle-closure glaucoma: elevate the head of the bed and give topical timolol; if the pressure does not fall, add acetazolamide 500 mg IV or PO with apraclonidine 1% and pilocarpine 1–2% drops every 15 minutes, with mannitol as an alternative. Laser iridotomy is definitive. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
3. Orbital compartment syndrome: immediate lateral canthotomy and cantholysis dividing the lateral canthal ligament, then recheck the intraocular pressure; watch for the oculocardiac reflex and profound bradycardia. [PEER-CS]
4. Suspected CRAO: emergent ophthalmology and neurology for ocular massage, intra-arterial thrombolytics or anterior chamber paracentesis, and transfer from a stand-alone department to a stroke center with both services. [PEER-PH]
5. CRAO with jaw claudication and a raised ESR: intravenous methylprednisolone at once, before biopsy. [PEER-PH]
6. Hyphema: bed rest with the head of the bed at 45 degrees; admit for hemoglobinopathy, raised intraocular pressure, secondary bleeding or penetrating trauma, and discharge grade I or II hyphema without these with follow-up in 2 to 7 days. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
7. Retinal detachment, posterior vitreous detachment, hypopyon and endophthalmitis: emergent ophthalmology, with intravitreal antibiotic injection for endophthalmitis. [ROSH]
8. Corneal abrasion and foreign body: evert the lids, give topical antibiotics and oral NSAIDs, and review contact lens injury within 24 hours with Pseudomonas coverage (ciprofloxacin, ofloxacin, tobramycin). Remove the foreign body and cover Bacillus and Clostridium; eye patches are contraindicated and a wood splinter is the most urgent removal. Topical corticosteroids are not used for conjunctivitis. [PEER-PH][ROSH][PEER-CS]

### Airway-threatening infection

1. Failing airway: stridor, inability to manage secretions, increased work of breathing, cyanosis.
2. Epiglottitis: position of comfort, defer vascular access, secure the airway in the operating room; a supraglottic airway device is contraindicated. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
3. Epiglottitis with less than 50% narrowing: intensive care admission with ceftriaxone, or cefepime and vancomycin if immunocompromised, and diabetes raises the likelihood of airway intervention. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
4. Ludwig angina: early awake nasotracheal fiberoptic intubation with tracheostomy if intubation fails, intensive care, fluids, broad-spectrum antibiotics and ENT for drainage. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
5. Peritonsillar abscess: assess the airway, then needle aspiration; discharge after drainage is acceptable if oral intake is preserved, with amoxicillin-clavulanate and follow-up within 24 to 36 hours. [PEER-CS]

### Antimicrobial choices

| Scenario | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Acute otitis media, first line | Amoxicillin 90 mg/kg/day PO for 5–10 days |
| After amoxicillin failure | Amoxicillin-clavulanate |
| Penicillin allergy | Cefdinir, cefuroxime, cefpodoxime or clindamycin |
| Adult otitis media without effusion or systemic infection | Amoxicillin-clavulanate for 7 days |
| Mastoiditis with recurrent otitis media or recent antibiotics | Vancomycin plus an antipseudomonal such as cefepime |
| Mastoiditis in a child with recurrent otitis media | Piperacillin-tazobactam or ampicillin-sulbactam |
| Necrotizing otitis externa | Ciprofloxacin, ceftazidime or piperacillin-tazobactam |
| Otitis externa with tympanic membrane perforation | Ciprofloxacin otic plus dexamethasone otic |
| Suppurative perichondritis | Ciprofloxacin — oral if mild, intravenous if severe or with abscess |
| Bacterial rhinosinusitis | Amoxicillin-clavulanate |
| Ludwig angina with penicillin anaphylaxis | Levofloxacin plus metronidazole |

### Ear, nose and throat procedures

- Perforated tympanic membrane: avoid acidic preparations (acetic acid, alcohols) and ototoxic aminoglycosides (neomycin, tobramycin, gentamicin); use ciprofloxacin with dexamethasone, or ofloxacin. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Necrotizing otitis externa — otitis externa failing topical therapy with granulation tissue or extension beyond the canal — needs parenteral antipseudomonal therapy, admission, diabetes control with insulin and fluids, and ENT involvement. Perichondritis needs the earring or foreign body removed, antipseudomonal cover, and ENT drainage with debridement of necrotic cartilage. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Impacted cerumen is softened with mineral oil, half-strength hydrogen peroxide, sodium bicarbonate or carbamide peroxide otic for about half an hour, and a live insect is killed with lidocaine solution, mineral oil or ethanol before removal. [PEER-PH][ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Ear foreign bodies: a right-angle hook, balloon catheter or irrigation for round objects, alligator forceps for soft or irregular objects, and a glue-covered cotton applicator for a round object filling the canal. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Epistaxis: direct pressure or a nasal clamp with oxymetazoline, silver nitrate cautery for a visible bleeding point, then a nasal tampon or balloon; brisk bleeding despite anterior measures indicates a posterior source needing a balloon or Foley catheter, a double-balloon epistaxis catheter or posterior packing. [ROSH]
- Tracheostomy: hemorrhage is the most common early complication, and massive late bleeding suggests a tracheoinnominate artery fistula — overinflate the cuff, apply internal and external pressure through the stoma, involve surgery. A mucus plug a suction catheter cannot pass is managed by removing the tube. [PEER-PH]

### Dental, oral and salivary

- Periapical abscess: penicillin or amoxicillin with or without metronidazole, analgesia, incision and drainage, and urgent dental follow-up; with soft tissue involvement use amoxicillin-clavulanate or ampicillin-sulbactam, or metronidazole plus levofloxacin in penicillin allergy, and fever or malaise means systemic infection. Trismus indicates extension into the masticator space: contrast-enhanced CT of the neck. [ROSH][PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Dry socket is irrigated with chlorhexidine 0.12% or warmed normal saline, or packed with iodoform gauze moistened with oil of clove, with analgesia. Acute necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis needs local debridement, chlorhexidine rinses, analgesics, penicillin and dental referral, with resolution usually dramatic within 48 hours. Aphthous stomatitis: dexamethasone elixir, with lesions resolving in 10 to 14 days; oral candidiasis: clotrimazole troches. [PEER-PH][ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Dental concussion with tenderness but no movement of an intact tooth: soft diet and pediatric dentist in 48 to 72 hours, and ibuprofen for barodontalgia after diving. Anterior temporomandibular joint dislocation reduces with the syringe technique, the patient rolling a 10 mL syringe between the teeth; temporomandibular joint disorder is treated with NSAIDs and amitriptyline for chronic refractory pain. [PEER-PH][ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Sialolithiasis: analgesics, sialogogues, sour hard candy and gentle massage; suppurative parotitis with fever: ampicillin-sulbactam; tinea capitis: oral griseofulvin or terbinafine, avoiding ketoconazole. [ROSH][PEER-PH][PEER-CS]

### Anaphylaxis, angioedema and dystonia

- Anaphylaxis: intramuscular epinephrine 1:1,000 at 0.01 mg/kg (maximum 0.5 mg) every 5 to 15 minutes, then an intravenous infusion at 0.05 to 1.0 mcg/kg/min if two doses fail. Severe tongue and oropharyngeal swelling with imminent airway loss: intravenous ketamine for intubation, and stop any ACE inhibitor. [PEER-CS]
- Acute dystonia: diphenhydramine, or benztropine as an alternative, resolving in 10 to 30 minutes and sometimes needing repeat dosing because of the short half-life; patients with cluster headache are screened for suicidal ideation, anxiety and depression. [PEER-CS]
