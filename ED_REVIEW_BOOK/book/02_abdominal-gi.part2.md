## Pathophysiology

### Hernia Formation and Strangulation

- Indirect inguinal hernia in an infant is a disorder of the processus vaginalis, an invagination of parietal peritoneum that precedes testicular descent; the testes descend between 25 and 35 weeks of gestation, after which the processus normally obliterates, and incomplete closure leaves a patent pathway. The resulting fascial defect is small with a narrow neck, which is why indirect hernias in infants and children are at higher risk of incarceration. [PEER-PH]
- Inguinal hernias are classified as indirect, entering the internal inguinal ring lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels, or direct, passing through the floor of Hesselbach triangle. Indirect hernias account for two-thirds of all inguinal hernias. Acquired abdominal wall weakness underlies direct, femoral, and Spigelian hernias at anatomically weak points: the umbilical ring, semilunar line, ostomy sites, both inguinal regions, and the esophageal hiatus. [PEER-PH]
- A Spigelian hernia develops in the Spigelian aponeurosis between the internal oblique and transversus abdominis; because the defect lies between fascial layers rather than beneath subcutaneous fat it is thin and small, so strangulation risk is disproportionately high — about 20% present incarcerated and up to 15% strangulated. A femoral hernia passes through the narrow, rigid femoral ring, so strangulation and incarceration are far more common than with inguinal hernias even though femoral hernias account for only 4% of groin hernias. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Incarceration becomes strangulation when venous outflow and lymphatic drainage are occluded by a tight fascial ring; progressive edema and swelling within a fixed defect then compromise arterial inflow, producing ischemia, mucosal ulceration, and gangrenous bowel with perforation or obstruction. Strangulation is unlikely without at least one systemic feature: temperature over 38°C, heart rate over 90 bpm, respiratory rate over 20 breaths/min, or leukocytosis. [PEER-PH]
- Attempted manual reduction returns an incarcerated hernia to the abdomen and is contraindicated when the overlying skin is erythematous, because ischemic bowel may be reduced into the peritoneal cavity. Umbilical hernia results from incomplete closure of the fascia of the umbilical ring, occurs in 10% to 30% of births with a higher prevalence in Black infants, and usually closes spontaneously because the aponeurotic fascial edges continue to grow. [PEER-PH]

### Reflux, Motility, and Esophageal Mucosal Injury

- The gastroesophageal barrier is maintained by the lower esophageal sphincter, the crural diaphragm, and the intra-abdominal position of the distal esophagus. Reduced sphincter tone, increased gastric emptying time, or esophageal dysmotility permit reflux; nicotine, caffeine, calcium channel blockers, ethanol, and anticholinergic drugs lower sphincter tone. Reflux is worse supine at night because gravity no longer opposes retrograde flow, and refluxate reaching the proximal esophagus produces globus, hoarseness, and cough. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Repeated acid exposure produces erosive esophagitis, and healing is accompanied by fibrosis that yields a peptic stricture at or near the gastroesophageal junction; the fixed narrowing produces progressive dysphagia to solids and can culminate in food impaction. Chronic reflux also produces cellular metaplasia of the distal esophagus, the substrate for Barrett esophagus and adenocarcinoma. [PEER-PH]
- Pill-induced esophagitis follows direct contact injury when a tablet lodges in the collapsed esophagus; doxycycline is a frequent cause because of its acidic nature, and ingestion immediately before sleep raises risk because salivation and swallowing frequency are markedly reduced during sleep. Achalasia results from myenteric aganglionosis, in which loss of inhibitory neurons prevents lower esophageal sphincter relaxation and abolishes orderly peristalsis. Distal esophageal spasm produces a corkscrew appearance on barium study. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Esophageal Perforation and Caustic Injury

- Boerhaave syndrome is a transmural rupture caused by a sudden rise in intraluminal pressure against a closed cricopharyngeus, with Valsalva during straining, forceful vomiting, coughing, heavy lifting, and childbirth as typical precipitants. Spontaneous rupture is most often of the distal esophagus, whereas traumatic rupture favors the proximal and middle thirds. Loss of barrier integrity allows saliva and gastric contents to enter the mediastinal, pleural, or peritoneal space, triggering a marked infectious and inflammatory response that can progress to mediastinitis, empyema, and septic shock, with mortality approaching 50%. Iatrogenic perforation from upper endoscopy is the most common cause overall. [PEER-PH]
- Corrosive alkalis cause liquefaction necrosis: hydroxide ions saponify lipids and denature proteins, permitting deep penetration and full-thickness injury with a high risk of stricture formation. Corrosive acids cause coagulation necrosis, in which hydrogen ions kill surface cells and produce a protective eschar that limits penetration, so acid injury is less deep than alkali injury but carries higher mortality because of severe gastric damage. Household bleach, typically a 3% to 6% sodium hypochlorite solution at pH 11, is dilute and only minimally corrosive, which is why small accidental ingestions in asymptomatic patients can be managed with discharge and follow-up. [PEER-PH]
- Button battery injury is electrochemical rather than simple impaction: current flowing through adjacent tissue generates hydroxide at the negative pole, producing caustic liquefaction necrosis that can perforate within 6 hours, and the double-ring sign on an anteroposterior radiograph reflects the two rims of the battery. Foreign body impaction follows anatomy — most pediatric esophageal foreign bodies lodge at the cricopharyngeus and most adult ones at the lower esophageal sphincter. Because the pediatric esophagus is compressible, an esophageal foreign body can obstruct the trachea and present with respiratory symptoms rather than GI symptoms. [PEER-PH]

### Portal Hypertension and Variceal Hemorrhage

- Portal hypertension raises the pressure gradient in the portosystemic collateral circulation, enlarging collaterals in the distal esophagus, gastric fundus, rectum, and anterior abdominal wall. Variceal rupture occurs when wall tension exceeds vessel capacity, and risk rises with larger variceal diameter, red wale marks, worsening liver function, and prior hemorrhage. Varices in the distal esophagus lie superficially within the mucosa, whereas those in the mid-esophagus lie deep in the submucosa, which is why the distal esophagus is the most common site of variceal bleeding. Variceal bleeding accounts for only 10% to 20% of all upper GI bleeds but is one of the leading causes of death in cirrhosis. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Octreotide is a somatostatin analogue that inhibits release of vasodilatory hormones and causes splanchnic vasoconstriction, decreasing portal venous inflow and portal pressure within seconds; it achieves earlier hemostasis and fewer rebleeds but has not been shown to reduce mortality. A transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt creates a tract between the portal vein and the hepatic vein, bypassing the liver to lower portal pressure, raising survival in severe variceal bleeding from 10% to 60% and achieving hemostasis in over 90% of patients, but worsening hyperammonemia by shunting nitrogenous waste past hepatic metabolism. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Balloon tamponade applies direct pressure to varices as a bridge to endoscopy; pressure must be lowered in a stepwise fashion, by 5 mm Hg every 3 hours down to 25 mm Hg in the esophageal balloon, to limit pressure necrosis, and the gastric balloon is inflated to 30 to 40 mm Hg only after radiography confirms it is below the diaphragm, with esophageal perforation the most lethal complication. Mallory-Weiss tears result from a sudden rise in intra-abdominal pressure against a closed glottis, producing a longitudinal partial-thickness mucosal laceration at the gastroesophageal junction with bleeding from submucosal arteries or veins. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Cirrhotic patients with GI bleeding bleed into a gut lumen loaded with bacteria, and bleeding causes a further fall in systemic vascular resistance; infection develops in 30% to 40% of cirrhotic patients with GI bleeding without prophylaxis, most often gram-negative, and bacterial translocation also precipitates variceal rebleeding and death. [PEER-PH]

### Peptic Ulcer Disease and Helicobacter pylori

- Peptic ulcer disease results from an imbalance between aggressive factors, gastric acid and pepsin, and mucosal defense. Helicobacter pylori infects 30% to 40% of the United States population and causes 70% to 80% of duodenal ulcers and 60% to 70% of gastric ulcers, with NSAIDs the second leading cause. H. pylori- and NSAID-related ulcers penetrate the mucosa and can erode into the gastroduodenal artery, causing massive arterial hemorrhage; bleeding is the most common complication, occurring in 15% to 20% of patients. [ROSH]
- Duodenal ulcer pain develops 2 to 3 hours after a meal because food buffers acid initially, whereas gastric ulcer pain begins minutes after eating. An intravenous proton pump inhibitor raises intragastric pH and stabilizes clot over an ulcer bed, reducing rebleeding. Bismuth quadruple therapy for H. pylori combines bismuth subsalicylate 300 mg four times daily, metronidazole 250 mg four times daily, tetracycline 500 mg four times daily, and lansoprazole 30 mg twice daily for 10 to 14 days. Perforation releases gastric and duodenal contents into the peritoneal cavity, producing chemical then bacterial peritonitis, with pain that is constant and radiates to the back, upper chest, or shoulders through diaphragmatic irritation. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Gallstone Formation and Biliary Inflammation

- Cholesterol stones form when the cholesterol content of bile is elevated relative to bile salts and phospholipids, so cholesterol crystallizes and nucleates. Pigment stones are composed mainly of calcium bilirubinate, with black stones accompanying intravascular hemolysis in sickle cell disease and brown stones accompanying biliary infection. Risk factors are advanced age, female sex, parity, obesity, rapid weight loss, family history, and oral contraceptive use; estrogen therapy increases biliary cholesterol secretion, fibrates reduce bile acid secretion, ceftriaxone promotes biliary sludging, and somatostatin analogues impair gallbladder emptying. [PEER-PH]
- Biliary colic occurs when a stone transiently obstructs the cystic duct: gallbladder contraction against a fixed obstruction raises intraluminal pressure, producing constant pain that peaks around midnight and radiates to the right shoulder or scapula through diaphragmatic irritation. Acute calculous cholecystitis occurs when obstruction of the gallbladder neck or cystic duct lasts long enough that the gallbladder cannot empty, so retained bile raises pressure, impairs mucosal perfusion, and produces wall inflammation with secondary bacterial infection; Escherichia coli, Enterococcus, Klebsiella, and Enterobacter are the usual organisms. Emphysematous cholecystitis is the same process with gas-producing organisms and is a disease of older men with diabetes. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Choledocholithiasis obstructs the common bile duct and raises conjugated bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, and liver enzymes. Cholangitis develops when obstruction and stasis permit bacterial translocation into the biliary tree, and infected bile under pressure decompresses into the hepatic veins and the systemic circulation, producing bacteremia with altered mental status and hypotension. Ascending cholangitis damages the duct epithelium, and repeated cycles of inflammation are the substrate for cholangiocarcinoma. Retained stones after cholecystectomy occur in up to 10% of patients. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- A pancreatic pseudocyst is a collection of pancreatic fluid enclosed by a fibrous wall and is a benign complication of chronic pancreatitis; mass effect on the biliary tree produces obstructive jaundice and is treated with endoscopic ultrasound-guided drainage. Carcinoid syndrome arises from metastatic midgut neuroendocrine tumors that secrete serotonin and other vasoactive substances into the systemic circulation, bypassing hepatic first-pass metabolism, producing flushing, diarrhea, bronchospasm, and right-sided cardiac valve disease. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Acute and Chronic Pancreatitis

- Acute pancreatitis begins with injury to acinar cells that permits premature, intrapancreatic activation of trypsinogen to trypsin. Activated trypsin then activates other zymogens, including phospholipase A2, elastase, and lipase, digesting cellular membranes and interstitial tissues and producing cellular breakdown, focal injury, edema, and interstitial inflammation. Enzyme activation results either from obstruction of the pancreatic duct, which raises ductal pressure and permits activation within the ducts, or from direct acinar toxicity. Between 5% and 10% develop necrotizing pancreatitis, in which autodigestion, microvascular thrombosis, and ischemia produce pancreatic and peripancreatic necrosis. [ROSH]
- Severe disease with third-spacing, vomiting, and reduced oral intake produces hypovolemia and pancreatic hypoperfusion, which worsens necrosis, so crystalloid resuscitation of 30 mL/kg is the immediate priority; normal saline is preferred when hypercalcemia is present because lactated Ringer contains 3 mEq/L of calcium. Ranson criteria are scored in two steps, five criteria on admission and six more at 48 hours; a glucose over 200 mg/dL and age over 55 years on admission predict higher mortality, and a temperature of 38.5°C with a heart rate of 120 bpm at admission and at 48 hours predicts severe disease. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Hypertriglyceridemia causes pancreatitis when triglycerides exceed 500 mg/dL, with more severe inflammation above 1,000 mg/dL, because chylomicron-rich plasma is viscous and releases free fatty acids that injure the capillary endothelium and acinar cells; both gallstone and hypertriglyceridemia-associated pancreatitis occur in pregnancy and raise maternal and fetal mortality. Chronic pancreatitis follows repeated episodes of acute injury, with progressive fibrosis and loss of exocrine and endocrine function, and alcohol is the most common cause. Carcinoid and pancreatic malignancy both arise from repeated inflammation and cellular turnover. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Cirrhosis, Encephalopathy, Ascites, and Hepatorenal Physiology

- Cirrhosis is progressive fibrosis with nodular regeneration that destroys normal hepatic architecture, impairing synthetic function, detoxification, and portal flow, with coagulopathy, jaundice, encephalopathy, portal hypertension, varices, and ascites as consequences. Alcohol-related liver disease reflects proinflammatory cytokine release during chronic heavy intake, usually more than 100 g of alcohol daily for years; hepatocyte injury and steatosis produce transaminases typically under 300 IU/L with an AST:ALT ratio greater than 2, because alcohol depletes pyridoxine and preferentially damages mitochondria, and one-month mortality approaches 35%. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Portal hypertension drives fluid into the peritoneal cavity through raised hydrostatic pressure in the splanchnic capillaries, compounded by low oncotic pressure from hypoalbuminemia and by renal sodium and water retention, which is why ascites is the most common complication of alcohol-related cirrhosis. The failing liver clears less nitrogenous waste and portosystemic collaterals shunt blood past hepatocytes, so ammonia and other neurotoxins reach the brain. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Lactulose lowers ammonia by three mechanisms: its hyperosmolar load is metabolized by colonic bacteria and draws water into the colon, producing a laxative effect that reduces ammonia absorption; it acidifies stool, trapping ammonia as non-absorbable ammonium; and it stimulates colonic bacteria to consume ammonia as a nitrogen source. Constipation increases colonic ammonia production, GI bleeding provides a protein load, and infection and sedating medication increase cerebral susceptibility. Benzodiazepines such as diazepam, clonazepam, and midazolam undergo phase I hepatic metabolism, which is impaired in cirrhosis, prolonging sedation; lorazepam, cleared by phase II glucuronidation, is the safer choice. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis arises from translocation of enteric bacteria across the gut wall into ascitic fluid that is already a favorable culture medium, aided by impaired local immunity and reduced opsonic activity, with E. coli, Enterococcus, and Streptococcus species most commonly implicated. Hepatorenal syndrome is functional renal failure: splanchnic arterial vasodilation in cirrhosis reduces effective circulating volume, activating the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, sympathetic nervous system, and antidiuretic hormone, so the kidneys respond with intense renal vasoconstriction and creatinine rises despite histologically normal kidneys; spontaneous bacterial peritonitis is one of the most common risk factors, present in up to 25% to 30%, and albumin infusion improves mortality. [PEER-CS][PEER-PH]
- Acetaminophen is the most common cause of drug-induced liver injury in the United States and is a predictable, dose-dependent reaction, whereas idiosyncratic reactions are patient-specific and dose-independent. Insulin resistance is the key mechanism of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, and the steatohepatitis subset is distinguished from simple steatosis only by liver biopsy. Repeated cycles of inflammation, hepatocyte death, and compensatory regeneration in cirrhosis provide the substrate for hepatocellular carcinoma, which develops almost exclusively in cirrhotic livers at an estimated 1% to 4% per year. [PEER-PH]

### Intestinal Obstruction, Ileus, and Volvulus

- Small bowel obstruction is a mechanical blockage that raises intraluminal pressure proximal to the obstruction; secretions and swallowed air accumulate, the bowel distends, and peristalsis increases against the block, producing colicky pain, while vomiting and sequestration of fluid in the gut lumen cause dehydration, hypokalemia, and metabolic alkalosis. Postoperative adhesions account for more than 60% of cases, usually from a single band, and adhesive obstruction usually occurs within 3 months of surgery but can occur decades later. Rising intraluminal pressure reduces bowel wall perfusion, and a distended loop can twist into a closed-loop obstruction that compromises venous outflow, then arterial inflow, and culminates in ischemia, necrosis, and perforation. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Large bowel obstruction is most often caused by colorectal cancer; the smaller luminal diameter and higher intraluminal pressure of the left colon mean obstruction there is more likely to present with distention and closed-loop physiology at the ileocecal valve, and a cecal diameter of 13 cm raises concern for impending ischemia and perforation. Paralytic ileus is failure of coordinated propulsion without a mechanical block, so gas reaches the colon and rectum, and hypokalemia is a common electrolyte cause. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Cecal volvulus occurs when a mobile cecum twists on its mesentery because the cecal mesentery failed to fuse with the posterior abdominal wall during development. Sigmoid volvulus arises when an elongated, redundant sigmoid colon with a narrow mesenteric base twists, forming a closed loop with a coffee bean-shaped appearance, and it is common in older, institutionalized, or immobile patients such as those with Parkinson disease. Ogilvie syndrome is massive colonic dilation without an obstructing lesion, attributed to malfunction of autonomic bowel control, with nursing home residence, anticholinergic medication, severe electrolyte disturbance, narcotic exposure, and spine or retroperitoneal trauma as risk factors. [ROSH]

### Intestinal Ischemia and Aortoenteric Fistula

- Acute mesenteric ischemia is most commonly caused by an occlusive embolus to the superior mesenteric artery, the vessel most susceptible because of its narrow origin and steep takeoff angle; the superior mesenteric artery supplies the entire small bowel and right colon, so occlusion threatens a large segment with a marginal collateral supply at the splenic flexure. Early ischemia causes intense visceral pain from muscular and neural ischemia while the peritoneum remains uninvolved, so the abdomen is soft without rebound tenderness, and by the time peritoneal signs appear transmural infarction has usually occurred. Bowel wall necrosis can occur as little as 6 hours after complete arterial occlusion. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Ischemic colitis is the most common cause of bowel ischemia and results from hypoperfusion rather than major vessel occlusion, following septic or hemorrhagic shock, hypotension such as myocardial infarction, embolic disease from atrial fibrillation or valvular disease, vasoconstriction, or complication of aortic aneurysm repair. Aortoenteric fistula is a communication between the aorta and the bowel that most commonly involves the third or fourth portion of the duodenum, where an aortic aneurysm or graft abuts the duodenal wall and erodes into it, and a brief herald bleed may slow or stop before massive hemorrhage and hemodynamic collapse. [ROSH]

### Necrotizing Enterocolitis and Developmental Gut Anomalies

- Necrotizing enterocolitis is bowel necrosis of unknown etiology that disproportionately affects premature infants, with immature intestinal immunity, an immature mucosal barrier, and enteral formula feeding implicated, and the ileum and proximal colon most often involved. Pneumatosis intestinalis, or intramural air in the bowel wall, is pathognomonic, while free air under the diaphragm and portal venous gas are late findings indicating perforation and transmural necrosis. [ROSH]
- Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis is idiopathic hypertrophy and elongation of the pylorus producing gastric outlet obstruction. Because only gastric contents are obstructed, vomiting is nonbilious and projectile and patients remain hungry afterward; prolonged vomiting loses hydrogen and chloride in excess of bicarbonate, producing hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis, and the kidney compensates by retaining bicarbonate and excreting potassium, worsening hypokalemia. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Intussusception occurs when a proximal segment of bowel telescopes into a distal segment, causing venous congestion, edema, and mucosal bleeding that produces the classic currant jelly stool; in children under 2 years a preceding viral illness causes lymphoid hyperplasia of Peyer patches in the terminal ileum, which becomes the lead point, and the ileocolic type predominates. Meckel diverticulum is a true diverticulum containing all three intestinal wall layers formed by incomplete obliteration of the vitelline duct, and its ectopic gastric mucosa secretes acid that ulcerates adjacent small bowel mucosa, producing painless rectal bleeding. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Malrotation arises from incomplete rotation of the intestine around the superior mesenteric artery, leaving an abnormally narrow mesenteric attachment so the midgut twists on a narrow pedicle, strangling the superior mesenteric artery and producing midgut ischemia; ischemic necrosis can occur within 1 hour, and upper GI series classically shows a corkscrew of contrast passing to the right of the vertebral column. Hirschsprung disease results from failure of neural crest cell migration, leaving an aganglionic segment of distal colon that cannot relax and generates a functional obstruction, with a hypertrophied proximal segment forming the transition zone; enterocolitis can progress to toxic megacolon. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Tracheoesophageal fistula is an abnormal connection between trachea and esophagus, and esophageal atresia prevents secretions and food from reaching the stomach, so newborns have cough, choking, gagging, frothing, and respiratory distress with feeding. Congenital heart lesions are the most common additional anomaly, and echocardiography is critical before repair because a right-sided aortic arch alters the surgical approach; a neonate with a ductal-dependent lesion becomes cyanotic and hypotensive as the ductus closes on day 3 to 5 and requires emergent prostaglandins. [PEER-PH]
- Biliary atresia is a progressive inflammatory obliteration of both intrahepatic and extrahepatic bile ducts producing obstructive jaundice with a predominance of direct, conjugated bilirubin. Neonatal unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia from insufficient breastfeeding reflects increased enterohepatic circulation, because inadequate caloric intake and dehydration reduce stool bulk and gut transit so bilirubin is deconjugated and reabsorbed rather than excreted. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Appendicitis, Diverticulitis, and Colonic Inflammation

- Appendicitis begins with obstruction of the appendiceal lumen, most often by a fecalith, followed by inflammation and infection. Visceral afferent fibers accompanying the sympathetic supply to the midgut are responsible for early poorly localized periumbilical pain, which localizes to the right lower quadrant when the parietal peritoneum becomes inflamed, so pain precedes vomiting; a retrocecal appendix irritates the psoas muscle and produces a positive psoas sign. [ROSH]
- Diverticulitis results from microperforation of a colonic diverticulum, releasing bacteria and inflammatory mediators into the pericolonic fat. Diverticula form where the vasa recta penetrate the muscular wall, and the left colon is most often affected because intraluminal pressure is highest there; abscess is the most common complication. Diverticular bleeding occurs because the vasa recta are injured at the diverticular neck, which is why the bleeding is typically painless and bright red. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Toxic megacolon is a life-threatening complication of colitis in which transmural inflammation causes loss of colonic muscular tone and massive dilation. Crohn disease is transmural inflammation that produces strictures, abscesses, and fistulae, and a fistula between loops of bowel and adjacent organs produces pneumaturia or fecaluria. Norovirus spreads readily among close contacts with an incubation period of 24 to 48 hours, and Clostridioides difficile disease develops when antibiotic exposure disturbs the normal colonic flora and permits toxin-producing organisms to colonize. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Perineal Soft Tissue Infection and Fistula Formation

- The dentate line divides the anal canal: internal hemorrhoids arise above it from the superior hemorrhoidal plexus and have visceral innervation, so they are painless, whereas external hemorrhoids arise below it from the inferior hemorrhoidal veins and are somatically innervated, so they are exquisitely tender. Perirectal abscess arises from infection of the anal glands at the dentate line, which spreads along the intersphincteric plane into the perianal, ischiorectal, or supralevator spaces. Pilonidal abscess is an infection of the skin overlying the coccyx at the superior natal cleft from an ingrown hair forming a sinus tract, does not communicate with the anorectal canal, and is usually caused by Staphylococcus aureus. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Fournier gangrene is a necrotizing soft tissue infection of the genital and perineal tissues that spreads rapidly along poorly perfused fascial planes, which is why tenderness and systemic toxicity can far exceed the visible skin findings and why swelling, fever, and pain may look deceptively benign early; it is polymicrobial in about two-thirds of cases, and clindamycin is included to suppress exotoxin production. [PEER-CS]

### Fluid, Electrolyte, and Nutritional Consequences

- Prolonged vomiting loses hydrogen and chloride in excess of bicarbonate from the stomach, producing a hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis, and the kidney compensates by retaining bicarbonate and excreting potassium, which worsens hypokalemia; this is the expected pattern in gastric outlet obstruction from hypertrophic pyloric stenosis. Sequestration of fluid in an obstructed gut lumen produces dehydration and hypokalemia, which further impairs intestinal motility; ileostomy patients are prone to hyponatremia from high-output losses. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- After bariatric surgery, rapid emptying of hyperosmolar nutrients into the small bowel draws fluid into the lumen and produces dumping symptoms of nausea, vomiting, flushing, palpitations, and colicky pain after eating, managed with smaller, more frequent meals. Persistent vomiting after Roux-en-Y gastric bypass can cause thiamine deficiency and Wernicke encephalopathy with ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, and confusion. After laparoscopic adjustable gastric banding, failure to improve with band deflation favors band slippage over simple overinflation, and wound redness two weeks after sleeve gastrectomy most often yields staphylococcal species. [PEER-PH]
- Peritoneal dialysis peritonitis presents with abdominal pain, cloudy effluent, fever, and nausea, and is diagnosed by a peritoneal fluid white blood cell count over 100 cells/mm3 with more than 50% neutrophils. Abdominal compartment syndrome develops when sustained intra-abdominal pressure above 12 mm Hg, against a normal baseline of 5 to 7 mm Hg, impairs venous return, reduces cardiac output, splints the diaphragm, and compresses the renal veins, producing multisystem organ dysfunction. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Referred Pain and Neurogenic Mechanisms

- Visceral pain from the foregut, midgut, and hindgut is carried by autonomic afferents that enter the spinal cord at different levels, so pain is initially poorly localized and referred to the corresponding dermatomes: epigastric for foregut structures, periumbilical for midgut, and suprapubic for hindgut. Localization occurs when inflammation reaches the parietal peritoneum, which is somatically innervated. Irritation of the diaphragm by intraperitoneal blood, pus, or air refers pain to the shoulder tip through the phrenic nerve (C3-C5), which is why a ruptured ectopic pregnancy can present with shoulder pain. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Testicular torsion results from inadequate fixation of the testis within the tunica vaginalis, permitting rotation of the spermatic cord and occlusion of the testicular vessels; ischemia is time-dependent, with salvage nearly 100% if surgery occurs within 6 hours but about 50% at 19 to 24 hours. Ovarian torsion occurs when the ovary and its vascular pedicle rotate, obstructing venous and lymphatic drainage with eventual arterial compromise, and about 70% occur on the right because the longer utero-ovarian ligament allows greater mobility. [PEER-CS]
- Ectopic pregnancy occurs when the blastocyst implants outside the uterine cavity, most often in the fallopian tube; as it enlarges it erodes into the tubal wall and adjacent vessels, producing intraperitoneal bleeding, and blood reaching the upper abdomen through the paracolic gutter irritates the diaphragm and causes right upper quadrant or shoulder pain. Rupture can occur during methotrexate treatment in 7% to 14% of cases, and heterotopic pregnancy is an absolute contraindication to methotrexate. [PEER-CS]
- Untreated pelvic inflammatory disease produces tubal scarring with future ectopic pregnancy, infertility, and chronic pain. Black widow spider venom releases acetylcholine and norepinephrine, producing sustained muscle cramping and severe abdominal pain that closely mimics an acute surgical abdomen. Lumbosacral radiculopathy from disc herniation or spondylosis can present as abdominal or flank pain, and acute coronary syndrome can refer pain to the epigastrium, particularly in elderly women. [PEER-CS]
