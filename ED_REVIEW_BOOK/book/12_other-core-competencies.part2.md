## Pathophysiology

This chapter is not mechanism-based. Its content is systems, communication, quality, law, ethics, professionalism, disposition, and biostatistics, and the source material offers no pathophysiology for most of it. The mechanism content that does exist is limited to a small number of specific entities, set out below. Where no mechanism is stated in the source material, none is given here.

### Silicone Embolism Syndrome

- Illicit silicone injection, typically at unlicensed "pumping parties" for hip and buttock augmentation, produces respiratory failure, a petechial rash, and pulmonary oedema.
- Presentation: shortness of breath, fever, hypoxia, petechial haemorrhage, and pulmonary oedema on chest radiograph in a transgender woman with a history of injected silicone. [PEER-PH]
- Management is supportive; steroids are considered, and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation is reserved for severe acute respiratory distress syndrome. [PEER-PH]

### Chest Binding in Transgender Men

- Mechanical compression of the chest wall produces the complications seen with binding.
- Back pain is the most common complication (54%), followed by skin problems, chest pain, shortness of breath, and overheating. Rare complications are chest wall scarring (8%) and rib fractures (3%). [PEER-PH]

### Shift Work Sleep Disorder

- Circadian rhythm disruption from night and rotating shift work produces delayed sleep latency, fragmented sleep, fatigue, and impaired cognition in an otherwise healthy clinician.
- Associated risks include metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, pregnancy complications, and cancer. [PEER-PH]

### Burnout and the Second Victim

- Burnout is described by three components rather than by a mechanism: depersonalisation, emotional exhaustion, and a reduced sense of accomplishment. Physicians carry a higher rate than other specialties. [PEER-PH]
- Poor work-life balance is the driver identified for burnout; family-friendly policies improve balance. [PEER-PH]
- Second victim syndrome is a trauma response in the clinician following a medical error, an adverse event, or a malpractice suit. The patient is the first victim and the clinician the second. It manifests as burnout, depression, substance use, guilt, sleep difficulty, and anxiety. [PEER-PH]

### Medication-Related Harm

- Combining an opioid with a sedative-hypnotic such as baclofen raises the risk of respiratory depression and respiratory arrest; this is the mechanism behind the harm from adding morphine to an existing regimen of naproxen, lidocaine, and baclofen. [PEER-PH]
- In older adults, opioid analgesia can precipitate delirium, which in turn leads to falls. A 70-year-old woman given morphine 8 mg for gout became delirious, fell, and sustained a femoral neck fracture. [PEER-PH]

### Sources of Diagnostic Error

- Anchoring bias overemphasises specific information, history, and examination findings in medical decision-making. It simplifies and speeds care but increases the rate of missed diagnoses. [ROSH]
- Implicit bias is subconscious and shaped by genetics, upbringing, and experience; explicit bias and overt racism are conscious. [PEER-PH]
- Gender bias in acute coronary syndrome is a mechanism of misdiagnosis: women are less often diagnosed with myocardial infarction, less often catheterised, and given fewer cardiac medications despite identical features. [PEER-PH]
