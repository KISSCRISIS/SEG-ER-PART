## High-Yield Exam Review

- **Metabolic acidosis**: pH < 7.35 with low bicarbonate and compensatory low pCO2 (pH 7.33, pCO2 30 mm Hg, HCO3 15 mEq/L). **Metabolic alkalosis**: pH > 7.45 with high bicarbonate and high pCO2 — prolonged vomiting is the prototype. [PEER-PHYS]
- In acute metabolic alkalosis, pCO2 rises about 7 mm Hg per 10 mEq rise in bicarbonate. [PEER-PHYS]
- **Salicylate overdose** creates two primary processes at once: direct stimulation of the medullary respiratory centre (respiratory alkalosis) and inhibition of oxidative phosphorylation (metabolic acidosis). Competing processes can leave the pH near normal despite severe acidosis. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Anion gap = sodium − (chloride + bicarbonate); normal is about 12 mEq/L. High-gap causes follow the MUDPILES mnemonic. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Winters formula: expected pCO2 = (1.5 × bicarbonate) + 8 ± 2. Delta ratio = (measured anion gap − 12) / (24 − measured bicarbonate); a ratio above 2.0 indicates a mixed high-gap metabolic acidosis with metabolic alkalosis. [ROSH]
- Normal anion gap metabolic acidosis: diarrhoea and acetazolamide, which causes renal loss of bicarbonate, potassium and free water. [ROSH]
- **Adrenal crisis**: hydrocortisone is the drug of choice. It has both glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid activity and is available intravenously. [PEER-PHYS]
- **Primary adrenal insufficiency**: hyponatremia in about 90%, hyperkalemia in about 60%, plus hypoglycaemia and skin hyperpigmentation; adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) is elevated because the rest of the axis is intact. Hyperkalemia is what separates primary from secondary adrenal insufficiency. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- The most common cause of adrenal insufficiency of any type is chronic corticosteroid therapy. [ROSH]
- **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia**: more than 90% is 21-hydroxylase deficiency; it presents as a salt-wasting crisis between the second and fifth weeks of life with hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis and dehydration. Treat with hydrocortisone. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Check the potassium before starting an insulin drip in diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA); delay insulin when the potassium is below 3.3 mEq/L. [PEER-PHYS]
- Sodium bicarbonate is not routine in DKA; it shifts potassium into cells and can cause hypokalemia. [PEER-PHYS]
- pH is the value that most reliably separates hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS) from DKA, with a classic cutoff of 7.30 — HHS above, DKA below. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- **HHS**: glucose above 600 mg/dL (often above 1000 mg/dL), pH above 7.3, normal bicarbonate and anion gap, trace or absent ketones, and serum sodium more likely to be elevated; average fluid deficit is 8–12 L. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Neurologic features of HHS — obtundation, hemiparesis, seizures — follow the degree of hyperosmolarity and typically appear above a plasma osmolality of 330 mmol/kg. [PEER-PHYS]
- Infection, particularly pneumonia and urinary tract infection, is the most common precipitant of HHS. The first treatment step is isotonic crystalloid. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Sulfonylureas such as glipizide and glyburide cause prolonged hypoglycaemia and warrant 24 hours of observation after overdose. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Thyroid storm is a clinical diagnosis. Treatment order is beta-blocker (propranolol), then thionamide, then iodine given at least 1 hour later; hydrocortisone is added. Salicylates are contraindicated. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- In the Burch and Wartofsky score, central nervous system dysfunction carries the greatest diagnostic weight; coma scores highest at 30 points. [ROSH]
- **Myxedema coma**: hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension, altered mentation and hyponatremia; treat with intravenous levothyroxine, hydrocortisone and a normal saline bolus. Mortality approaches 30% with treatment, near 100% without it. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- **Pheochromocytoma**: alpha blockade always precedes beta blockade. Phentolamine is first-line for hypertensive emergency; a beta-blocker given first causes unopposed alpha stimulation and worsens hypertension. Glucocorticoids are contraindicated. [PEER-PHYS]
- Headache is the most common symptom of pheochromocytoma, present in 90% of symptomatic patients; diagnosis is by plasma or urinary fractionated metanephrines. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- **Hypercalcemia**: isotonic saline is the first step; a level above 14 mg/dL is life-threatening. Restoring intravascular volume alone usually lowers calcium by 1.6–2.4 mg/dL. [ROSH]
- **Hyperkalemia**: intravenous calcium is given first to stabilise the myocardium; it does not change the serum potassium level. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Hypokalemia refractory to potassium replacement indicates hypomagnesemia; magnesium is required for normal sodium-potassium pump function. [ROSH]
- Post-thyroidectomy hypocalcaemia: Chvostek sign, Trousseau sign and QT prolongation; hypoparathyroidism produces hypocalcaemia with hyperphosphataemia. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- **Wernicke encephalopathy** is a clinical diagnosis requiring two of: dietary deficiency, oculomotor abnormality, cerebellar dysfunction, altered mental status or memory impairment. Treat with intravenous thiamine; untreated mortality is 10–20%. [ROSH]
- In children with DKA, cerebral oedema is the most serious complication and follows overaggressive treatment. [ROSH]

## Core Concepts

### Acid-base disturbances

- Read every blood gas in order: pH (acidemia below 7.35, alkalemia above 7.45), then pCO2 (respiratory, normal 35–45 mm Hg), then bicarbonate (metabolic, normal 21–27 mEq/L). [ROSH]
- A pH of 7.25 with pCO2 65 mm Hg and bicarbonate 14 mEq/L is a mixed metabolic and respiratory acidosis. A pH of 7.31 with pCO2 10 mm Hg and bicarbonate 10 mEq/L is a high anion gap metabolic acidosis with an added primary respiratory alkalosis: when the measured pCO2 falls below the Winters-predicted range, a second primary disorder is present. [ROSH]
- Respiratory alkalosis from hyperventilation — panic attack, early acute respiratory distress syndrome — gives a high pH, low pCO2 and normal bicarbonate (pH 7.50, pCO2 25 mm Hg, bicarbonate 23 mEq/L). Hyperventilation also lowers cerebral blood flow and ionised calcium, producing distal paresthesias and muscle spasm. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Alveolar hypoventilation — decreased respiratory rate, decreased tidal volume, increased dead space — produces acute respiratory acidosis with hypercapnia above 45 mm Hg; opioid toxicity acts through decreased respiratory drive. [ROSH]
- In chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, chronic compensation gives a near-normal pH with a high pCO2 and high bicarbonate; acute exacerbation shows a falling pH with a further rise in pCO2 on the same bicarbonate background. [ROSH]
- Metabolic alkalosis arises from hydrogen ion loss or bicarbonate gain. Chloride loss reduces extracellular volume, increases mineralocorticoid activity and drives further potassium and hydrogen excretion, amplifying the alkalosis. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Chloride-responsive alkalosis — vomiting, nasogastric losses, loop and thiazide diuretics — has a low urine chloride and responds to normal saline; chloride-resistant causes have a urine chloride above 25 mEq/L. Vomiting from obstruction gives alkalosis with hypokalemia, hypomagnesaemia and hypocalcaemia. [ROSH]
- Xenobiotics that inhibit cellular respiration — carbon monoxide, cyanide, hydrogen sulfide, sodium azide — produce severe lactic acidosis. [PEER-PHYS]

### Adrenal insufficiency

- Primary adrenal insufficiency is failure of the gland itself: autoimmune destruction most commonly, then tuberculosis or fungal infection, haemorrhagic infarction as in Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome, metastatic disease, and congenital adrenal hyperplasia. Secondary arises from impaired pituitary ACTH secretion; tertiary from reduced hypothalamic corticotropin-releasing hormone. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Cortisol deficiency removes vasomotor tone and gluconeogenic drive, giving hypotension that may be vasopressor-refractory, and hypoglycaemia. Aldosterone deficiency causes renal sodium loss with potassium retention. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Adrenal crisis follows abrupt corticosteroid withdrawal in a steroid-dependent patient, physical stress such as surgery, trauma or severe infection, pituitary damage, or underlying Addison disease. Chronic exogenous steroids suppress endogenous cortisol by negative feedback, so no stress response can be mounted when septic shock supervenes. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Rifampin is a potent inducer of the cytochrome P450 system and increases hepatic metabolism of hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone; it can precipitate adrenal crisis in a patient with known adrenal insufficiency. [ROSH]
- A child with known congenital adrenal hyperplasia who develops a urinary tract infection cannot mount a compensatory stress response and needs intravenous stress-dose hydrocortisone alongside standard sepsis care. [PEER-PHYS]

### Cortisol excess: Cushing syndrome

- Iatrogenic Cushing syndrome from prescribed glucocorticoids — most often prednisone — is likely the most common cause; Cushing disease, from pituitary ACTH hypersecretion usually due to an adenoma, is second. [ROSH]
- Features: abdominal and back fat deposition, rounded facies, facial plethora, striae, proximal muscle wasting, easy bruising, hypertension, hyperglycaemia, osteoporosis and psychiatric disturbance. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- In children, excess cortisol suppresses growth hormone, testosterone and oestrogen, causing growth arrest and delayed puberty — unlike simple dietary obesity, in which children are tall and grow rapidly. [ROSH]
- Screening is by 24-hour urinary cortisol or a low-dose dexamethasone suppression test: dexamethasone at night, cortisol measured in the morning, with adequate suppression excluding the diagnosis. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Adrenal incidentalomas occur in up to 5% of the general population and 10% of older patients; nearly all are benign and nonfunctioning — increased mass vascularity raises concern for malignancy. [ROSH]

### Diabetes emergencies: DKA and HHS

- DKA results from insulin deficiency with counter-regulatory hormone excess. Osmotic diuresis from glucosuria causes profound dehydration; free fatty acid breakdown generates ketones and an anion gap metabolic acidosis; acidosis depresses the myocardium, causes vasodilation and drives Kussmaul respiration. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Expected laboratory pattern: glucose usually above 250–300 mg/dL, low bicarbonate, glucosuria and ketonuria, elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate. Serum potassium may be high, normal or low even though total body potassium is depleted. The usual trigger is medication noncompliance; infection, pregnancy, myocardial infarction, hyperthyroidism, substance misuse, and insulin pump failure or catheter occlusion are others. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Presentation is non-specific: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fatigue, polyuria and polydipsia, deep rapid breathing, a fruity breath odour and dehydration. In a child, new-onset type 1 diabetes commonly presents first as DKA after a viral illness. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Euglycaemic DKA — glucose above 70 mg/dL and below 250 mg/dL — is strongly associated with sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors such as empagliflozin and canagliflozin. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- HHS is severe hyperglycaemia with hyperosmolarity and extreme dehydration, with little or no ketone formation. Neurological presentation is more common and mortality substantially higher than in DKA. Thiazides precipitate HHS in older adults with type 2 diabetes by impairing insulin secretion, increasing insulin resistance and promoting diuresis. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Diabetes can be diagnosed in the ED in a symptomatic patient with a random plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or more; type 2 diabetes reflects insulin resistance with progressive loss of insulin secretion. [ROSH]

### Hypoglycaemia

- Symptomatic hypoglycaemia in adults occurs at glucose 40–50 mg/dL, with altered mental status, diaphoresis, tremor and tachycardia. Sulfonylureas such as glipizide and glyburide are insulin secretagogues; long-acting agents produce hypoglycaemia persisting beyond 24 hours in overdose, with greater risk in older patients and in renal or hepatic impairment. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- In a conscious patient, sustained correction requires a meal containing complex carbohydrate, fat and protein — a turkey and cheese sandwich contains 200–300 calories. Simple sugars correct the glucose only transiently and risk a repeat episode. [PEER-PHYS]
- Hypoglycaemia is a well-recognised mimic of acute stroke; a small proportion of patients present with focal deficits including hemiplegia, so point-of-care glucose is obtained before head CT. [PEER-PHYS]
- Insulinoma is a rare pancreatic tumour causing recurrent hypoglycaemia in patients without diabetes; diazoxide suppresses insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells. [PEER-PHYS]
