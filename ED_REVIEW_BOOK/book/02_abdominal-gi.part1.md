## High-Yield Exam Review

- Indirect inguinal hernia is the most common hernia; two-thirds of inguinal hernias are indirect and indirect hernias are twice as common as direct. [PEER-PH]
- Femoral hernia is only 4% of groin hernias but up to 40% present incarcerated or strangulated, the highest strangulation risk of the common hernias. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Spigelian hernia is a slit-like defect at the semilunar line lateral to the rectus abdominis; about 20% present incarcerated and up to 15% strangulated. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Indirect hernia arises from incomplete closure of the processus vaginalis and protrudes lateral to the epigastric vessels. Preterm infants are at higher risk — 3–5% at term versus up to 30%. [PEER-PH]
- Incarcerated hernia without strangulation: analgesia, supine Trendelenburg with ice, then gentle constant pressure at the base of the defect. Exquisite pain, systemic illness, or purple skin change means strangulation — NPO and immediate surgical consultation. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Reducible umbilical hernia in an infant closes spontaneously within 2 years, and repair is deferred to age 4–5 years. [PEER-PH]
- Mallory-Weiss tear: partial-thickness mucosal tear at the gastroesophageal junction after forceful vomiting; 8–15% of upper GI bleeding and alcohol-associated in up to 80%. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Boerhaave syndrome is full-thickness esophageal rupture diagnosed by water-soluble contrast esophagram; it is usually distal esophageal with mortality up to 50%. Iatrogenic injury, most often upper endoscopy, is the most common cause of perforation overall. [PEER-PH]
- Button battery in the esophagus requires emergent endoscopic removal, because injury occurs within 6 hours; the double-ring sign distinguishes it from a coin. [PEER-PH]
- Resolved food impaction in a patient handling secretions: discharge with endoscopy referral within 12–24 hours; glucagon may relieve the bolus. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Peptic ulcer disease is the most common cause of upper GI bleeding; H. pylori causes 70–80% of duodenal and 60–70% of gastric ulcers, with NSAIDs second. [ROSH]
- Cirrhotic patients with GI bleeding of any cause receive ceftriaxone prophylaxis, which reduces mortality, rebleeding, and infection. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Variceal bleeding: octreotide 50–100 µg bolus then 50 µg/hr. Sengstaken-Blakemore esophageal balloon pressure is reduced by 5 mm Hg every 3 hours to 25 mm Hg, with the gastric balloon at 30–40 mm Hg. [PEER-PH]
- Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt raises survival in severe variceal bleeding from 10% to 60% but worsens hyperammonemia. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Charcot triad appears in only about half of cholangitis cases and Reynolds pentad in under 10%; definitive management is ERCP decompression after broad-spectrum antibiotics. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Ultrasound is the initial study for suspected cholecystitis (sensitivity 94%, specificity 78%): wall thickening over 3 mm, pericholecystic fluid, and a sonographic Murphy sign. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- A common bile duct under 6 mm is normal below age 60. Intermediate-risk choledocholithiasis — age over 55, abnormal liver function tests, dilated duct — calls for MRCP or endoscopic ultrasound. [PEER-PH]
- Gallstones are the most common cause of acute pancreatitis worldwide. Resuscitate with 30 mL/kg crystalloid, preferring lactated Ringer unless hypercalcemia is present. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Postoperative adhesions cause over 60% of small bowel obstruction; CT with intravenous contrast is the modality of choice and most cases resolve conservatively over 48–72 hours. Ischemic bowel mandates emergent surgery. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Colorectal cancer is the most common cause of large bowel obstruction, and a cecal width of 13 cm suggests impending ischemia. Sigmoid volvulus is decompressed with flexible sigmoidoscopy and a rectal tube. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Acute mesenteric ischemia: CT angiography is the test of choice, the most common cause is superior mesenteric artery embolism, and necrosis can occur within 6 hours. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Aortoenteric fistula most commonly involves the duodenum and is the most common life-threatening cause of GI bleeding after abdominal aortic aneurysm repair. [ROSH]
- An ascitic polymorphonuclear (PMN) count over 250 cells/mm3 defines spontaneous bacterial peritonitis. Lactulose is first-line for hepatic encephalopathy; lorazepam is safer than midazolam. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Pyloric stenosis: nonbilious projectile vomiting at 2–6 weeks with an olive mass. Intussusception: currant jelly stool reduced by air enema. Necrotizing enterocolitis: pneumatosis intestinalis in a premature infant. Hirschsprung disease: failure to pass meconium after 48 hours. [ROSH]

## Core Concepts

### Hernias of the Abdominal Wall

- Inguinal hernias are 75% of all abdominal wall hernias and are the most common hernia in women as well as men; because direct and indirect inguinal hernias are both repaired, distinguishing them clinically is not essential. Femoral hernia passes through the femoral ring, inferior to the inguinal ligament, medial to the femoral vein, and lateral to the lacunar ligament, and is more common in women. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Ventral hernias are usually acquired and follow previous surgery; the natural weak points are the umbilicus, semilunar line, ostomy sites, inguinal regions, and esophageal hiatus. Spigelian hernia lies between fascial layers, so the defect is small and thin and may not be palpable, especially in obesity. A chronically reducible umbilical hernia in a patient with obesity and diabetes is managed with weight reduction, glycemic control, and outpatient surgical referral, and rectus sheath hematoma occurs below the arcuate line. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS][ROSH]

### Esophageal Disorders and Caustic Injury

- Reflux disease results from reduced lower esophageal sphincter tone, delayed gastric emptying, or dysmotility; nicotine, caffeine, calcium channel blockers, ethanol, and anticholinergics lower sphincter tone, and symptoms are worse lying flat. Untreated erosive esophagitis heals with fibrosis into a peptic stricture at the gastroesophageal junction, and chronic reflux produces cellular metaplasia; endoscopy is indicated with age over 60, weight loss, anemia, GI bleeding, dysphagia, or GI cancer in a first-degree relative. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Pill esophagitis follows direct contact when a tablet lodges in the collapsed esophagus, most often doxycycline, and ingestion before sleep raises risk. Achalasia reflects myenteric aganglionosis with dysphagia to solids and liquids, and distal esophageal spasm produces a corkscrew esophagus with manometry as the diagnostic test. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Caustic alkalis cause liquefaction necrosis with a high risk of stricture, while caustic acids cause coagulation necrosis with a protective eschar, so injury is less deep but mortality is higher from gastric damage. Household bleach at pH 11 is only minimally corrosive, so an asymptomatic small accidental ingestion may be discharged, whereas stridor, hoarseness, or drooling after a strong caustic requires emergent endoscopy. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding and Portal Hypertension

- Peptic ulcer disease is the most common cause of upper GI bleeding in adults and in patients under 50, and bleeding complicates 15% to 20% of cases; erosion of the gastroduodenal artery causes massive hemorrhage requiring endoscopic ligation. Sequencing is airway, antibiotics, blood, consultation, drugs and devices, then esophagogastroduodenoscopy, and any patient with bloody or coffee-ground emesis is an impending airway emergency. [ROSH]
- Varices form in the distal esophagus and gastric fundus, and bleeding risk rises with larger variceal diameter, red wale marks, worse liver function, and prior bleeding; the distal esophagus is the most common site because varices there lie superficially. Variceal bleeding is only 10% to 20% of upper GI bleeds but a leading cause of death in cirrhosis. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Gallbladder, Biliary Tree, and Pancreas

- Biliary risk factors are advanced age, female sex, parity, obesity, rapid weight loss, family history, and oral contraceptive use, and women are affected two to three times as often as men. Biliary colic is constant right upper quadrant or epigastric pain radiating to the right shoulder, peaks around midnight, and does not exceed 6 hours; gallstones are asymptomatic in most patients, but 20% to 40% develop complications. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Acute cholecystitis follows obstruction of the gallbladder neck or cystic duct with secondary infection, occurring in 10% of symptomatic gallstone patients within 10 years, and is the most common surgical emergency after age 65, with urgent cholecystectomy as definitive treatment. Emphysematous cholecystitis is a gas-producing infection in older men with diabetes. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Cholangitis is bacterial translocation into an obstructed, static biliary tree from stones, benign strictures, malignancy, or instrumentation, and mild to moderate disease responds to antibiotics alone in 70% to 80%; retained stones occur in up to 10% after cholecystectomy, and cholangiocarcinoma arises after repeated inflammation from inflammatory bowel disease, recurrent acute cholecystitis, or ascending cholangitis. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Acute pancreatitis follows pancreatic duct obstruction or direct acinar toxicity with premature enzyme activation; most cases are interstitial edematous and resolve within a week, while 5% to 10% develop necrotizing pancreatitis. Hypertriglyceridemia-induced disease correlates with triglycerides above 500 mg/dL, is more severe above 1,000 mg/dL, and is treated with analgesia, antiemetics, intravenous fluids, insulin, and lipid-lowering agents. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Liver and Its Complications

- Cirrhosis is fibrosis with nodular regeneration causing coagulopathy, jaundice, encephalopathy, portal hypertension, varices, and ascites; causes include viral and autoimmune hepatitis, alcohol-related hepatitis, steatotic liver disease, vascular and biliary tract disease, and hemochromatosis. Alcohol-related liver disease follows more than 100 g of alcohol daily for years, with transaminases under 300 IU/L and an AST:ALT ratio greater than 2. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Hepatic encephalopathy is a neuropsychiatric syndrome of liver dysfunction and portosystemic shunting, with constipation, GI bleeding, infection, and sedating medication as precipitants. Benzodiazepines such as diazepam, clonazepam, and midazolam undergo phase I metabolism, which is impaired in cirrhosis, so lorazepam is safer. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis is present in 33% of patients with chronic liver disease who have even one element of the triad of fever, abdominal pain, and encephalopathy, with E. coli, Enterococcus, and Streptococcus as usual organisms, cefotaxime for treatment, and ciprofloxacin prophylaxis after an episode. Hepatorenal syndrome is functional renal failure from reactive renal vasoconstriction with histologically normal kidneys, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis is a risk factor in up to 25% to 30%, and albumin improves mortality. [PEER-CS][PEER-PH]
- Acetaminophen is the most common cause of drug-induced liver injury in the United States, while isoniazid, amiodarone, amoxicillin-clavulanate, and phenytoin cause idiosyncratic injury. Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease is the most common liver disorder in the United States with insulin resistance as the key mechanism, and chronic hepatitis C accounts for nearly one third of hepatocellular carcinoma cases. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Intestinal Obstruction, Volvulus, and Ischemia

- Small bowel obstruction presents with colicky pain, nausea, vomiting, distention, and inability to pass flatus; causes are adhesions over 60%, neoplasms about 20%, hernias about 10%, and gallstone ileus 1% to 4%. CT with intravenous contrast is the modality of choice and most cases resolve over 48–72 hours with conservative management, while air in the colon and rectum on plain radiograph favors ileus. [PEER-CS][ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Large bowel obstruction is most often caused by colorectal cancer, and a cecal width of 13 cm suggests impending ischemia; sigmoid volvulus is common in older, immobile patients and appears as a coffee bean-shaped loop, while cecal volvulus follows failure of the cecal mesentery to fuse posteriorly. Ogilvie syndrome is massive colonic dilation without obstruction from malfunction of autonomic bowel control, and neostigmine may relieve it. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Acute mesenteric ischemia presents with pain out of proportion to a soft abdomen without rebound, and a normal examination does not exclude it; ischemic colitis is the most common bowel ischemia and causes severe left-sided pain with bloody stools after shock, embolic disease, or aortic aneurysm repair. An aortic aneurysm can present as abdominal, back, flank, or groin pain, and a shock index (heart rate divided by systolic blood pressure) over 1 is associated with higher mortality. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

### Enteric and Colonic Disease

- Crohn disease is transmural inflammation causing strictures, abscesses, and fistulae, with stricture and obstruction the most common complication; anemia is the most common laboratory abnormality and erythema nodosum is a characteristic extraintestinal manifestation. Toxic megacolon presents with acute severe pain, high fever, tachycardia, hypotension, and a distended tympanitic abdomen, with plain radiography showing transverse colon dilation over 6 cm. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Norovirus is the most common cause of acute diarrheal illness at 26% of emergency department diarrhea cases; Clostridioides difficile is suggested by at least three unformed stools in 24 hours with healthcare exposure, age 65 years or older, and antibiotic use within the preceding 10 weeks, with clindamycin the most likely culprit and oral vancomycin the treatment. Diverticulitis is microperforation of a colonic diverticulum in 10% to 25% of patients with diverticulosis, with CT preferred and amoxicillin-clavulanate for uncomplicated disease. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

### Anorectal, Perianal, and Soft Tissue Infection

- The dentate line separates internal hemorrhoids, above it with visceral innervation, from external hemorrhoids, below it with somatic innervation; thrombosed external hemorrhoid is a painful purplish perianal mass, and excision within 72 hours of pain onset provides immediate relief and lowers recurrence to 5% to 19%. Anal fissure is a superficial tear of the anoderm at the posterior midline and then the anterior midline, and a lateral fissure raises concern for Crohn disease, HIV, leukemia, tuberculosis, or syphilis. [ROSH][PEER-CS][PEER-PH]
- Perirectal abscess causes severe constant rectal pain, fever, and a fluctuant tender mass, and a fluctuant mass on rectal examination mandates CT; a simple perianal abscess is drained with a radial incision as close to the anal verge as possible. Pilonidal abscess is infection of the skin overlying the coccyx from an ingrown hair and is usually caused by Staphylococcus aureus. Fournier gangrene is a rapidly spreading polymicrobial necrotizing infection in which swelling, fever, and pain may look deceptively benign early; early aggressive debridement is the only definitive treatment and blood and imaging studies must not delay surgical consultation. [ROSH][PEER-CS][PEER-PH]

### Pediatric Gastrointestinal Emergencies

- Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis is idiopathic hypertrophy and elongation of the pylorus causing gastric outlet obstruction; it presents at 2 to 6 weeks with nonbilious projectile vomiting, an olive-shaped mass in the right upper quadrant, and hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis. Intussusception is the most common cause of intestinal obstruction in children under 2 years, typically after a viral illness, with colicky pain, a sausage-shaped mass, and currant jelly stool; ultrasound is diagnostic and air enema is both diagnostic and therapeutic, while adult intussusception is 5% of cases and usually has a pathologic lead point such as malignancy requiring resection. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Necrotizing enterocolitis is bowel necrosis of unknown etiology in premature infants with mortality of 15% to 30%, presenting within the first 3 weeks of life with poor feeding, lethargy, bilious vomiting, abdominal distension, and bloody stool; pneumatosis intestinalis is pathognomonic, and treatment is bowel rest with nasogastric decompression and intravenous antibiotics. Hirschsprung disease results from failure of neural crest cell migration, leaving an aganglionic distal colon; 75% involve the rectosigmoid and the most common presentation is failure to pass meconium within 48 hours, with rectal biopsy confirming the diagnosis. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Malrotation arises from incomplete rotation around the superior mesenteric artery with abnormal mesenteric attachment and roughly 75% develop volvulus; upper GI series is the gold standard and ischemic necrosis can occur within 1 hour, so management is resuscitation, antibiotics, nasogastric decompression, and emergent laparotomy for a Ladd procedure. Meckel diverticulum is a true diverticulum of all three wall layers from incomplete obliteration of the vitelline duct, present in 1% to 2% of the population, and causes painless rectal bleeding in early childhood from ectopic gastric tissue. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Abdominal Pain Mimics

- Any female of childbearing age with abdominal pain or vaginal bleeding requires evaluation for ectopic pregnancy; a complex inhomogeneous adnexal mass is present in 89% or more of cases, and management is emergent obstetric consultation, type and screen, and O-negative blood. Abdominal pain after methotrexate requires point-of-care ultrasound because 7% to 14% rupture during treatment, and heterotopic pregnancy is an absolute contraindication to methotrexate. [PEER-CS]
- Testicular torsion is a time-sensitive cause of abdominal pain and a genitourinary examination is mandatory in any male with abdominal pain; absence of the cremasteric reflex suggests torsion with sensitivity about 96% and specificity 88%, and salvage is nearly 100% within 6 hours but about 50% at 19 to 24 hours. Ovarian torsion presents with acute severe lower abdominal pain and an ovarian mass, with a cyst between 5 and 10 cm carrying the highest probability and about 70% occurring on the right. [PEER-CS]
- Untreated pelvic inflammatory disease causes tubal scarring with ectopic pregnancy, infertility, and chronic pain, and tubo-ovarian abscess is treated with intravenous ceftriaxone 1 g, doxycycline 100 mg, and metronidazole 500 mg. Black widow envenomation causes abdominal pain and muscle cramping closely mimicking an acute surgical abdomen, and acute coronary syndrome can refer pain to the epigastrium, particularly in elderly women. [PEER-CS]
