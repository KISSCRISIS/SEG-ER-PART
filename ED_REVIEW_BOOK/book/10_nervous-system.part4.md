## Clinical Pearls

### Cerebrovascular Pearls

- A fingerstick blood glucose is the first step in every suspected stroke. Hypoglycemia reproduces focal deficits exactly, is immediately reversible, and no imaging or thrombolysis decision should be made before it is excluded [ROSH][PEER-CS].
- A negative noncontrast head CT does not exclude subarachnoid hemorrhage beyond the first 6 hours. Sensitivity is 98–100% within 6 hours; after that window the next step is CT angiography of the head and neck rather than reassurance [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- A history of intracranial hemorrhage is an absolute contraindication to thrombolysis regardless of how early the patient presents; ischemic stroke within 3 months, gastrointestinal bleeding within 21 days, and multilobar or extensive infarction on CT are the others. An observed seizure at stroke onset with postictal deficits is relative only. Mechanical thrombectomy benefits large-vessel occlusion at 6 to 24 hours, and 6 to 16 hours in selected patients [ROSH][PEER-CS].
- Blood pressure targets differ by stroke type and are a common source of error. Hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage is treated with intravenous labetalol to a systolic pressure of 140 mm Hg to limit hematoma expansion; aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage is kept below 160 mm Hg systolic and below 110 mm Hg mean arterial pressure, with nimodipine added because it improves mortality [ROSH][PEER-CS].

### Headache and Vertigo Pearls

- The most common error with the Ottawa SAH Rule is applying it outside its population. It is validated only for alert patients over 15 years with a nontraumatic, new-severe headache who are not neurologically impaired, so a patient with a new focal deficit or a patient outside those criteria cannot be cleared by it. Specificity is only about 15% [PEER-CS].
- The head impulse test is interpretable only when nystagmus is present. A corrective saccade on rapid head rotation means peripheral vertigo; absence of a corrective saccade means a central cause. The full HINTS examination approaches 100% sensitivity for central vertigo [PEER-CS].
- Vertebral artery dissection should be considered in any patient with constant dizziness, neck pain, and headache — particularly after manipulation and particularly in a younger patient. Dizziness is its most common presenting symptom, and only about half of patients have trauma or an identifiable risk factor [PEER-CS].
- Cluster headache is diagnosed by timing and autonomic features rather than pain severity. Acute treatment is 100% oxygen by nonrebreather at 12–15 L/min for 15 minutes, or subcutaneous sumatriptan 6 mg; verapamil decreases recurrence [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Giant cell arteritis is a cannot-miss headache in a patient over 50 years, especially with jaw claudication and visual change; intravenous methylprednisolone is given when vision is threatened, without waiting for biopsy. Post–dural puncture headache is positional, begins within 48 hours of the procedure, is reduced by a noncutting needle but not by bed rest, and is definitively treated with an epidural blood patch [ROSH][PEER-CS].
- Tension-type headache is the most common primary headache and is defined by what it lacks — no photophobia, no phonophobia, no focal deficit [PEER-PH].

### Seizure Pearls

- Status epilepticus is defined by time, not appearance: a seizure beyond 5 minutes, or recurrent seizures without return to baseline. Seizures become more refractory with every minute and every subsequent antiepileptic dose, and benzodiazepines are frequently underdosed — lorazepam is 0.1 mg/kg to a maximum of 4 mg [PEER-CS][ROSH].
- Alcohol withdrawal seizures occur within 6 to 48 hours of the last drink and are usually a single generalized tonic-clonic event or a short cluster. Status epilepticus is not consistent with alcohol withdrawal alone and mandates a search for hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, intracranial hemorrhage, meningitis, severe metabolic derangement, or co-ingestion [PEER-CS].
- Seizures after isoniazid ingestion are refractory to standard therapy, because isoniazid competes with pyridoxine and produces a functional pyridoxine and gamma-aminobutyric acid deficiency. Pyridoxine is given together with the benzodiazepine [ROSH][PEER-CS].
- A simple febrile seizure is generalized, under 15 minutes, a single episode in 24 hours, in a child aged 6 months to 5 years, and needs an antipyretic and reassurance only; it accounts for 85% of febrile seizures. Complex features are duration over 15 minutes, focal onset, more than one episode in 24 hours, or age under 6 months [ROSH][PEER-CS].
- A prolonged postictal state or persistent altered mentation should raise suspicion for nonconvulsive status epilepticus, confirmed on electroencephalography; beta-lactam antibiotics are an associated trigger. Phenytoin is likely to be ineffective for seizures from tricyclic antidepressant overdose [PEER-CS][PEER-PH].

### Infection Pearls

- Antibiotics for suspected bacterial meningitis are never delayed for CT or lumbar puncture; delay increases mortality and neurologic deficits. Blood cultures are obtained first only when they cause no delay, and the CSF culture remains valid if obtained within 2 hours of the first dose. Head CT before lumbar puncture is for focal deficit, altered mental status, head trauma, new-onset seizures, papilledema, immunocompromise, or known CNS disease [PEER-CS][ROSH].
- Dexamethasone is given just before or with the first antibiotic dose and continued every 6 hours for 4 days, with the greatest benefit in pneumococcal disease. Empiric coverage is ceftriaxone plus vancomycin, with ampicillin added for patients over 50 years, under 3 months, or immunocompromised, and vancomycin added where ceftriaxone resistance exceeds 1% [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Encephalitis is meningitis plus neurologic dysfunction — focal weakness, seizures, or personality change — and altered mental status is the most specific presentation of viral encephalitis in children. Close contacts of meningococcal meningitis need prophylaxis with rifampin, ciprofloxacin, or ceftriaxone [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Shunt infection presents in the first month after placement and may produce only headache, nausea, vomiting, lethargy, or altered mental status once the infection obstructs the system; fever may be absent. Coagulase-negative staphylococci cause 50% and Staphylococcus aureus 33% [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- A shunt tap opening pressure greater than 20 cm H2O means distal obstruction, because CSF cannot drain into the peritoneum. Collapsed slit ventricles mean overshunting, not underdrainage. Paralysis of upgaze and the "setting sun" appearance are the bedside signs of raised pressure from shunt malfunction [PEER-PH][ROSH].

### Spinal Pearls

- Spinal epidural abscess is a can't-miss cause of back pain, and the examination is unreliable early: the classic triad of back pain, fever, and neurologic deficit is present in only about 15% of cases and fever in only 30–50%, while roughly two-thirds have a normal neurologic examination at the first visit. Back pain is the most common symptom, present in 70–90%. Risk factors are intravenous drug use, diabetes, immunosuppression, and recent spinal procedures [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- C-reactive protein is the most sensitive laboratory test and MRI of the spine with gadolinium the diagnostic study of choice, imaging the entire spine because the abscess may be non-contiguous [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Cauda equina syndrome is a surgical emergency. The red flags are acute bowel incontinence, urinary retention with a post-void residual greater than 200 mL, and saddle anesthesia; the pudendal nerve (S2 through S4) controls external anal sphincter tone and sensation. Compression must be relieved to prevent permanent deficit, and antibiotics are given as well [PEER-CS].

### Neuromuscular Pearls

- Myasthenia gravis is a disease of fatigability: weakness worsens with use and improves with rest, proximal exceeds distal, and half of patients present with ocular findings. A negative inspiratory force below 30 cm H2O warrants critical care admission, and −20 cm H2O with CO2 retention requires intubation — respiratory failure, not limb weakness, is what kills. The ice pack test takes 2 minutes on the affected eye, with 2 mm of improvement in ptosis considered positive [PEER-CS][PEER-PH].
- Guillain-Barré syndrome is ascending, symmetric, and areflexic, and follows a diarrheal or viral illness — Campylobacter jejuni is the most common trigger. Intravenous immune globulin and plasmapheresis are equally effective and shorten recovery, corticosteroids have consistently failed, and succinylcholine is contraindicated [PEER-PH][PEER-CS].
- Albuminocytologic dissociation — raised protein with a normal white cell count — is present in only 66% of Guillain-Barré syndrome cases at 1 week, so a normal lumbar puncture does not exclude the diagnosis [ROSH][PEER-CS].
- Weakness that improves with continued stimulation points to a presynaptic lesion such as Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome and should prompt a search for a perihilar mass. Ascending flaccid paralysis with ataxia after outdoor exposure suggests tick paralysis, where a complete skin and hair examination is both diagnostic and therapeutic [ROSH][PEER-PH].

### Delirium, Movement and Toxic-Metabolic Pearls

- Delirium is acute, fluctuating, and attentional; dementia is gradual and progressive. The Confusion Assessment Method and the Delirium Triage Screen are the bedside tools, and reversible causes — withdrawal, toxic ingestion, metabolic derangement — must be excluded before a primary neurologic diagnosis is accepted [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- An acute dystonic reaction can involve the larynx and be fatal. Benztropine 1–2 mg IV or IM or diphenhydramine 25–50 mg is given and continued for 3 days to prevent recurrence; laryngeal dystonia is treated with diphenhydramine 50 mg IV or benztropine 1–2 mg [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Lithium has a narrow therapeutic range of 0.8–1.2 mEq/L. Any reduction in renal perfusion — diarrheal illness, dehydration, heat exposure — precipitates toxicity, and dialysis is indicated when the level exceeds 4 mEq/L with impaired kidney function or at any level with mental status change, seizure, or dysrhythmia [PEER-CS].
- Wernicke encephalopathy is treated empirically with thiamine 500 mg IV to optimise passive diffusion across the blood-brain barrier. Only about 10% of patients have the full triad, and about 80% progress to Korsakoff syndrome, whose memory deficit rarely improves [PEER-CS].
- Sympathomimetic toxicity with a core temperature above 41.1°C is a toxicologic emergency requiring intubation, sedation, and paralysis; wide-complex tachycardia from cocaine's sodium channel blockade is treated with sodium bicarbonate 1 to 2 mg/kg IV while keeping serum pH below 7.55. The therapeutic goal of naloxone is adequate respirations and oxygenation, not full arousal [PEER-CS].
- Myxedema coma is treated empirically with intravenous levothyroxine 200–400 mcg and hydrocortisone before laboratory confirmation, and its hyponatremia is managed with free water restriction [PEER-CS].

## مراجعة عربية مركزة

يبدأ تقييم المريض المشتبه بإصابته بسكتة دماغية (stroke) بقياس سكر الدم بالوخز الإصبعي، لأن نقص سكر الدم يحاكي العجز البؤري ويُصحَّح فوراً. التصوير المقطعي المحوسب بدون صبغة هو الفحص الأول، ويكشف النزف تحت العنكبوتية (subarachnoid hemorrhage) بحساسية 98–100% خلال 6 ساعات؛ وبعدها يُنتقل إلى تصوير الأوعية. يُعطى مميع الخثرة الوريدي (tPA) خلال 4.5 ساعة، ويُمنع عند وجود نزف داخل القحف سابق أو سكتة إسكيمية خلال 3 أشهر أو نزف هضمي خلال 21 يوم. في النزف المخي التلقائي يُعطى labetalol وريدياً للوصول إلى ضغط انقباضي 140 mm Hg، بينما يُحافَظ في النزف تحت العنكبوتية على ضغط انقباضي أقل من 160 mm Hg مع إعطاء nimodipine لأنه يخفض الوفيات.

في الصداع، لا تُطبَّق قاعدة Ottawa لاستبعاد النزف تحت العنكبوتية خارج فئتها المحددة؛ فالمريض ذو العجز العصبي البؤري الجديد لا يمكن استبعاده بها. صداع ما بعد البزل القطني (post–dural puncture headache) وضعي ويبدأ خلال 48 ساعة، وتقلل الإبرة غير القاطعة من حدوثه. في الدوار، غياب الحركة التصحيحية عند اختبار دفع الرأس يشير إلى سبب مركزي. يُعالج الصداع العنقودي (cluster headache) بالأكسجين 100% بمعدل 12–15 L/min لمدة 15 دقيقة أو sumatriptan 6 mg تحت الجلد، ويُستخدم verapamil للوقاية.

حالة الصرع (status epilepticus) هي نوبة تتجاوز 5 دقائق أو نوبات متكررة دون عودة للوعي، والعلاج الأول lorazepam بجرعة 0.1 mg/kg وحد أقصى 4 mg، وكثيراً ما يُعطى بجرعة ناقصة. لا يمكن إرجاع حالة الصرع إلى انسحاب الكحول وحده، بل يجب البحث عن نقص السكر أو نقص الصوديوم أو نزف أو التهاب سحايا. في التهاب السحايا الجرثومي لا تُؤجَّل المضادات الحيوية من أجل التصوير أو البزل القطني، ويُعطى ceftriaxone مع vancomycin، مع إضافة ampicillin لمن تجاوز 50 عاماً أو يقل عن 3 أشهر أو لديه نقص مناعة، ويُضاف dexamethasone مع الجرعة الأولى.

في الطوارئ الشوكية، ألم الظهر هو العرض الأكثر شيوعاً في الخراج فوق الجافية الشوكي (spinal epidural abscess)، لكن الثلاثي الكلاسيكي لا يظهر إلا في نحو 15% من الحالات، والرنين المغناطيسي مع الصبغة هو الفحص المختار. متلازمة ذيل الفرس (cauda equina syndrome) طارئة جراحية تتمثل باحتباس البول مع حجم متبقٍّ أكبر من 200 mL وسلسل برازي وتخدير سرجي. في الوهن العضلي الوبيل (myasthenia gravis)، إن كان اختبار قوة الشهيق السلبية أقل من 30 cm H2O وجب إدخال المريض للعناية المركزة. وتُعالج متلازمة غيلان-باريه (Guillain-Barré syndrome) بالغلوبيولين المناعي الوريدي أو تبدل البلازما، ولا تفيد الستيروئيدات. أخيراً، يُعالج اعتلال الدماغ الفيرنيكي (Wernicke encephalopathy) بالثيامين 500 mg وريدياً.
