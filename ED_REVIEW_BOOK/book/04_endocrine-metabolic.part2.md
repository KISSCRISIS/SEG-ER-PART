## Pathophysiology

### The adrenal cortex

- Cortisol maintains normoglycaemia via gluconeogenesis and blood pressure by sensitising vessels to vasoconstrictors; aldosterone drives renal sodium reabsorption and potassium excretion under the renin-angiotensin system. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Cortisol loss gives hypotension and hypoglycaemia, aldosterone loss hyponatremia and hyperkalemia; glucocorticoid deficiency lowers vascular responsiveness to angiotensin II and norepinephrine, so adrenal crisis hypotension can be vasopressor-refractory. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- In primary adrenal insufficiency the gland fails while the axis stays intact, so ACTH is high and stimulates melanocytes, producing the hyperpigmentation of Addison disease. Aldosterone is largely ACTH-independent, so hyperkalemia separates primary from secondary disease. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Chronic exogenous glucocorticoids suppress ACTH and corticotropin-releasing hormone by negative feedback, so no stress response can be mounted. Adrenal dysfunction is common in septic shock; rifampin induces cytochrome P450 and accelerates hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone metabolism. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Congenital adrenal hyperplasia is deficiency of a cortisol-synthesis enzyme, over 90% 21-hydroxylase, with accompanying aldosterone deficiency: impaired renal sodium reabsorption and potassium excretion give hyponatremia and hyperkalemia, and sodium loss drags water into the urine causing dehydration with metabolic acidosis. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]

### The hypothalamic-pituitary unit

- The pituitary lies in the sella turcica, joined to the hypothalamus by the infundibulum and hypophysial stalk; the anterior lobe secretes growth hormone, TSH, ACTH, gonadotropins and prolactin. [PEER-PHYS]
- A posterior pituitary lesion causes arginine vasopressin deficiency, so the kidney cannot concentrate urine and polyuria with polydipsia follows; vasopressin is synthesised in the hypothalamus, so damage anywhere along the tract disrupts release. [ROSH]
- Suprasellar extension compresses the optic chiasm where fibres from the bilateral temporal visual fields cross, producing bitemporal hemianopsia; headache, diplopia and seizures also occur. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Pituitary apoplexy is haemorrhage or infarction, usually within a macroadenoma; pregnancy enlarges the pituitary, dopamine agonists cause atrophy with reduced angiogenesis, and adenomas disrupt vasculature. Abrupt headache, visual defects and ophthalmoplegia follow, with hypotension when ACTH delivery fails. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Post-traumatic hypopituitarism from stalk disruption causes secondary adrenal insufficiency. Sheehan syndrome is pituitary infarction after postpartum haemorrhage, giving panhypopituitarism with inability to lactate and cold intolerance. [ROSH]

### The thyroid axis

- Falling T3 and T4 release thyrotropin-releasing hormone, driving pituitary TSH release and thyroid hormone secretion; free T4 provides negative feedback on TSH. [ROSH]
- Hypothyroidism is generalised slowing: glycosaminoglycan accumulation gives nonpitting facial and lower-extremity oedema (myxedema), with reduced heart rate and contractility, respiratory muscle fatigue and impaired respiratory compensation to hypercapnia and hypoxaemia. [PEER-PHYS]
- Myxedema coma is the decompensated form: hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension and reduced ventilation, with hyaluronic acid deposition giving nonpitting facial and pretibial oedema and macroglossia with oropharyngeal oedema that can make the airway difficult. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Thyroid storm is a hypermetabolic state of severe thyrotoxicosis, usually precipitated by acute stress such as infection, myocardial infarction, surgery or trauma, with high-output heart failure as a complication; diagnosis is clinical with low TSH and elevated free T4 and T3. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Amiodarone is 37% iodine by weight and causes thyroid disease in either direction: iodine blocks organification and release of hormone, while increased T3 and T4 synthesis or destructive thyroiditis causes thyrotoxicosis. [PEER-PHYS]
- Lithium concentrates in the thyroid, competes for iodide transport, impairs T3 synthesis and release and inhibits peripheral T4 to T3 conversion. In nonthyroidal illness, reduced type I 5'-deiodinase activity gives a low T3 with normal or slightly increased reverse T3 and normal T4 and TSH. [ROSH]
- Postpartum thyroiditis is an autoimmune destructive thyroiditis that may be hyperthyroid, hypothyroid or both; subacute (de Quervain) thyroiditis follows a viral illness. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]

### Catecholamines and pheochromocytoma

- Pheochromocytoma is a catecholamine-secreting tumour of adrenal medullary chromaffin cells. Alpha-1 stimulation causes vasoconstriction and raised systemic vascular resistance, normally offset by beta-2 mediated vasodilation, so blocking beta before alpha leaves vasoconstriction unopposed. [PEER-PHYS]
- Glucocorticoids stimulate catecholamine release and can trigger hypertensive crisis and pheochromocytoma multisystem crisis; other triggers are metoclopramide, glucagon, tricyclic antidepressants and beta-blockers without alpha blockade. Hereditary disease associates with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 and von Hippel-Lindau syndrome. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]

### Insulin and the hyperglycaemic emergencies

- Insulin governs metabolism and storage of carbohydrate, fat and protein; its relative absence dysregulates counter-regulatory hormones and produces hyperglycaemia, osmotic diuresis with dehydration, ketone formation from free fatty acids and an elevated anion gap metabolic acidosis, with hepatic glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis. [PEER-PHYS]
- Excess glucose overwhelms renal tubular reabsorption, causing glucosuria and an osmotic diuresis that drags sodium, potassium, magnesium, calcium, phosphorus and water into the tubule. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Acidosis shifts potassium out of cells, so serum potassium in diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) may be high, normal or low despite severe total body depletion; insulin then drives potassium intracellularly and can cause precipitous hypokalemia. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- In hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS) glucose rises over days: falling renal glucose clearance and reduced insulin sensitivity produce extreme hyperglycaemia, hyperosmolarity and profound dehydration with little ketone formation. Hyperosmolarity reduces cerebral blood flow, causing delirium, coma and focal deficits, typically above a plasma osmolality of 330 mmol/kg. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Euglycaemic DKA associates with sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, which impair renal glucose reabsorption; urinary glucose losses may reduce insulin secretion and increase counter-regulatory hormones such as epinephrine and glucagon. Thiazides precipitate HHS by impairing insulin secretion, increasing insulin resistance and promoting fluid loss. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Sulfonylureas block pancreatic beta-cell potassium channels, causing depolarisation and insulin release; diazoxide does the opposite, suppressing insulin secretion. Glucagon from pancreatic alpha cells activates hepatic glycogenolysis through the cyclic adenosine monophosphate pathway. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]

### Acid-base physiology

- Primary metabolic disorders change bicarbonate and respiratory disorders change pCO2; compensation pulls pH toward normal, so in metabolic alkalosis the lungs decrease minute ventilation and pCO2 rises. Acidosis drives deep, rapid (Kussmaul) respiration. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Salicylates inhibit enzymes of oxidative phosphorylation, producing profound metabolic acidosis, while simultaneously stimulating the medullary respiratory centre to produce an independent respiratory alkalosis. Xenobiotics inhibiting cellular respiration include carbon monoxide, cyanide, hydrogen sulfide, clenbuterol and sodium azide. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Acetazolamide increases renal excretion of bicarbonate, potassium and free water, producing a non-anion gap (hyperchloraemic) acidosis; diarrhoea causes normal anion gap acidosis through bicarbonate loss and vomiting causes alkalosis through hydrogen ion loss. [ROSH]
- Hyperventilation lowers arterial CO2 and, since cerebral blood flow is autoregulated by CO2, cerebral vasculature constricts and perfusion falls; the distal paresthesias can outlast the panic attack, and alkalosis also lowers ionised calcium. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]

### Sodium and water

- Hyperglycaemia shifts water intravascularly and dilutes serum sodium, which falls approximately 1.6 mEq/L for every 100 mg/dL rise in glucose above 100 mg/dL. [ROSH]
- In syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH) antidiuretic hormone cannot be suppressed, so free water is reabsorbed at the late distal tubule and collecting duct; patients are euvolemic with concentrated urine despite low serum osmolality, and the excess water is intracellular, so there is no oedema, ascites or heart failure. [ROSH]
- Hyponatremia in children most often follows total body sodium loss from the gastrointestinal or urinary tract, or increased body water from SIADH, kidney injury, hyperglycaemia, heart failure or nephrotic syndrome. Exercise-associated hyponatremia follows excessive fluid intake, and correcting sodium too quickly risks osmotic demyelination syndrome. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]

### Potassium, magnesium and calcium

- Potassium is predominantly intracellular with less than 2% circulating, normally 3.5–5.0 mEq/L. Hyperkalemia impairs neuromuscular transmission through persistent depolarisation from elevated extracellular potassium, producing weakness, paralysis and conduction abnormalities. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Hypokalemic periodic paralysis produces transient intracellular potassium shift into skeletal muscle; triggers are alcohol, a carbohydrate-rich meal and rest after strenuous exercise, with calcium and sodium channel mutations described. Magnesium deficiency impairs sodium-potassium pump function, giving hypokalemia refractory to potassium replacement alone. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Parathyroid hormone (PTH) raises serum calcium by activating osteoclasts, increasing intestinal and renal calcium reabsorption, increasing vitamin D synthesis and increasing renal phosphorus excretion; low calcium stimulates PTH synthesis and calcitriol inhibits its release. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Primary hyperparathyroidism from a solitary adenoma produces hypercalcaemia with hypophosphataemia and classically presents as asymptomatic hypercalcaemia. Hypoparathyroidism after thyroid surgery gives hypocalcaemia with hyperphosphataemia, and the raised calcium-phosphate product precipitates in soft tissue, the basal ganglia being especially susceptible. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Hypercalcaemia in malignancy arises from a PTH-related protein from solid tumours that binds PTH receptors, from osteoclastic activity of bony metastases, and from vitamin D analogues produced by lymphomas. Other causes are milk-alkali syndrome, vitamin D or A toxicity, immobilisation and granulomatous disease such as sarcoidosis. [ROSH]
- Vitamin D deficiency produces hypocalcaemia with paresthesias, tetany and QT prolongation plus osteomalacia and rickets; rickets reflects decreased mineralisation of the epiphyseal growth plate with physeal widening, cupping and fraying of the metaphyses and bowing of the legs. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]

### Thiamine, other vitamins and refeeding

- Thiamine (vitamin B1) is a cofactor for transketolase, pyruvate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase and is required for conversion of pyruvate to acetyl coenzyme A; deficiency causes abnormal myelin and abnormal glucose-derived neurotransmitter production in the central nervous system, and body stores last about 18 days. [ROSH][PEER-PHYS]
- Dry beriberi damages peripheral nerves and includes Wernicke encephalopathy and Korsakoff syndrome; wet beriberi causes cardiovascular dysfunction with dyspnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea and oedema; a gastrointestinal form causes abdominal pain with lactic acidosis. Korsakoff syndrome gives anterograde and retrograde amnesia with normal long-term memory and cognition. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
- Vitamin B12 requires gastric intrinsic factor for absorption in the terminal ileum, and pernicious anaemia is autoimmune destruction of intrinsic factor; deficiency causes subacute combined degeneration with symmetric loss of vibratory and proprioceptive sensation, spasticity, gait ataxia and distal paresthesias. Folate is water-soluble with no significant stores, so alcohol use disorder depletes it rapidly. [ROSH]
- Refeeding syndrome follows repletion after starvation: once amino acids are available for protein synthesis, phosphorus shifts intracellularly and is depleted extracellularly, hypophosphataemia being the most classic abnormality alongside hypokalemia and hypomagnesaemia, which can cause dysrhythmias, heart failure, pericardial effusions and cardiorespiratory failure. [PEER-PHYS][ROSH]
