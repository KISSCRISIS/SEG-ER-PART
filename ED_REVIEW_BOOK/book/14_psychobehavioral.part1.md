## High-Yield Exam Review

- Verbal de-escalation is the first technique for aggression; chemical and physical restraint follow only when it fails. [PEER-CS]
- Intramuscular midazolam sedates more rapidly than any other intramuscular agent used for chemical sedation. [PEER-PH]
- Haloperidol and ziprasidone carry the highest risk of QT prolongation; risperidone does not significantly prolong the QT and is preferred in older adults. [PEER-CS]
- Olanzapine and clozapine carry the highest metabolic risk; clozapine is reserved for refractory schizophrenia because of agranulocytosis. [PEER-CS]
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome: altered mental status, hyperthermia, dysautonomia, bradyreflexia and rigidity days to weeks after starting or increasing an antipsychotic. [PEER-CS]
- Tardive dyskinesia appears months to years later as painless repetitive orofacial movements; acute dystonia is treated with diphenhydramine. [PEER-CS]
- Previous suicide attempt is the strongest predictor of completed suicide; the highest rate is in men older than 75 years. [PEER-PH]
- The SAD PERSONS scale scores 0–4 low, 5–6 medium, 7–10 high; a high-risk patient with an active plan and firearm access must be kept involuntarily for psychiatric evaluation. [PEER-CS]
- Every patient with altered mental status needs a capillary blood glucose check, even when the cause appears obvious. [PEER-PH]
- Sudden onset of psychosis over hours to days suggests a medical cause; insidious onset with auditory hallucinations suggests a primary psychiatric disorder, while visual hallucinations point to a medical cause. [PEER-PH]
- A stable exacerbation of a known psychiatric disorder with a normal physical examination needs no routine laboratory testing or imaging before psychiatric evaluation. [PEER-PH]
- Alcohol withdrawal is treated with benzodiazepines or phenobarbital; lorazepam 2 mg IV reduced recurrent withdrawal seizures from 24% to 3% over a 6-hour observation period. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Symptom-triggered benzodiazepine dosing in alcohol withdrawal lowers total dose, shortens treatment and shortens length of stay. [PEER-CS]
- Buprenorphine is a partial mu-opioid agonist with a ceiling effect on sedation, but can precipitate withdrawal in a patient still receiving full agonists such as methadone. [PEER-CS]
- Naltrexone, acamprosate and disulfiram are the only FDA-approved medications for alcohol use disorder; naltrexone additionally reduces craving. [PEER-PH]
- Opioid withdrawal is not life-threatening; benzodiazepine withdrawal is. Myalgias occur in opioid withdrawal but not alcohol withdrawal. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Purging by vomiting causes hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis; refeeding syndrome with hypophosphatemia is the danger when a normal diet is restarted. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- The TEN-4-FACESp rule flags bruising concerning for abuse in children under 4 years; any bruising in a child under 6 months has a very high likelihood of intentional trauma. [ROSH]
- Delirium is abrupt and fluctuating; dementia is progressive over months to years and does not fluctuate. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Suspected child abuse is reported to child protective services and suspected elder mistreatment to state Adult Protective Services. [ROSH]

## Core Concepts

### Psychosis: Primary Psychiatric versus Medical

- Medical cause suggested by abnormal vital signs, non-auditory hallucinations, substance use, or acute mental status change without personal or family psychiatric history. [PEER-CS]
- Primary psychiatric illness: gradual onset at ages 15–25, disorganized thought and behaviour, auditory hallucinations; onset of confusion after age 40 suggests a medical cause. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Cocaine psychosis risk factors: younger age at first use, male sex, prolonged use, high doses and pre-existing mental illness. [PEER-PH]
- Command hallucinations persisting despite sobriety suggest organic psychosis; those resolving with sobriety suggest substance-induced psychosis. [PEER-CS]
- Anti-N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR) encephalitis is an autoimmune cause of new-onset psychosis in young women, with a viral-like prodrome preceding it by weeks. [PEER-CS]

### Schizophrenia Spectrum

- Schizophrenia: prevalence up to 1%, onset late teens to mid-30s; diagnosis requires symptoms for at least 6 months including delusions or disorganized speech plus hallucinations or negative symptoms. [ROSH]
- Schizophreniform disorder and brief psychotic disorder differ from schizophrenia by symptom duration; the prodrome is dominated by negative symptoms. [PEER-CS]
- Positive symptoms are hallucinations, delusions and disorganization; negative symptoms are flat affect, alogia and apathy. [PEER-PH]
- Delusional disorder: a delusion persisting at least 1 month, not medically explained, not meeting criterion A for schizophrenia; persecutory is the most common subtype. [PEER-CS]
- Violence risk rises with auditory hallucinations, prior violence, extreme excitement, systematization of delusions and long illness duration. [ROSH]

### Antipsychotic Pharmacology

- Atypical antipsychotics are more effective than typical agents against negative symptoms. [PEER-PH]
- Extrapyramidal symptoms result from dopamine D2 blockade in the basal ganglia; high-potency first-generation agents (haloperidol, droperidol, fluphenazine) are most often implicated. [PEER-CS]
- Tardive dyskinesia risk rises with older age and longer therapy; the olanzapine wafer is bioequivalent to the tablet, and clozapine can be used safely in drug-induced parkinsonism. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

### Mood Disorders

- Major depressive disorder: five or more symptoms nearly every day for at least 2 consecutive weeks, at least one being depressed mood or anhedonia. [ROSH]
- Major depression with psychotic features adds delusions or hallucinations with themes of guilt or hopelessness; it is more common in women. [ROSH]
- Bipolar I and II differ by the occurrence of a manic episode, which features pressured speech, grandiosity, decreased need for sleep and flight of ideas. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Postpartum depression affects about 10% of patients up to 12 months after delivery and requires at least 5 symptoms of major depression for at least 2 weeks. [PEER-CS]
- Seasonal affective disorder is a subclass of major depressive disorder with fall-winter or spring-summer onset; winter-onset disease is treated with phototherapy. [PEER-CS]
- Steroid-induced psychosis usually develops within the first 6 weeks of corticosteroid therapy; the dose thought necessary exceeds 40 mg of prednisone daily. [ROSH]
- Tramadol and other opioids can cause sedation and increase the risk of depression and suicide. [ROSH]
- Antidepressants have delayed onset, with sertraline taking 4–6 weeks; bupropion is avoided in eating disorders and in patients with seizure risk. [ROSH]

### Anxiety, Somatic and Functional Disorders

- Panic attacks: discrete intense fear without real danger, peaking within about 10 minutes and lasting up to 1 hour, with at least 4 of 13 cognitive and physical symptoms. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Panic disorder is recurrent panic attacks with a normal examination and vital signs. [PEER-PH]
- Agoraphobia is fear of being alone in public places where escape would be difficult; up to 75% of patients experience panic attacks there. [PEER-CS]
- Anxiety is a diagnosis of exclusion; a serum glucose is the first test, as hypoglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis both mimic it. [PEER-CS]
- Generalized anxiety disorder: excessive worry dominating life on most days for at least 6 months, with a lifetime prevalence around 10%. [ROSH]
- Illness anxiety disorder is persistent unrealistic worry about illness despite repeated negative testing; comorbid with generalized anxiety disorder in about 70%. [ROSH]
- Functional neurologic symptom disorder is a diagnosis of exclusion for involuntary loss of neurologic function, sometimes with la belle indifference; in malingering symptoms are conscious. [PEER-CS]
- Factitious disorder is intentional production of symptoms without external reward; malingering is distinguished by obvious secondary gain. [PEER-PH]
- Obsessive-compulsive disorder: intrusive thoughts neutralised by repetitive rituals; cognitive behavioural therapy is first-line, with SSRI therapy when impairment is severe. [ROSH]

### Substance Use Disorders

- Substance use disorder is a problematic pattern of use causing clinically significant distress and impairment; risk factors include positive family history and prior abuse. [ROSH]
- DSM-5 defines alcohol use disorder by eleven criteria and two or more are diagnostic; continuing to drink after alcohol-related injuries is hazardous use. [PEER-PH]
- Risky drinking, predictive of later alcohol use disorder and dependence, is 15 or more standard drinks per week in men. [ROSH]
- Opioid use disorder requires two or more specific behaviours in 12 months causing impairment or distress; tolerance and dependence with a withdrawal syndrome qualify. [PEER-PH]
- Long-term severe alcohol abuse is strongly associated with alcoholic cardiomyopathy, a dilated cardiomyopathy with reduced ejection fraction. [PEER-PH]

### Eating Disorders

- Anorexia nervosa: abnormally low body weight, distorted body image and fear of weight gain; the restrictive subtype limits intake, the binge-eating/purging subtype induces vomiting or abuses laxatives. [PEER-CS]
- Associated features include osteoporosis, thinning hair, hypothyroidism with bradycardia, and muscle wasting. [ROSH]
- Bulimia nervosa: recurrent binge eating with loss of control followed by compensatory behaviours; weight is usually normal, onset is 13–18 years, and it is three times more common in women. [ROSH]
- Refeeding syndrome occurs when a normal diet is started too quickly, worsening baseline hypophosphatemia through increased phosphate use for adenosine triphosphate production. [ROSH]
- Pica is persistent eating of non-nutritive substances for more than 1 month; between 10% and 25% of eating disorder cases occur in men. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

### Dementia and Delirium

- Dementia is progressive decline over months to years that does not fluctuate; delirium is a fluctuating alteration in behaviour and sensorium with abrupt onset, worse at night. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Delirium precipitants: infection, dehydration, electrolyte derangement, intoxication, trauma, benzodiazepines and anticholinergics, and unfamiliar settings or a lost hearing aid. [ROSH]
- Alzheimer disease reflects progressive loss of cholinergic innervation; Lewy body dementia causes visual hallucinations and parkinsonian features; frontotemporal disease causes disinhibition and hyperorality. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### The Agitated Patient

- Sequence: verbal de-escalation and environmental modification first, then chemical sedation, then physical restraint; approaching psychotic patients first with sedation and restraints can escalate behaviour. [PEER-CS]
- Benzodiazepines such as diazepam are preferred for sympathomimetic toxicity requiring sedation and are the mainstay for phencyclidine intoxication and its seizures. [PEER-CS][PEER-PH]
- In pregnancy, verbal de-escalation comes first, then oral diphenhydramine; haloperidol is widely used for moderate to severe agitation. [ROSH]

### Abuse, Neglect and Exploitation

- Physical abuse is the most easily identified form of child maltreatment, neglect is the most common form, and bruising is the most common abusive injury. [ROSH]
- Concerning fractures: metaphyseal corner fractures from shaking, posterior rib fractures, and any fracture in a non-ambulatory child. [ROSH]
- Concerning history: delay in seeking care, conflicting or changing stories, and explanations inconsistent with the child's age. [ROSH]
- Elder abuse: up to 2 million people aged 60 and older are abused or neglected yearly in the United States; psychological and emotional abuse is the most prevalent form and occurs most in the home. [ROSH]
- Intimate partner violence: a central injury pattern is more likely non-accidental than extremity injury, the face is the most common site, and psychological aggression is the most common type. [PEER-CS][PEER-PH]
- Human trafficking takes three forms and requires coercion; branding tattoos showing possession are specific to it. [ROSH][PEER-CS]

### Grief and Physician Wellness

- Fearful nightmares characterise PTSD and are uncommon in grief, which produces sad dreams that trigger sadness rather than fear. [PEER-PH]
- Complicated grief prevents a return to normal life for longer than 6 to 12 months; violent death is a risk factor. [PEER-PH]
- PTSD and acute stress disorder differ by chronicity: acute stress disorder resolves within 1 month, and PTSD persists beyond it. [PEER-PH]
- Second victim syndrome describes the emotional effect of a patient safety event on the responsible physician; residents are at higher risk of depression and suicide than the general population. [PEER-PH]
