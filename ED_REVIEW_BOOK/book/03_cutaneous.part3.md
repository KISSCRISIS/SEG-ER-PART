## Diagnosis

### Recognising skin cancer

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Pearly papule or nodule with raised, rolled borders and telangiectasias on sun-exposed skin, or a persistent sore that bleeds with minor trauma and recurs; morpheaform subtype is a flat scar-like plaque | Basal cell carcinoma [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Well-defined red or brown thickened, crusted patch on lip, ear, head, or neck | Squamous cell carcinoma [ROSH] |
| Firm, fixed unilateral neck mass in an older adult; 75% of neck masses in this age group are neoplastic | Squamous cell carcinoma metastatic to cervical nodes [PEER-PH] |
| Asymmetry, irregular border, colour variation, diameter over 6 mm, evolution; any new mole after age 35. Dark nail band reaching the nail fold | Melanoma; subungual melanoma (Hutchinson sign) [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Firm, painless, elliptical purple macules along skin tension lines in advanced HIV | Kaposi sarcoma; biopsy required [PEER-PH] |

### Differentiating skin and soft tissue infection

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| "Cobblestoning" on ultrasound | Cellulitis — drainage not indicated [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Well-circumscribed, hypoechoic, centrally avascular fluid collection | Abscess — incise and drain [PEER-PH] |
| Well-demarcated erythema with dermal oedema and peau d'orange | Erysipelas, usually of the lower extremity [ROSH] |
| Pain out of proportion, crepitus, bullae, dusky skin, spread over hours | Necrotizing soft tissue infection [ROSH] |
| Perioral crusting then tender erythroderma, exfoliation, positive Nikolsky sign | Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome [ROSH] |
| Foul-smelling umbilical drainage with surrounding erythema before day 14 | Omphalitis (normal cord separation has discharge but no erythema) [ROSH] |
| Beefy red painful rash in skin folds with satellite lesions | *Candida* diaper dermatitis [ROSH] |
| Recurrent painful nodules and abscesses with cellulitis in the axilla | Hidradenitis suppurativa [ROSH] |

### Differentiating blistering disease

| Feature | Pemphigus vulgaris | Bullous pemphigoid | SJS / TEN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blister | Flaccid, fragile | Tense | Flaccid, sloughing |
| Nikolsky sign | Positive | Negative | Positive |
| Mucous membranes | Almost all patients; often first | Uncommon | Present |
| Extent | Not staged by area | Not staged by area | SJS under 10% BSA; overlap 10–30%; TEN over 30% [ROSH] |

- Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome is separated from SJS/TEN by sparing of the intraoral mucous membranes; the lips may be involved, but intraoral involvement is not seen. [ROSH]

### Purpura, vasculitis, and exanthem

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Palpable purpura in crops on buttocks and lower extremities at pressure points, with abdominal and joint pain in a child | Immunoglobulin A vasculitis [ROSH] |
| Palpable purpura, urticaria, fever, arthritis, and lymphadenopathy 7–10 days after a beta-lactam | Hypersensitivity vasculitis (serum sickness–like reaction) [PEER-PH] |
| Tender, erythematous, nonulcerated, immobile pretibial nodules with fever and arthralgias | Erythema nodosum [ROSH] |
| Schistocytes on the peripheral smear with thrombocytopenia | Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, not immune thrombocytopenia [PEER-PH] |
| Oozing intravenous sites, low fibrinogen, high D-dimer, prolonged PT and aPTT, low factors V and VIII | Disseminated intravascular coagulation [PEER-PH] |
| Target lesions with a dusky centre, oral erosions, cough, and shortness of breath | Erythema multiforme; consider *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* [ROSH] |
| Dusky haemorrhagic pustules on palms or soles with migratory arthralgias | Gonococcemia [PEER-PH] |
| Diffuse rash, facial oedema, eosinophilia, and raised alanine aminotransferase 3 weeks after a new drug | Drug-induced hypersensitivity syndrome (DRESS) [ROSH][PEER-PH] |

### Papulosquamous, eczematous, and infectious eruptions

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Herald patch 2–5 cm then a fir-tree papulosquamous rash along cleavage lines on the trunk | Pityriasis rosea [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Well-demarcated plaques with a silver scale on scalp, extensor elbows, knees, gluteal cleft; Auspitz sign | Psoriasis [ROSH] |
| Linear streaks of erythema, papules, vesicles, or bullae confined to the contact area | Allergic contact dermatitis (type IV) [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Intensely pruritic interdigital excoriations with similarly affected contacts | Scabies [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Scaly reddish-brown itchy groin rash that does not fluoresce under Wood lamp | Tinea cruris, most often *Trichophyton rubrum* [ROSH] |
| Patchy nonscarring hair loss with peripheral scaling; boggy suppurative lesion is a kerion | Tinea capitis due to *Trichophyton* [ROSH] |
| Painful oral and genital ulcers that wax and wane without resolving, with abdominal cramping | Behçet syndrome; scrotal lesions occur in up to 75% [ROSH] |

### Confirmatory and supportive testing

- Punch or shave biopsy confirms basal cell carcinoma; biopsy is required for Kaposi sarcoma and for pemphigus vulgaris and bullous pemphigoid. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Ultrasound distinguishes abscess from cellulitis and detects subcutaneous lipoma with approximately 95% sensitivity and 94% specificity. MRI is the imaging modality of choice to exclude osteomyelitis under a pressure ulcer. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Blood cultures are indicated in sepsis physiology; bloodstream infection occurs in as many as 79% of patients with sepsis related to an acute soft tissue infection in the setting of a chronic decubitus ulcer. [PEER-PH]
- Urinalysis and creatinine are the key screens in immunoglobulin A vasculitis, in which glomerular involvement is the main predictor of long-term outcome and permanent kidney injury occurs in up to 2% of children. Contrast enema is diagnostic and therapeutic for suspected intussusception. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Wood lamp: *Microsporum* fluoresces, *Trichophyton* does not, *Candida* fluoresces. Potassium hydroxide preparation shows segmented hyphae in dermatophyte infection. Vesicles on the tip of the nose indicate herpes zoster ophthalmicus and require full ophthalmologic evaluation. [ROSH]

## Management

### Time-critical pathways

1. **Necrotizing soft tissue infection** — intravenous fluids, piperacillin-tazobactam plus vancomycin plus clindamycin, immediate surgical consultation and debridement; diagnosis is confirmed by direct visualization of the fascial planes in the operating room. [ROSH]
2. **SJS/TEN** — stop the offending medication, give crystalloid supportive care, and transfer to a specialized burn centre; SCORTEN determines prognosis. [ROSH]
3. **Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome** — intravenous oxacillin or nafcillin, analgesia, fluid replacement, and gentle wound care; avoid tight dressings and adhesives. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
4. **Omphalitis** — wound culture and admission for intravenous antibiotics. [ROSH]
5. **Anaphylaxis or angioedema** — epinephrine; urticaria without airway or systemic involvement is not anaphylaxis. [ROSH]

### Antimicrobial selection

| Scenario | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Uncomplicated cellulitis, no significant comorbidity | Oral first-generation cephalosporin (cephalexin) for 5–6 days [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Cellulitis with systemic toxicity or failure of outpatient therapy, or over a decubitus ulcer | Vancomycin plus piperacillin-tazobactam, intravenously [PEER-PH] |
| Necrotizing soft tissue infection | Piperacillin-tazobactam plus vancomycin plus clindamycin [ROSH] |
| MRSA coverage | Vancomycin, linezolid, or clindamycin; for community-acquired MRSA, oral doxycycline, clindamycin, or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Abscess after incision and drainage | Antibiotics only for immunosuppression, failure to resolve, systemic infection, extremes of age, or recurrence [PEER-PH] |
| Human bite | Amoxicillin-clavulanate first line; trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole plus metronidazole as an alternative [ROSH] |
| Dog or cat bite | Amoxicillin-clavulanate monotherapy, covering *Pasteurella multocida*, skin flora, and anaerobes [PEER-PH] |
| Limited impetigo | Topical mupirocin or retapamulin for 5 days [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Oral thrush with odynophagia (candidal esophagitis) | Oral fluconazole; nystatin swishes or buccal miconazole for mild disease [PEER-PH] |

### Draining and excising cutaneous lesions

- A simple abscess in an otherwise healthy patient is treated by incision and drainage alone; needle aspiration is inadequate even under ultrasound guidance. Fluctuant epidermoid cysts need drainage with removal of the cyst wall, with anaesthesia infiltrated around rather than into the lesion. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Wounds and ulcers

| Ulcer type | Management |
|---|---|
| Venous stasis ulcer | Compression and elevation are the mainstays; systemic antibiotics only when infected (fever, lymphangitis, localised heat or tenderness, spreading erythema); topical antibiotic creams are avoided because they cause allergic contact dermatitis [PEER-PH] |
| Diabetes-related neuropathic ulcer | Local debridement and care coordination [ROSH] |
| Pressure ulcer | Moist dressings, debridement of necrotic tissue, nutritional support, and pressure relief; a stage 3 ulcer with eschar needs hydrogel dressing and surgical consultation [PEER-PH][ROSH] |

- Only pressure ulcers with active infection should be cultured and given antibiotics; all ulcers are colonized. Progression to deeper infection is signalled by spreading erythema, warmth, induration, purulent drainage, tissue necrosis, and systemic symptoms. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Dermatitis, papulosquamous disease, and skin infection

| Condition | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Atopic dermatitis in an infant | Emollient ointments first line; low-potency topical corticosteroids or topical tacrolimus with emollients for flares [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Localised plaque psoriasis | Topical betamethasone or another high-potency topical corticosteroid [ROSH] |
| Seborrheic dermatitis | Reassurance and frequent shampooing; remove scale with a fine-tooth comb after oil or petrolatum [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Pityriasis rosea | Reassurance alone; topical triamcinolone or hydrocortisone and oral antihistamines (cetirizine, diphenhydramine) for pruritus [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Allergic contact dermatitis | Localised: topical high-potency corticosteroid and removal of the exposure. Extensive: oral prednisone, often a 3-week taper, with cool compresses, emollients, and topical triamcinolone [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Irritant diaper dermatitis | Zinc oxide 40% ointment with diaper-free periods [ROSH] |
| *Candida* diaper dermatitis | Topical nystatin; assess for concurrent thrush [ROSH] |
| Tinea cruris | Topical antifungal for 2 weeks [ROSH] |
| Tinea capitis or kerion | Oral antifungal (terbinafine, griseofulvin, or fluconazole); selenium sulfide, ketoconazole, or ciclopirox shampoo may increase cure rates [ROSH] |
| Scabies | Permethrin 5% cream washed off after 8–14 hours, repeated in 1 week if symptoms persist; wash clothing and bedding in hot water and treat close contacts [PEER-PH][ROSH] |

### Immune-mediated and vasculitic disease

- Pemphigus vulgaris is treated with steroids and immunomodulators alongside dermatology consultation; doxycycline forms part of the treatment of bullous pemphigoid. [ROSH]
- Erythema nodosum: NSAIDs such as ibuprofen reduce pain, swelling, and inflammation, and potassium iodide is an alternative initial agent; it resolves over several weeks, though new lesions may recur for 3–6 months. [ROSH]
- Immunoglobulin A vasculitis is managed supportively with NSAIDs for joint pain, used with care when the kidneys are involved; steroids may be substituted in significant kidney impairment. Most cases resolve within 6–8 weeks, with recurrence in up to 33%. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Admission is indicated for uncontrollable pain, renal insufficiency, gastrointestinal bleeding, altered mental status, dehydration, or an elevated serum creatinine. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Recurrent aphthous stomatitis: topical clobetasol 0.05% gel three times daily and sucralfate rinse four times daily; oral corticosteroids for complex disease. [ROSH]
- A first lifetime outbreak of genital herpes is an indication for antivirals regardless of time since onset, because primary outbreaks last up to 4 weeks; recurrent outbreaks are treated within 72 hours. [PEER-PH]
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation: identify and treat the underlying cause; when sepsis is suspected, antibiotics are the greatest priority, and blood components are given according to clinically significant bleeding or the need for an invasive procedure. [PEER-PH]

### Referral and disposition

- Basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma have no acute emergency department treatment; patients need short-interval dermatology follow-up for biopsy and possible excision, plus sun protection. [ROSH]
- Superficial cellulitis of the hand without an abscess is admitted for observation and antibiotics because it may progress rapidly to a deep space infection. [PEER-PH]
- Human bites require thorough irrigation, imaging to exclude a foreign body, tetanus vaccination if more than 10 years have elapsed, and prophylactic antibiotics. [ROSH]
