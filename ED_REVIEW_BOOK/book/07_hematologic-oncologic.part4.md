## Clinical Pearls

- Stop the transfusion first, always. Any fever during a transfusion is an acute haemolytic reaction until excluded, and most such reactions come from a human error in collection, testing or patient identification — so recheck the unit and the wristband before anything else. [PEER-PH] Anaphylaxis during transfusion with angioedema and hives should make you think of IgA deficiency rather than a simple urticarial reaction. [ROSH]
- Separate the two pulmonary reactions by their haemodynamics: TRALI gives fever, hypotension and a normal cardiac silhouette within 6 hours, while TACO gives hypertension and volume overload near the end of a large transfusion. A high fever points to TRALI. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Cyanosis that does not improve with supplemental oxygen and a saturation stuck near 85% is methaemoglobinaemia until proven otherwise; co-oximetry confirms it, and methylene blue is the antidote for levels above 30% or for symptomatic patients. Infants under 4 months are particularly vulnerable. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- In haemophilia, head trauma is a bleeding episode regardless of symptoms, GCS or a normal neurological examination. Give factor concentrate before the CT — delayed intracranial bleeding carries significant mortality, and imaging must never delay replacement. [PEER-PH][ROSH] Memorise the factor VIII dose formula: weight in kg × 0.5 × the desired percentage rise. Life-threatening or CNS bleeding needs 50 units/kg to reach about 100% activity; haemarthrosis needs only 40–50%. Haemophilia B bleeding needs 100 IU/kg of factor IX. [PEER-PH][ROSH] Never give an intramuscular injection to a haemophilic patient with acute bleeding, and replete factor before measuring compartment pressures — the measurement itself can worsen the bleed. [PEER-PH]
- Distinguish von Willebrand disease from haemophilia by the bleeding pattern and the treatment: mucosal and skin bleeding predominates, desmopressin covers minor bleeding in types 1 and 2, and only type 3 — the absolute absence of von Willebrand factor — needs factor VIII and von Willebrand factor concentrate at 25–50 units/kg. Desmopressin does not work in type 3, and aspirin is avoided in all types. [PEER-PH][ROSH] von Willebrand disease prolongs the PTT through the fall in its carrier-bound factor VIII; a prolonged PTT with mucocutaneous bleeding and a positive family history is the classic pairing. [PEER-PH]
- DIC is never the diagnosis — it is a complication. Treat the underlying illness and the cascade stops; nothing else does. Infection is the most common trigger. Support with cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate when the patient is bleeding with a low fibrinogen. [PEER-PH][ROSH] DIC versus TTP turns on the coagulation screen: DIC prolongs PT and aPTT and drops fibrinogen, while TTP leaves PT, aPTT and fibrinogen normal with schistocytes and severe thrombocytopenia. Both share schistocytes on smear. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Do not wait for the full pentad to treat TTP. Microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia plus thrombocytopenia with no other explanation is enough; plasma exchange is the most effective therapy, untreated mortality approaches 100%, and haematology should be consulted emergently. Fever, renal impairment and neurological change are each present in only a minority. [PEER-PH][ROSH] Treating a platelet count of 7,000/µL with new confusion as a platelet problem is the trap: the emergency is intracranial haemorrhage, and the answer is an emergent noncontrast head CT. Spontaneous bleeding risk climbs below 20,000/µL and especially below 10,000/µL. [PEER-PH]
- Paediatric ITP after a viral illness is usually self-limited and needs only acetaminophen; adult ITP is more often chronic and starts with corticosteroids, reserving intravenous immunoglobulin for very low counts or active severe bleeding. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- In sickle cell disease the reticulocyte count separates the two abrupt anaemias: it is suppressed in aplastic crisis, most often from parvovirus B19 after the slapped-cheek rash, and elevated in splenic sequestration. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Acute chest syndrome is the leading cause of death in sickle cell disease, and infection — most often *Streptococcus pneumoniae* — is its most common precipitant. Treat with oxygen, intravenous normal saline, intravenous antibiotics, analgesia and transfusion to a haemoglobin above 10 g/dL. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- A child with sickle cell disease and an acute stroke needs exchange transfusion, targeting haemoglobin S below 30% and total haemoglobin 10 g/dL. Tissue plasminogen activator is neither indicated nor approved in children. Haemorrhagic stroke and subarachnoid haemorrhage, by contrast, are diseases of adults with sickle cell disease. [PEER-PH]
- Meperidine is out for vaso-occlusive pain — its metabolite normeperidine causes anxiety, clonus and seizures. Assess pain by what the patient or parent reports, not by objective data. Ischaemic priapism is managed with intravenous analgesia first, before any urological intervention. [PEER-PH]
- Fever may be the only sign of a life-threatening bacterial infection in a neutropenic patient: single oral temperature ≥38.3°C, or ≥38°C for over an hour, with an ANC below 1,000–1,500/µL. Empiric therapy must cover *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* — cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem or imipenem-cilastatin — with cultures sent and admission arranged. The nadir falls 7–14 days after the last chemotherapy infusion. [PEER-PH][ROSH] In a neutropenic patient with fever and right lower quadrant pain, especially with blood in the stool, think neutropenic enterocolitis (typhlitis) and obtain computed tomography. [PEER-PH]
- Tumour lysis syndrome is a phosphate problem before it is a uric acid problem: hyperphosphataemia drives the hypocalcaemia, and uric acid and calcium phosphate precipitation cause the kidney injury that then worsens the hyperkalaemia. Anticipate it in acute leukaemias, high-grade non-Hodgkin lymphoma and Burkitt lymphoma, and treat with aggressive fluids and emergent dialysis. [PEER-PH][ROSH] Hyperleukocytosis above 100,000/µL is most dangerous in acute myeloid leukaemia, where rigid myeloblasts sludge in the pulmonary and cerebral microcirculation. Symptoms may be subtle — altered mental status, fatigue, headache, blurred vision — and leukostasis carries a mortality rate up to 40%. Treat with intravenous fluids plus leukapheresis or induction chemotherapy. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- For hypercalcaemia of malignancy, resuscitate with normal saline first and then give a bisphosphonate such as zoledronic acid or pamidronate; furosemide is no longer recommended for routine use. In myeloma, hypercalcaemia masquerades as depression, lethargy, nausea and confusion. [PEER-PH]
- Polycythaemia vera announces itself with pruritus after a warm shower, erythromelalgia — burning hands and feet with dusky red discoloration — and headache. Treat with acetylsalicylic acid 81 mg and phlebotomy to a haematocrit below 45%, with saline volume replacement. A raised haematocrit from dehydration is a relative polycythaemia: resuscitate and repeat the count before pursuing a myeloproliferative diagnosis. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Know the safe antibiotic in G6PD deficiency: cephalexin. Avoid nitrofurantoin, ciprofloxacin, phenazopyridine, dapsone, primaquine and sulfonamides. In alpha-thalassaemia intermedia avoid nitrofurantoin, sulfonamides, dapsone, primaquine and chloroquine. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- In a malnourished patient, folate deficiency develops before vitamin B12 deficiency because folate stores last only 3–4 months while hepatic B12 stores last 3–5 years — and only B12 deficiency brings the neuropathy, gait instability and cognitive change. [ROSH]
- Two findings that redirect the whole workup: hepatosplenomegaly with painless lymphadenopathy and B symptoms in a teenager means Hodgkin lymphoma with Reed-Sternberg cells on biopsy, and facial or neck swelling that is worst on waking means superior vena cava syndrome, which is malignant in the great majority of cases. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Advanced liver disease is not simply a bleeding state: protein C and antithrombin fall with the procoagulant factors, so these patients are also at increased risk of deep vein thrombosis. [PEER-PH]

## مراجعة عربية مركزة

تُبنى هذه المراجعة على ثلاثة مبادئ عملية: إيقاف النزف، وتصحيح السبب، وعدم تأخير العلاج النوعي انتظاراً للتحاليل.

تفاعلات نقل الدم: القاعدة الأولى في أي تفاعل مشتبه به هي إيقاف نقل الدم فوراً وإبقاء الوريد مفتوحاً بمحلول normal saline. الحمى وارتفاع الحرارة أثناء النقل تُعالَج كتفاعل انحلالي حاد حتى يُستبعد، وغالباً يكون الخطأ بشرياً في تحديد هوية المريض أو الوحدة. التفاعل التحسسي البسيط (allergic transfusion reaction) يسبب حكة وشرى وعلاجه diphenhydramine مع إمكانية إكمال النقل، أما الوذمة الوعائية مع ضيق النفس فتشير إلى عوز IgA. يجب التمييز بين إصابة الرئة الحادة المرتبطة بنقل الدم (TRALI) وفرط الحجم الدوراني المرتبط بنقل الدم (TACO): فالأول يترافق مع حمى وانخفاض ضغط ووذمة رئوية غير قلبية خلال 6 ساعات، والثاني مع ارتفاع ضغط ووذمة رئوية بسبب فرط الحجم. نقل الدم بكميات كبيرة يسبب نقص كالسيوم بسبب مادة citrate، وعلاماته تنميل حول الفم وعلامة Chvostek وتطاول QTc، وعلاجه إعطاء الكالسيوم وريدياً.

اضطرابات النزف: الهيموفيليا A نقص العامل VIII والهيموفيليا B نقص العامل IX، وكلتاهما مع PT طبيعي وaPTT مطاول. في إصابة الرأس يُعطى العامل قبل التصوير المقطعي وليس بعده، بجرعة 50 unit/kg للهيموفيليا A و100 IU/kg للهيموفيليا B. ويُحسب العامل الثامن بالمعادلة: الوزن × 0.5 × النسبة المطلوبة. يجب تجنب الحقن العضلي، ومتلازمة الحيز (compartment syndrome) من مضاعفات النزف العضلي. مرض فون ويلبراند (von Willebrand disease) هو أشهر اضطراب نزفي وراثي؛ يُعالج بالـ desmopressin في النزف البسيط، أما النوع الثالث فيحتاج مركزات العامل VIII وعامل فون ويلبراند، ويُمنع الأسبرين.

التخثر المنتشر داخل الأوعية (DIC): حالة ثانوية أشيع أسبابها العدوى، والعلاج الوحيد الذي يوقف تقدمها هو علاج المرض الأساسي، مع cryoprecipitate عند وجود نزف وانخفاض الفيبرينوجين. فرفرية نقص الصفيحات الخثارية (TTP) تُشخَّص بفقر دم انحلالي مجهري مع شهادة schistocytes وقلة صفيحات مع تخثر طبيعي، وعلاجها تبادل البلازما (plasmapheresis) بشكل عاجل لأن الوفاة تقارب 100% دون علاج.

فقر الدم المنجلي (sickle cell disease): متلازمة الصدر الحادة هي السبب الأول للوفاة، وعلاجها الأكسجين والسوائل الوريدية والمضادات الحيوية ونقل الدم بهدف hemoglobin أعلى من 10 g/dL. عدد الشبكيات يفصل بين الأزمة اللاتنسجية (منخفض، وسببها parvovirus B19) واحتجاز الطحال (مرتفع). الجلطة الإقفارية عند الأطفال تُعالج بتبادل الدم وليس بـ tPA، أما الجلطة النزفية فأشيع عند البالغين. ويُمنع meperidine في أزمات الألم.

حالات الطوارئ الأخرى: حمى نقص العدلات تُعرَّف بحرارة ≥38.3°C وعدلات مطلقة أقل من 1000–1500 مع تغطية Pseudomonas بـ cefepime؛ ومتلازمة انحلال الورم (tumor lysis syndrome) تُعالج بالسوائل المكثفة والحوار (dialysis)؛ وفرط كالسيوم الخباثة يُعالج بالسوائل ثم zoledronic acid؛ وفرط الكريات الحمر يُعالج بالأسبرين 81 mg والفصد بهدف hematocrit أقل من 45%. ويُمنع nitrofurantoin في عوز G6PD وفي الثلاسيميا.
