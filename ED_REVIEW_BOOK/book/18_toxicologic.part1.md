## High-Yield Exam Review

- Six toxidromes organize this chapter: anticholinergic, sympathomimetic, cholinergic, opioid, serotonin syndrome, and neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS).
- Anticholinergic toxidrome: warm, dry, flushed skin; dry mucous membranes; mydriasis; tachycardia; hyperthermia; urinary retention; reduced bowel sounds; agitation and hallucinations. The mechanism is inhibition of muscarinic acetylcholine receptors. [ROSH]
- Sympathomimetic toxidrome: wet, warm, flushed skin; dilated reactive pupils; tachycardia; hypertension; tachypnea; hyperthermia; increased bowel sounds; agitation.
- Skin is the discriminator between anticholinergic (dry) and sympathomimetic (wet) toxicity; bowel sounds are present in cocaine toxicity and reduced in anticholinergic toxicity. [ROSH]
- Cholinergic toxidrome (SLUDGE, DUMBBELS): salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhea, gastrointestinal cramps, emesis, miosis, bronchorrhea, bronchospasm, bradycardia, fasciculations, weakness and paralysis.
- Cholinergic crisis follows acetylcholinesterase inhibition: organophosphate and carbamate insecticides, nerve agents (sarin, VX, soman, tabun), and the acetylcholinesterase inhibitor rivastigmine. [ROSH]
- Atropine is the mainstay of organophosphate poisoning and reverses the muscarinic effects; pralidoxime prevents aging of the inhibited enzyme and does not take priority over atropine. The clinical end point of atropine is attenuation of bronchial secretions. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Physostigmine is contraindicated in organophosphate poisoning and in anticholinergic toxicity with QRS widening (> 100 ms). [PEER-PH]
- Opioid toxidrome: altered mental status, respiratory depression, miosis, diminished bowel sounds, bradycardia, hypotension and hyporeflexia. The goal of naloxone is adequate respiratory function, not normal mental status. [ROSH]
- Clonidine produces an opioid-like toxidrome through central alpha-2 agonism; naloxone is less likely to reverse it in adults but has been used with positive effect in clonidine-poisoned children. [ROSH]
- Serotonin syndrome: clonus is the most specific and most common finding, with hyperreflexia greater in the lower extremities, myoclonus, hyperthermia, autonomic instability and altered mental status. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Serotonin syndrome is treated with benzodiazepines, hydration and cooling; cyproheptadine 12 mg orally is the classic antidote in moderate to severe cases. [ROSH]
- NMS: hyperthermia, lead-pipe rigidity, autonomic instability, altered mental status and hyporeflexia; clonus is absent, which separates it from serotonin syndrome. Management is to stop the offending agent, give benzodiazepines such as lorazepam, cool aggressively and give fluids. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Ethylene glycol: high anion gap metabolic acidosis without significant lactate or ketone elevation, osmolar gap, acute kidney injury and calcium oxalate crystals in urine. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Ethylene glycol produces a lactate gap: glycolate is misread as lactate by blood gas analyzers, while serum lactate is unaffected. [PEER-PH]
- Methanol: formic acid accumulation with anion gap metabolic acidosis, optic disc hyperemia and retinal edema. [ROSH]
- Isopropyl alcohol: ketosis without acidosis, an osmolar gap with a normal anion gap. Treatment is supportive with intravenous hydration, thiamine and folate; fomepizole has no role. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Acetaminophen: N-acetyl-p-benzoquinoneimine (NAPQI) is the toxic metabolite; N-acetylcysteine regenerates depleted hepatic glutathione as a sulfhydryl donor; the most common adverse effect is an anaphylactoid reaction. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Acetaminophen risk is predicted from a 4-hour level plotted on the Rumack-Matthew nomogram, which applies only to acute single ingestions; the toxic dose is > 150 mg/kg. [ROSH]
- Salicylate toxicity: hyperpnea, gastrointestinal upset and tinnitus. Respiratory alkalosis appears earliest, followed by anion gap metabolic acidosis; the target urine pH is 7.5–8.0. [ROSH]
- Carbon monoxide poisoning is the leading cause of poisoning morbidity and mortality. Pulse oximetry is normal, so co-oximetry is required; carboxyhemoglobin half-life falls from 5 hours on room air to about 60 minutes on high-flow oxygen by nonrebreather mask. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Hyperbaric oxygen reduces delayed neurologic sequelae after carbon monoxide poisoning; seizures and syncope are indications. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Tricyclic antidepressant toxicity is sodium channel blockade: QRS > 100 ms, terminal R wave in lead aVR, and the three Cs of coma, convulsions and cardiac dysrhythmia. Sodium bicarbonate is first-line. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Calcium channel blocker overdose is hypotension, bradycardia and hyperglycemia; beta-blocker overdose is distinguished by hypoglycemia. High-dose insulin is first-line for severe calcium channel blocker overdose. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Sulfonylurea exposure: a single 5 mg glipizide tablet can be life-threatening in a child. Dextrose and octreotide are the treatment, and 24-hour observation is required. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Digoxin toxicity: scooped ST segments, PR prolongation, hyperkalemia and bidirectional ventricular tachycardia. Digoxin-specific antibody fragments are indicated for bidirectional ventricular tachycardia and for symptomatic bradyarrhythmia such as lightheadedness and syncope. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Isoniazid overdose causes seizures refractory to conventional therapy; pyridoxine is the antidote. [ROSH]
- Methemoglobinemia: cyanosis with a pulse oximetry reading around 85% that does not respond to oxygen; methylene blue is the antidote. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Cyanide poisoning: lactic acidosis, high venous oxygen content, bitter almond odor and smoke inhalation; hydroxocobalamin is first-line. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Iron poisoning is staged; vomiting and its frequency are the key markers of toxicity, and deferoxamine is the chelator. Valproic acid toxicity most frequently causes central nervous system depression with hyperammonemia, treated with L-carnitine. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Lithium toxicity causes gastrointestinal, neurological and cardiac effects; arginine vasopressin resistance complicates chronic toxicity, and hemodialysis is indicated with altered mental status plus a level of 3 mEq/L. [ROSH]

## Core Concepts

### The toxidrome method

- A toxidrome is a constellation of vital-sign, pupillary, skin, bowel-sound and neuromuscular findings that narrows the exposure before any laboratory result is available.
- Five bedside variables carry most of the discrimination: mental status, pupil size and reactivity, skin moisture, heart rate and blood pressure, and bowel sounds.
- Toxidromes overlap. Cocaine and anticholinergic agents both give agitation, mydriasis, tachycardia and hyperthermia; skin moisture and bowel sounds separate them. [ROSH]
- Serotonin syndrome and NMS both give hyperthermia, rigidity, altered mental status and autonomic instability; the neuromuscular examination separates them.
- Diagnose hallucinogen and sympathomimetic exposure clinically. Urine testing supports some agents but the mainstay of diagnosis is the toxidrome. [ROSH]

### Toxidrome comparison

| Toxidrome | Mental status | Pupils | Skin | Heart rate | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anticholinergic | Delirium, hallucinations | Mydriasis | Dry, warm, flushed | Tachycardia | Urinary retention, reduced bowel sounds |
| Sympathomimetic | Agitation, psychosis | Mydriasis, reactive | Wet, warm, flushed | Tachycardia | Increased bowel sounds, hypertension |
| Cholinergic | Confusion, coma | Miosis | Diaphoretic | Bradycardia | Bronchorrhea, fasciculations, lacrimation |
| Opioid | Depressed, coma | Miosis | Normal | Bradycardia | Respiratory depression, absent bowel sounds |
| Serotonin | Agitated, altered | Mydriasis | Diaphoretic | Tachycardia | Clonus, lower-extremity hyperreflexia |
| NMS | Stupor, altered | Normal | Diaphoretic | Tachycardia | Lead-pipe rigidity, hyporeflexia |

### Antidote logic

- Some antidotes reverse a receptor effect: naloxone at opioid receptors, flumazenil at the benzodiazepine site on the gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) receptor, atropine at muscarinic receptors, glucagon bypassing blocked beta-1 receptors. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Some antidotes restore a depleted substrate or cofactor: N-acetylcysteine restores glutathione, pyridoxine restores the cofactor for GABA synthesis, L-carnitine restores the urea cycle substrate in valproate toxicity, thiamine and pyridoxine divert ethylene glycol metabolites. [ROSH]
- Some antidotes bind the toxin directly: digoxin-specific antibody fragments, hydroxocobalamin for cyanide, deferoxamine for iron, idarucizumab for dabigatran, protamine for heparin. [ROSH]
- Some treatments work by mass action or displacement rather than binding: sodium bicarbonate for sodium channel blockade, high-dose insulin for calcium channel blocker and beta-blocker poisoning, lipid emulsion for local anesthetic systemic toxicity. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Physostigmine and flumazenil are the two antidotes whose contraindications matter as much as their indications. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Decontamination principles

- Activated charcoal is appropriate for acetylsalicylic acid, for verapamil, and for ibuprofen overdose; it is most useful within about an hour of ingestion and requires an alert patient who can protect the airway. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Charcoal is avoided with alcohols (rapid absorption), metals (poor binding) and caustics (contraindicated). [PEER-PH]
- Aspiration of charcoal can cause life-threatening pneumonitis; patients should not be intubated solely to receive it. [ROSH]
- For a nerve agent, decontamination — removing clothing and washing with soap and water — precedes entry into the health care facility, because nerve agents persist on skin and clothing. [PEER-PH]
- In a cholinergic crisis, staff don personal protective equipment and the patient's clothes are removed and rinsed to avoid further exposure. [ROSH]
- The first step in any pediatric ingestion, however benign the product, is to establish the time, the substance and the amount ingested. [PEER-PH]

### Anticoagulant reversal as toxicology

- Warfarin inhibits vitamin K recycling and limits factors II, VII, IX and X; its effect is measured by prothrombin time or international normalized ratio (INR). Life-threatening bleeding is always reversed. [ROSH]
- Dabigatran is a direct thrombin inhibitor that prevents conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin; idarucizumab is its monoclonal antibody fragment reversal agent. [ROSH]
- Factor Xa inhibitors such as apixaban and rivaroxaban prevent conversion of prothrombin to thrombin; hemorrhage is the most concerning adverse effect and half-lives are long, so reported overdose is admitted for at least 24 hours. [ROSH]
- Enzyme inducers such as carbamazepine increase metabolism of direct thrombin inhibitors; diltiazem raises apixaban concentration; amiodarone enhances warfarin anticoagulation. [ROSH]
- Heparin solutions intended for intravenous therapy are far more concentrated than flush solutions; heparin must not be used to flush catheter locks or intravenous ports. [ROSH]
