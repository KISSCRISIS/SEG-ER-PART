## Diagnosis

### Transfusion reactions — pattern recognition

| Findings | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Fever, rigors, low back pain, hypotension ~30 min in | Acute haemolytic reaction [PEER-PH] |
| Mild fever only, within 4 hours | Febrile nonhaemolytic reaction [ROSH] |
| Angioedema, hives, respiratory distress, shock | Anaphylaxis — consider IgA deficiency [ROSH] |
| Hypoxaemia, bilateral infiltrates, normal heart size, fever, hypotension, within 6 hours | TRALI [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Dyspnoea, hypoxia, hypertension, pulmonary oedema after large-volume transfusion | TACO [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Circumoral numbness, cramps, Chvostek sign, QTc prolongation after massive transfusion | Citrate hypocalcaemia [PEER-PH][ROSH] |

- Any fever during transfusion triggers the workup: recheck the product and patient identification, because most acute haemolytic reactions follow human error. [PEER-PH]
- Positive direct antiglobulin testing with fever means antibodies bound to incompatible donor red cells; expect haemoglobinuria, DIC and kidney failure. [ROSH]
- Fever above 38°C separates TRALI from TACO, which is marked by hypertension and volume overload. [ROSH]

### Bleeding disorders — laboratory pattern

| Pattern | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Prolonged aPTT, normal PT, low factor VIII | Haemophilia A |
| Prolonged aPTT, normal PT, low factor IX | Haemophilia B [ROSH] |
| Prolonged aPTT from low factor VIII plus mucocutaneous bleeding | von Willebrand disease [PEER-PH] |
| Low platelets, low fibrinogen, prolonged PT and aPTT, high D-dimer | DIC [ROSH] |
| Low platelets, schistocytes, normal PT, aPTT and fibrinogen | TTP [PEER-PH] |

- Severity follows residual activity: below 1% presents within the first year of life, while 5–40% may go years before a significant bleed. [ROSH]

### Thrombocytopenia and microangiopathy

- Spontaneous bleeding risk rises below 20,000/µL and markedly below 10,000/µL; altered mental status at these counts needs emergent noncontrast head CT. [PEER-PH]
- HUS follows *E. coli* bloody diarrhoea by 5–10 days in a child; TTP in pregnancy mimics HELLP syndrome, which is uncommon before 24 weeks. [PEER-PH]

### Sickle cell disease

| Finding | Complication |
|---|---|
| New infiltrate plus fever, cough, chest pain, wheeze or tachypnoea | Acute chest syndrome [ROSH] |
| Abrupt haemoglobin fall with reticulocytopenia | Aplastic crisis [PEER-PH] |
| Abrupt haemoglobin fall with high reticulocyte count | Splenic sequestration [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Hand and foot swelling, pain, erythema at 6–24 months | Dactylitis [PEER-PH] |
| Persistent hip or thigh pain, worse with walking, limited internal rotation | Avascular necrosis [PEER-PH] |
| Hemiparesis, focal seizure, gait or speech disturbance in a child | Stroke — usually embolic in children [PEER-PH] |

- Assess vaso-occlusive pain by the patient's or parent's report; objective data should not minimise it. [PEER-PH]
- A reticulocyte count below 3%, usually below 1%, defines aplastic crisis; parvovirus B19 is the commonest cause, preceded by the slapped-cheek rash of erythema infectiosum. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Acute chest syndrome is a new infiltrate with fever above 38.5°C, cough, wheeze, tachypnoea or chest pain; *Streptococcus pneumoniae* is the organism most often implicated. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Anaemia — size-based classification

| Mean corpuscular volume | Causes |
|---|---|
| Low, with low ferritin and low iron | Iron deficiency |
| Low, with normal ferritin, normal-to-high iron, normal reticulocytes, target cells | Thalassaemia [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Normal, with low haptoglobin and high reticulocytes | Haemolytic anaemia |
| Above 100 fL, with hypersegmented neutrophils | Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency [ROSH] |
| Pancytopenia with low reticulocytes | Aplastic anaemia [PEER-PH] |

- New anaemia in an adult requires assessment for occult malignancy or gastrointestinal bleeding; send total iron binding capacity, ferritin and serum iron. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Haemolytic anaemia with a murmur, syncope and exertional chest pain suggests valve-related shear haemolysis such as aortic stenosis. [PEER-PH]

### Oxidative haemolysis and methaemoglobinaemia

- G6PD deficiency: jaundice, pallor, dark urine and fatigue 2–4 days after an oxidant trigger, with raised lactate dehydrogenase and reticulocytes, Heinz bodies, low haptoglobin and a negative Coombs test. [ROSH]
- Triggers include primaquine, dapsone, rasburicase, nitrofurantoin, fava beans, henna tattoos and infection of any cause; thalassaemia shows target cells rather than Heinz bodies. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Methaemoglobinaemia: cyanosis with saturation near 85% that does not correct with oxygen; co-oximetry showing methaemoglobin above 2% confirms it while the arterial partial pressure of oxygen remains normal. Benzocaine, antimalarials, nitrates and aromatic benzenes are acquired causes. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Oncological diagnosis

| Finding | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Lymphadenopathy, fevers, night sweats, weight loss, high lactate dehydrogenase | Lymphoma [ROSH] |
| Painless cervical or supraclavicular mass, mediastinal widening, Reed-Sternberg cells | Hodgkin lymphoma [ROSH] |
| Rapidly enlarging painless jaw or facial mass in an endemic region | Burkitt lymphoma [PEER-PH] |
| Anaemia, back or rib pain, lytic lesions, hypercalcaemia, Bence Jones proteinuria | Multiple myeloma [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Pancytopenia with blasts and hepatosplenomegaly | Leukaemia [ROSH] |

- Leukaemia is the most common childhood cancer; fever, petechiae, adenopathy and organomegaly together make it the leading paediatric diagnosis. [PEER-PH]
- A rapidly growing jaw and orbital mass with facial numbness suggests endemic Burkitt lymphoma, linked to Epstein-Barr virus; IgG accounts for about 60% of monoclonal immunoglobulins in myeloma. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

## Management

### Acute transfusion reaction

1. Stop the transfusion; keep the line open with normal saline. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
2. Recheck the product and patient identification. [PEER-PH]
3. Assess for hypotension and respiratory distress; escalate to intubation and vasopressors as indicated. [PEER-PH]
4. Pruritus or urticaria only — diphenhydramine, and the transfusion may continue; febrile nonhaemolytic reaction — acetaminophen. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
5. TRALI — supportive care with oxygenation, often mechanical ventilation; anaphylaxis with angioedema — treat as anaphylaxis, with IgA deficiency the implicated mechanism. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Massive transfusion

- Threshold: ≥10 units of packed red cells in 24 hours, half the blood volume replaced within 3 hours, or loss above 150 mL/min; reserve it for haemorrhagic shock. [ROSH]
- Use a 1:1:1 ratio of platelets, red cells and fresh frozen plasma; the closer the product resembles whole blood, the better the outcome. [ROSH]
- Give warmed blood when hypothermia is driving coagulopathy, and intravenous calcium for citrate-induced hypocalcaemia. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- One unit of packed red cells raises haemoglobin by about 1 g/dL and haematocrit by 3%. [ROSH]

### Bleeding disorders — replacement and reversal

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Haemophilia A, life-threatening or CNS bleed | 50 units/kg factor VIII to about 100% activity, before imaging [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Haemophilia B, head injury | 100 IU/kg factor IX [ROSH] |
| von Willebrand disease, minor bleeding | Desmopressin [ROSH] |
| von Willebrand disease type 3 or major bleeding | Factor VIII and von Willebrand factor concentrate 25–50 units/kg [PEER-PH] |
| Warfarin with intracranial haemorrhage | Four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate plus vitamin K, goal INR <1.4 [ROSH] |
| Dabigatran with persistent bleeding | Idarucizumab [ROSH] |
| Apixaban with intracranial haemorrhage | Four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate [ROSH] |
| DIC with bleeding and hypofibrinogenaemia | Cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate with supportive care [PEER-PH][ROSH] |

- Head injury in haemophilia is treated before imaging; factor must not be delayed for CT. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Avoid intramuscular injections; replete factor before measuring compartment pressures. Aspirin is avoided in von Willebrand disease, and desmopressin is ineffective in type 3. [PEER-PH]

### DIC, TTP and ITP

- DIC: treat the underlying illness — the only intervention that halts the cascade. [PEER-PH]
- TTP: consult haematology emergently, admit to critical care, and begin plasma exchange without delay; untreated mortality approaches 100%. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Paediatric post-viral ITP: supportive care with acetaminophen, as most cases in children aged 2–6 years resolve within 6 months. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Adult ITP: corticosteroids first, with intravenous immunoglobulin reserved for very low counts or active severe bleeding. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Sickle cell disease

1. Vaso-occlusive pain — intravenous analgesia guided by patient or parent report; avoid meperidine, whose metabolite normeperidine causes anxiety, clonus and seizures. [PEER-PH]
2. Acute chest syndrome — oxygen, intravenous normal saline, intravenous antibiotics, analgesia, and transfusion to a haemoglobin above 10 g/dL. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
3. Acute ischaemic stroke in a child — exchange transfusion targeting haemoglobin S below 30% and total haemoglobin 10 g/dL; tissue plasminogen activator is neither indicated nor approved in children. [PEER-PH]
4. Ischaemic priapism — intravenous analgesia first, then urology and haematology consultation. [PEER-PH]
5. Admit for suspected acute chest syndrome, serious infection, white cells above 30,000/mm3, platelets below 100,000/mm3, or refractory pain. [PEER-PH]

### Febrile neutropenia

1. Recognise: single oral temperature ≥38.3°C, or ≥38°C for over an hour, with an absolute neutrophil count below 1,000–1,500/µL; severe neutropenia is below 500/µL. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
2. Anticipate the nadir 7–14 days after the last chemotherapy infusion. [ROSH]
3. Send blood and urine cultures and start empiric antibiotics covering *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* — cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem or imipenem-cilastatin — adding vancomycin in selected patients. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
4. Suspect neutropenic enterocolitis (typhlitis) with fever and right lower quadrant pain, especially bloody stool, and obtain computed tomography. [PEER-PH]

### Tumour lysis and hyperleukocytosis

- Anticipate tumour lysis in bulky chemosensitive disease — acute leukaemias, high-grade non-Hodgkin lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma — hours to days after starting therapy. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Look for hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hyperuricaemia and secondary hypocalcaemia; treat with aggressive intravenous fluids and emergent dialysis, giving calcium gluconate for the hyperkalaemia and hypocalcaemia. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Suspect leukostasis with a white cell count above 100,000/µL plus hypoxia, respiratory distress, altered mental status, headache, blurred vision or stroke-like symptoms. [ROSH]
- Acute myeloid leukaemia carries the highest risk; treat with intravenous fluids plus emergent leukapheresis or induction chemotherapy, with oncology involved immediately. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Hypercalcaemia, hyperviscosity and superior vena cava syndrome

- Hypercalcaemia of malignancy: normal saline first, then a bisphosphonate such as zoledronic acid or pamidronate; calcitonin and steroids are also useful, and furosemide is no longer recommended for routine use. [PEER-PH]
- Hypercalcaemia in myeloma causes nausea, anorexia, lethargy, confusion, depression and somnolence. [PEER-PH]
- Hyperviscosity from polycythaemia: acetylsalicylic acid 81 mg, phlebotomy targeting a haematocrit below 45%, saline volume replacement and haematology consultation. [PEER-PH]
- Macrophage activation syndrome with cytopenia: decreased fibrinogen and a low erythrocyte sedimentation rate support the diagnosis. [ROSH]

### Anticoagulation-related bleeding

- Stable gastrointestinal bleeding on apixaban: hold the anticoagulant and consult gastroenterology for localisation and possible endoscopy. [ROSH]
- Epistaxis on a factor Xa inhibitor: direct pressure with oxymetazoline or phenylephrine; if bleeding continues and the source is seen, cauterise with silver nitrate; if still uncontrolled, pack with a thrombogenic material or insert a nasal tampon. [ROSH]
- Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia: stop heparin and start a non-heparin anticoagulant — argatroban, or bivalirudin when a cold, pulseless limb indicates arterial thrombosis. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Closed head injury on warfarin with a negative initial CT: obtain and document the INR to stratify delayed intracranial haemorrhage risk. [PEER-PH]
