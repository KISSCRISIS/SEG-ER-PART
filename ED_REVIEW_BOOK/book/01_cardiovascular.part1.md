## High-Yield Exam Review

- Chest compressions are the single most important intervention in cardiac arrest and the strongest prehospital determinant of survival. [ROSH]
- EtCO2 reflects perfusion during CPR: near 20 mm Hg is good, keep it above 10 mm Hg, and a falling value suggests compressor fatigue. [PEER-PH]
- Ventricular fibrillation (VF) and pulseless ventricular tachycardia (VT) are shockable: defibrillate early, prioritising compressions and shocks over advanced airway placement. [PEER-PH]
- Paediatric defibrillation: 2 J/kg then 4 J/kg. Paediatric synchronised cardioversion: 0.5–1 J/kg. [PEER-PH]
- Refractory VF/pulseless VT after three shocks: change pad vector or use dual sequential defibrillation; esmolol 0.5 mg/kg bolus may improve ROSC. [PEER-PH]
- Pseudo-pulseless electrical activity is profound shock with an impalpable pulse: treat with pressors and fluids, not compressions. Point-of-care ultrasound separates it from true PEA. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- PEA arrest in end-stage renal disease with sine waves is hyperkalaemic: calcium chloride, insulin and glucose. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Targeted temperature management after ROSC: 32–37.5 °C for at least 72 hours; active non-compressible bleeding is a relative contraindication. [ROSH]
- Torsades de pointes is treated with magnesium sulfate; polymorphic VT with a normal QT suggests ischaemia. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Adenosine for stable narrow-complex supraventricular tachycardia (SVT): 6 mg then 12 mg; contraindicated in antidromic AVRT and pre-excited atrial fibrillation. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Stable wide-complex tachycardia: procainamide 17 mg/kg over 30–45 minutes. Unstable: synchronised cardioversion. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Synchronised cardioversion: 50–100 J for SVT; atrial flutter often responds to 25–50 J. [PEER-PH]
- Atrial fibrillation rate control: diltiazem in chronic lung disease, beta-blocker after recent infarction or in systolic heart failure. [PEER-PH]
- Aortic dissection: pain in 95%, chest pain in 72.7%, back pain in 53.2%. A beta-blocker first, targeting heart rate below 60 bpm and systolic pressure 100–120 mm Hg; fibrinolytics are contraindicated. [PEER-PH]
- Type A dissection requires emergent surgery; type B may be endovascular. Transoesophageal echocardiography is the test of choice when contrast is contraindicated. [PEER-PH]
- Abdominal aortic aneurysm is a diameter above 3 cm; above 5.5 cm is the strongest rupture risk factor, and untreated rupture is fatal within 1 week. [PEER-PH]
- Most abdominal aortic aneurysms are asymptomatic with a pulsatile abdominal mass; bedside ultrasound is fastest when unstable, CT angiography most accurate. [ROSH]
- Acute limb ischaemia: atherosclerosis is the commonest thrombotic cause, atrial fibrillation the commonest embolic source; sensory loss, paralysis and absent Doppler signals mean irreversible ischaemia. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- In acute coronary syndrome, aspirin has the greatest mortality benefit and troponin is the most specific biomarker. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- STEMI with a door-to-balloon time of 90 minutes should be transferred immediately for primary percutaneous coronary intervention. [PEER-PH]
- Left bundle-branch block: concordant ST elevation ≥1 mm, concordant ST depression ≥1 mm in V1–V3, or discordant ST elevation ≥1 mm that is 30% of the preceding S wave identifies occlusion myocardial infarction. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- de Winter T waves are a STEMI equivalent from proximal left anterior descending (LAD) occlusion; Wellens syndrome is highly specific for critical LAD stenosis. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Right coronary artery occlusion causes inferior infarction and can produce transient complete heart block with a narrow QRS; atropine is first-line. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Aortic stenosis: systolic crescendo–decrescendo murmur radiating to the carotids; avoid nitroglycerin; congestive heart failure carries the poorest prognosis. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy murmur increases with Valsalva and standing and decreases with squatting and hand grip. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Cardiac tamponade: right ventricular diastolic collapse is the most specific sign, right atrial collapse the earliest. [ROSH]
- Acute pericarditis is treated with a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug plus colchicine; fever above 38 °C is an indication for admission. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Infective endocarditis: Staphylococcus aureus is the commonest organism, the tricuspid valve the commonest in intravenous drug use, and congestive heart failure the commonest complication. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

## Core Concepts

### Acute Coronary Syndromes

- The spectrum is unstable angina, NSTEMI and STEMI; unstable angina and NSTEMI are separated by troponin, which is normal in unstable angina. [ROSH]
- Risk factors: male sex, family history, increasing age, hyperlipidaemia, hypertension, diabetes, tobacco and cocaine use. [PEER-PH]
- Anginal equivalents: shoulder, arm or jaw discomfort, dyspnoea and epigastric pain. [PEER-PH]
- The HEART score (history, ECG, age, risk factors, troponin) allows discharge at ≤3 with two negative troponins; obesity is a clinical risk factor. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- High-sensitivity troponin improves sensitivity and negative predictive value; coronary CT angiography shortens hospital stay in acute chest pain. [ROSH]

### ECG Patterns and Territory

| ECG finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| ST elevation in II, III, aVF | Inferior infarction — right coronary artery |
| ST elevation in V5–V6 with inferior changes | Left circumflex occlusion |
| ST elevation in V2–V4 | Anterior infarction — LAD |
| ST depression in V1–V3, tall R waves, upright T waves | Posterior infarction — posterior descending artery |
| Upsloping precordial ST depression, tall symmetric T waves, ST elevation in aVR | de Winter — proximal LAD occlusion |
| Deep inverted or biphasic T waves in V2–V3, pain free | Wellens syndrome — critical LAD stenosis |
| Isolated 1 mm ST elevation in aVR with V5–V6 depression | Subendocardial ischaemia — left main or multivessel disease |
| Diffuse ST elevation with PR depression, no reciprocal change | Pericarditis, Dressler syndrome |
| Persistent ST elevation with Q waves weeks after anterior STEMI | Left ventricular aneurysm |
| Electrical alternans, low-voltage QRS | Pericardial effusion, tamponade |
| Broad notched R in I, aVL, V5–V6, late S in V1, QRS >120 ms | Left bundle-branch block |
| RSR′ in V1–V2 with T-wave inversion, QRS >120 ms | Right bundle-branch block |
| Left axis deviation with a narrow QRS | Left anterior fascicular block |

- Hyperacute T waves are the earliest ECG change in acute ST elevation myocardial infarction. [ROSH]

### Bradyarrhythmias and Conduction Blocks

- First-degree AV block: PR interval ≥200 ms with every impulse conducted. [PEER-PH]
- Mobitz type I: progressive PR prolongation before a dropped QRS; transient, and seen after inferior infarction, digoxin toxicity, myocarditis or cardiac surgery. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Mobitz type II: constant PR interval with sudden non-conducted P waves, often grouped 2:1, 3:1 or 4:1. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Complete AV block: AV dissociation. A junctional escape gives 40–60 bpm with a narrow QRS; an infranodal escape gives under 40 bpm with a wide QRS. Nodal blocks may respond to atropine, infranodal blocks do not. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Left anterior fascicular block causes left axis deviation with a normal QRS duration; left posterior fascicular block causes right axis deviation. [PEER-PH]
- New right bundle-branch block with left anterior fascicular block is an indication for pacing. [PEER-PH]
- Pacemaker malfunction: oversensing is the commonest cause of failure to pace, lead-tip fibrosis of undersensing. A magnet restores default fixed-rate settings on a pacemaker and disables tachyarrhythmia sensing on an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Twiddler's syndrome: rotation of the pulse generator causing arm twitching. Pacemaker syndrome: fatigue, orthostatic dizziness and exercise intolerance from loss of AV synchrony. [PEER-PH]
- Pacemaker pocket infection: blood cultures, intravenous antibiotics, cardiology consultation and admission, with removal of the device and all hardware. [ROSH]

### Tachyarrhythmias

- Atrial fibrillation: irregularly irregular with no distinct P waves; the commonest sustained dysrhythmia, with an untreated rate averaging about 170 bpm. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Atrial flutter: atrial re-entry at 250–350 bpm with sawtooth waves; 2:1 conduction gives 150 bpm, so a persistent rate of 150 should raise suspicion. [PEER-PH]
- SVT: regular narrow-complex tachycardia at 120–200 bpm with hidden P waves; AV nodal reentrant tachycardia is the commonest paroxysmal SVT, about two-thirds of cases. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Wolff–Parkinson–White: PR interval under 120 ms, delta wave, widened QRS. Orthodromic AVRT is 90–95% of AVRT and is adenosine-responsive; pre-excited atrial fibrillation and antidromic AVRT are treated with procainamide, and AV-nodal blockers are contraindicated. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Ventricular tachycardia is wide-complex above 100 bpm; capture and fusion beats confirm it, and with previous infarction a wide-complex tachycardia is almost always VT. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Congenital long QT syndrome: Jervell and Lange-Nielsen (with deafness) and Romano-Ward (without); a corrected QT interval above 0.44 s is abnormal and above 0.55 s is strongly associated with sudden death. [PEER-PH]
- Drug-induced QT prolongation: tricyclics, fluconazole, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, ondansetron, methadone, antipsychotics and quinidine. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Pericardial Disease

- Acute pericarditis: pleuritic pain worse lying flat and relieved sitting forward with a friction rub; idiopathic in up to 90%, coxsackievirus the commonest infectious cause. The ECG shows diffuse ST elevation with PR depression. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Constrictive pericarditis: a rigid non-compliant pericardium after tuberculosis, surgery or radiation; Kussmaul sign, pericardial knock and prominent y descent; definitive treatment is pericardiectomy. [ROSH]
- Effusion and tamponade: trauma most often produces haemodynamic compromise; right atrial collapse is earliest, right ventricular diastolic collapse most specific; about 250 mL is needed for cardiomegaly on chest radiograph. [ROSH]

### Valvular Heart Disease

| Lesion | Murmur | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Aortic stenosis | Systolic crescendo–decrescendo to the carotids | Commonest valvular disease over 65; senile calcification commonest in the US; valve area under 1.0 cm² is severe; avoid nitroglycerin; congestive heart failure is the worst prognostic feature |
| Aortic regurgitation | Early diastolic blowing at the left sternal border | Marfan syndrome, ascending dissection; brisk pulses; nitroprusside and dobutamine |
| Mitral regurgitation | Holosystolic apex to axilla | Mitral valve prolapse commonest cause; papillary muscle rupture after infarction; pulmonary oedema; nitroprusside |
| Mitral stenosis | Mid-diastolic rumble at the apex | Rheumatic heart disease |
| Mitral valve prolapse | Midsystolic click, late systolic murmur | Palpitations commonest symptom; propranolol; outpatient cardiology follow-up |
| Tricuspid regurgitation | Holosystolic at the left lower sternal border | Raised right atrial pressure; commonest cause is right atrial and ventricular dilation; frequent in intravenous drug use endocarditis |

- The mitral valve is the only valve with two cusps. [ROSH]

### Heart Failure

- Heart failure is classified as systolic or diastolic by ejection fraction. [ROSH]
- High-output failure is elevated cardiac output with low systemic vascular resistance; causes include arteriovenous fistula, cirrhosis, pregnancy, severe anaemia, thiamine deficiency, thyrotoxicosis and hypermetabolic states such as leukaemia and lymphoma. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- BNP is released by myocytes in response to stretch and high end-diastolic pressure; a high body mass index falsely lowers BNP and NT-proBNP. Elevated BUN (45 mg/dL) and elevated troponin or lactate predict adverse outcome. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- An S3 heart sound is the most specific finding for acute decompensated heart failure; widespread B-lines on thoracic ultrasound support it. [ROSH]
- Nitroglycerin reduces preload and afterload; non-invasive positive pressure ventilation decreases intubation and in-hospital mortality; out-of-hospital morphine causes harm. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Intravenous nitroglycerin: 1 mg bolus every 5 minutes until a 25% reduction in systolic blood pressure. [ROSH]
- Carvedilol and metoprolol, and ACE inhibitors, improve survival; digoxin does not. [PEER-PH]
- Decompensated right ventricular failure in chronic pulmonary hypertension is treated with high-flow nasal cannula, which raises FiO2 without raising intrathoracic pressure. [PEER-PH]

### Cardiomyopathies and Myocarditis

- Dilated cardiomyopathy: dilation and impaired contraction of both ventricles; up to one-third link to cytoskeletal, sarcomeric or nuclear envelope gene mutations. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Restrictive cardiomyopathy: non-dilated ventricles with impaired filling and preserved early systolic function, and biatrial enlargement; amyloidosis thickens the wall. [PEER-PH]
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: autosomal dominant sarcomere mutation with asymmetric anterior septal hypertrophy. Dyspnoea is the commonest symptom; a leading cause of sudden death in young athletes; echocardiography is diagnostic. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy: fibrofatty infiltration producing an epsilon wave, prolonged S-wave upstroke and T-wave inversion in V1–V3; an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator is definitive. [ROSH]
- Takotsubo cardiomyopathy: transient apical ballooning beyond a single coronary territory; emotional stress in postmenopausal women, physical stress in men; resolves in 2–3 weeks. Phenylephrine is first-line for instability. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Peripartum cardiomyopathy: left ventricular systolic dysfunction with ejection fraction under 45% in late pregnancy or postpartum. E-point septal separation above 10 mm indicates decompensation. ACE inhibitors and angiotensin II receptor blockers are contraindicated — use hydralazine with nitrates. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Myocarditis: myocardial inflammation, most often viral (Coxsackie B); endocardial biopsy is the gold standard but rarely performed; admit for cardiac monitoring. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Congenital Heart Disease

- Acyanotic lesions: ventricular septal defect — the commonest congenital lesion, over 30% of defects — and coarctation. Cyanotic lesions: tetralogy of Fallot, transposition of the great vessels and tricuspid atresia. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Ductal-dependent lesions: critical coarctation, hypoplastic left heart syndrome, pulmonary atresia with intact interventricular septum, transposition, tetralogy of Fallot, interrupted aortic arch, critical aortic or pulmonary stenosis. [PEER-PH]
- Infants with congestive heart failure present with sweating with feeding, poor weight gain, weak cry, cough and wheeze. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Prostaglandin E1 (alprostadil) 0.05 mcg/kg/min intravenously maintains ductal patency; adverse effects are apnoea, bradycardia and hypotension. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Hyperoxia test: a PaO2 above 250 mm Hg suggests a non-cardiac cause, below 100 mm Hg congenital heart disease, and 100–250 mm Hg a partial mixing lesion. [PEER-PH]
- Tet spell: oxygen and the knee-to-chest position first, then fluid and morphine, with esmolol for refractory spells. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Coarctation is the commonest cause of severe hypertension in infants, is juxtaductal in 98%, and presents with weak femoral pulses and an arm–leg pressure difference. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Tetralogy of Fallot: harsh systolic ejection murmur with a single second heart sound; the commonest structural congenital lesion presenting outside the neonatal period, about 1 in 3,000 births. [PEER-PH]

### Aortic, Peripheral Vascular and Hypertensive Emergencies

- Hypertensive emergency is elevated pressure with end-organ damage: reduce mean arterial pressure by 20–25% in the first hour, then to 160/100–110 mm Hg over 6 hours with a titratable infusion such as nicardipine. [PEER-PH]
- More aggressive control is needed in aortic dissection, severe preeclampsia or eclampsia, and pheochromocytoma crisis. [PEER-PH]
- Asymptomatic hypertension needs no acute treatment and no specific systolic threshold applies. [ROSH]
- Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome: parieto-occipital vasogenic oedema from severe hypertension, preeclampsia or a calcineurin inhibitor such as tacrolimus; MRI is the imaging modality of choice. [ROSH]
- Preeclampsia: hydralazine. Sympathomimetic or pheochromocytoma crisis: phentolamine after benzodiazepines. Cocaine-related hypertension: benzodiazepine. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Stanford type A dissection involves the ascending aorta and needs emergent surgery; type B may be endovascular. Pregnancy is an independent risk factor, usually third trimester or postpartum. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Acute limb ischaemia: plaque-rupture thrombosis causes about 80% of lower limb and 50% of upper limb cases; a known embolic source, a normal contralateral limb and sudden onset favour embolism. [PEER-PH]
- The Rutherford criteria grade limb threat by Doppler signals, weakness, pain and sensation; absent arterial and venous signals with paralysis mean irreversible ischaemia. [PEER-PH]
- Carotid dissection is the commonest cause of stroke under age 45 and presents with unilateral neck or fronto-orbital headache and a partial Horner syndrome of miosis and ptosis without anhidrosis. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Splenic artery aneurysm is the commonest visceral artery aneurysm; mycotic aneurysm usually complicates endocarditis and is treated with vancomycin plus piperacillin-tazobactam. [ROSH]

### Endocarditis, Cardiac Tumours and Devices

- Risk factors: prosthetic valves, indwelling cardiac devices, intravenous drug use and previous rheumatic heart disease; the mitral valve is commonest overall and the tricuspid valve in intravenous drug use. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Staphylococcus aureus is the commonest organism. Obtain at least three blood cultures, start intravenous antibiotics and order a transthoracic echocardiogram, proceeding to transoesophageal echocardiography if negative in a high-risk patient. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Myxoma is the commonest primary cardiac tumour: 75–85% arise in the left atrium at the fossa ovalis; a tumour plop is an early diastolic sound. Metastatic disease is the commonest cardiac tumour overall. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Left ventricular assist device: bleeding is the commonest major non-mechanical complication and the driveline the likely infection source. Mean arterial pressure best estimates blood pressure; absent device sounds with no pulse require immediate CPR. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
