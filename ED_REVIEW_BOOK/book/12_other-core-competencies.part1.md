## High-Yield Exam Review

- Face-to-face conversation is the best medium for conflict resolution: eye contact, no interruptions, both seated. Email and text are prone to misinterpretation. [PEER-PH]
- 80% of medical errors involve miscommunication during transitions of care; 24% of malpractice claims follow a faulty handoff. A formal handoff tool such as I-PASS significantly decreases errors. [PEER-PH]
- ED I-PASS: Expected disposition, Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situational awareness, Synthesis by receiver. [PEER-PH]
- Most important item at sign-out: anticipated disposition. Goal of sign-out: patient safety and quality care. [PEER-PH]
- Boarding patients are signed out like active ED patients because the emergency physician may need to reassess them; code status is critical. [PEER-PH]
- Interpretation converts one spoken language into another; translation converts one written language into another. Neither is verbatim - cultural reframing is part of the task. [PEER-PH]
- A spoken-language interpreter sits behind the patient, out of the line of vision; the clinician keeps eye contact with the patient. A sign-language interpreter sits in the patient's line of sight. [PEER-PH]
- Ad hoc interpretation by a minor is prohibited under Section 1557 of the ACA except in a true life-or-death emergency with no formal interpreter available; ad hoc interpreters make twice as many errors. [PEER-PH]
- Both death-notification frameworks open with what the family already knows: GRIEV_ING gathers information first, SPIKES assesses perception first. [PEER-PH]
- Say "dead", not a euphemism. Avoid "intubated" - say "breathing tube". [PEER-PH]
- NURSE (Name, Understand, Respect, Support, Explore) responds to emotion rather than supplying facts or false reassurance. [PEER-PH]
- Witnessed pediatric resuscitation: invite the family in with a supportive staff member (AHA Class I). Not delegated to the code leader. [PEER-PH]
- Positive predictive value (PPV) is the proportion of a test's positive results that are truly positive; it depends on disease prevalence. [PEER-PH]
- Negative predictive value (NPV) is the probability that a patient with a negative result truly does not have the disease. [ROSH]
- Before a test is ordered, sensitivity and specificity are most useful; after the result is available, predictive values are most useful. [ROSH]
- In a normal distribution, 2 standard deviations (SD) around the mean contain 95.4% of values; 1 SD contains 68.2%, 3 SD contain 99.7%. [ROSH]
- Number needed to treat (NNT) is the inverse of the absolute risk reduction, rounded up to the next whole patient. [ROSH]
- Only 15% of errors originate from individuals; 85% originate from flawed systems. [PEER-PH]
- Errors are disclosed to the patient even when no harm has occurred, with information about monitoring; open disclosure and apology decrease litigation risk. [PEER-PH]
- Second victim syndrome is the clinician's trauma response after a medical error, adverse event, or lawsuit: the patient is the first victim, the clinician the second. [PEER-PH]
- Misdiagnosis is the most common cause of malpractice litigation. Missed fractures are 23% of diagnosis-related claims, but missed myocardial infarction carries the largest indemnities. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- An occurrence malpractice policy covers the incident regardless of when a claim is filed; a claims-made policy must be active at both the incident and the claim; tail coverage pays claims filed after a claims-made policy ends. [PEER-PH]
- A "$1,000,000/$3,000,000" policy pays up to $1,000,000 per occurrence and $3,000,000 total per year across claims. [ROSH]
- Hospice eligibility requires a potential life expectancy of 6 months or less; full code status does not disqualify a patient. [PEER-PH]
- POLST documenting no CPR and no intubation, comfort-focused, is honoured by extubation in the emergency department with comfort care. [PEER-PH]
- Approximately 2% of emergency department patients leave against medical advice; roughly 3% leave without being seen. [PEER-PH]
- A receiving hospital may decline a transfer under EMTALA only for lack of capability or capacity - never for insurance status or convenience. [PEER-PH]
- Active shooter response: run first, then hide, then fight as a last resort. Nurses are the staff group most frequently victimised by workplace violence, followed by physicians. [PEER-PH]
- Kotter's 8-step change model begins with creating a sense of urgency and need for change. [PEER-PH]

## Core Concepts

### Communication, Conflict, and De-escalation

- Angry patient de-escalation: acknowledge the grievance, sit at the same height, respect personal space, shift to problem-solving, offer small concessions. If this fails, sedation or restraint. [PEER-PH]
- When a colleague's behaviour is the problem, express concerns directly and ask them to clarify their motivations. Focus on problem behaviours ("it is frustrating when we cannot get antibiotics started within an hour") rather than personal characteristics ("you are so lazy"). [ROSH]
- Conflict resolution: collaboration (working together toward a resolution acceptable to both), accommodation (when the cost to the accommodating party is minimal and no bad precedent is set), and negotiation (give-and-take ending in an acceptable agreement). Preserve future rapport while resolving the issue. [ROSH]
- Crisis resource management: the team leader assigns specific roles, monitors globally rather than the details, and uses bidirectional and closed-loop communication. [PEER-PH]

### Handoffs and Transitions of Care

- Miscommunication during transitions underlies 80% of medical errors; faulty handoffs account for 24% of malpractice claims. [PEER-PH]
- ED I-PASS: Expected disposition, Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situational awareness, Synthesis by receiver. Concise and relevant; avoid irrelevant detail. [PEER-PH]
- Content rules: anticipated disposition first, sickest patients prioritised, pending results reviewed, sign-out time protected. A full history and physical, complete history, and medication list are not required. [PEER-PH]

### Language Access and Medical Interpretation

- Any patient with limited English proficiency must be offered interpreter services; language barrier is a social determinant of health. [PEER-PH]
- Section 1557 of the ACA prohibits ad hoc interpretation, and specifically prohibits the use of minors, except in a true life-or-death emergency with no formal interpreter available. Ad hoc interpreters make twice as many errors; HHS does not permit asking the patient's permission to use uncertified staff. [PEER-PH]
- Best practice: brief the interpreter on the goals of the encounter before the interview, keep eye contact with the patient, ask one question at a time, avoid jargon. [PEER-PH]
- A stable patient may not be interpreted by an uncertified clinician even after a long wait. In a genuinely emergent presentation, a non-certified speaker may obtain a basic history while a formal interpreter is arranged. [PEER-PH]

### Breaking Bad News and Death Notification

- GRIEV_ING: gather information first - what does the family already know? Be direct. The physician delivers the news. Stay for questions. [PEER-PH]
- SPIKES: Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Emotions, Strategy. Assess perception first, then a warning shot ("I have bad news"), then deliver. [PEER-PH]
- Use lay terms: "breathing tube", not "intubation". Use "dead" rather than a euphemism. "Sorry" is appropriate after a death. [PEER-PH]
- NURSE: Name, Understand, Respect, Support, Explore. Identify the emotion (shock, guilt) and connect with it; do not answer a guilt question with a factual medical explanation or with false reassurance. [PEER-PH]
- Organised sequence: gather the family, introduce the staff present, educate about the events, verify understanding, pause to allow information processing. [ROSH]
- Witnessed pediatric resuscitation is AHA Class I: a supportive staff member updates the parents and invites them into the resuscitation room, with decreased PTSD and anxiety. [PEER-PH]
- A child life specialist leads medical play (exploring equipment such as a needleless angiocatheter on a stuffed animal) to desensitise a child and restore control, and supports siblings after a child's death. Breaking bad news remains the physician's role. [PEER-PH]

### Patient Experience and Feedback

- Satisfaction drivers: staff communication, empathy, and wait times. Continual updates about triage and wait times improve overall satisfaction; amenities and staff experience matter less. [PEER-PH]
- A seated rather than standing clinician posture leads patients to perceive the encounter as longer, by up to 25%. [ROSH]
- Patient satisfaction is directly linked to medical liability risk - dissatisfied patients are more likely to sue. The Joint Commission requires a complaint management system. [PEER-PH]
- Feedback: learners feel they receive too little while teachers feel they give a lot, and attending satisfaction with feedback quality exceeds resident satisfaction. Real-time feedback (One-Minute Preceptor) beats end-of-shift feedback; signal phrases alert the learner. [PEER-PH]
- Constructive feedback uses specific examples rather than generalised advice; self-evaluation, positive reinforcement, and teaching pearls help. [PEER-PH]
- Setting expectations before assigning a patient is critical for a new resident: the ED pace is unique and senior staff often assume newcomers already understand its customs. [PEER-PH]

### Patient Safety, Error Disclosure, and the Second Victim

- High-reliability organisations rely on a robust error and near-miss reporting and tracking system, with all staff as stakeholders. [PEER-PH]
- Hierarchy suppresses safety concerns. Lidocaine and bupivacaine are both amide local anaesthetics with cross-reactivity, and lidocaine is often stocked in procedure kits without an electronic alert, so an intern who recalls a bupivacaine allergy should pause and ask to check the allergy list. [PEER-PH]
- Errors are disclosed to the patient and family and also reported to the hospital system; apology decreases litigation risk. [PEER-PH]
- Second victim syndrome features burnout, depression, substance use, guilt, sleep difficulty, and anxiety; it may present as erratic behaviour, lateness, yelling at staff, or drinking. [PEER-PH]
- Impairment is a mental disorder, physical disorder, or substance misuse interfering with a clinician's ability to provide safe, competent care - working while sick, bipolar disorder, cognitive decline, a shoulder fracture, or alcohol misuse. Signs include poor hygiene, lateness, emotional lability, and increased work-related errors. [ROSH]

### Implicit Bias, Microaggressions, and Allyship

- A microaggression is a subtle negative message, verbal or implied, directed at a people group. Categories: microassaults (intentional), microinsults (unintentional insensitivity), microinvalidations (diminishing experiences). [PEER-PH]
- Most microaggressions arise from implicit bias - subconscious and shaped by genetics, upbringing, and experience. Explicit bias and overt racism are conscious. [PEER-PH]
- Gender bias in acute coronary syndrome: women are less often diagnosed with myocardial infarction, less often catheterised, and given fewer cardiac medications despite identical features. Standardised tools such as the HEART score do not include gender. [PEER-PH]
- Bias reduction: standardised protocols where applicable (a haemorrhage protocol eliminated a racial disparity), individuation by getting to know patients as individuals, and removal of gender and race from case presentations unless relevant. Three conditions for change: pay attention to biases, intend to change, practise strategies. [PEER-PH]
- Perceived bias lowers patient satisfaction, which lowers adherence to medical recommendations. [PEER-PH]
- Allyship is the active, consistent practice of unlearning and re-evaluating in which a person in a position of power operates in solidarity with a marginalised group - a lifelong process. Tools include advocacy, self-reflection, education, bias confrontation, accountability, empathy, and listening. Educating clinicians about buprenorphine and take-home naloxone is advocacy. [PEER-PH]

### Trauma-Informed Care

- Six principles: safety; trust and transparency; peer support; collaboration and mutuality; empowerment, voice and choice; cultural, gender, and historical acknowledgement. Beneficence, justice, and non-maleficence belong to medical ethics, not to this framework. [PEER-PH]
- Window of tolerance: the optimal zone of arousal in which a patient can process emotions and surrounding events. Trauma shrinks the window; outside it a patient freezes (detached) or fights/flights (agitated). It is unique to each person. [PEER-PH]
- Response to a disclosure of abuse: listen non-judgmentally, use active listening and open-ended questions, respect boundaries, and do not interrupt or ask for specific details, which retraumatises. [PEER-PH]
- A patient who has been sexually assaulted decides whether to report. Evidence may be collected without speaking to law enforcement in most states unless state law mandates reporting; a support person is allowed. [PEER-PH]

### Gender Identity and Terminology

- Gender identity is the gender or genders with which a person identifies; sex is assigned at birth on the basis of genitalia; gender expression is outward communication; sexual orientation is attraction. [PEER-PH]
- A transgender woman was assigned male at birth and identifies as female; a cisgender person's gender aligns with the sex assigned at birth. Patients are identified by their preferred gender. [PEER-PH]
- Transmasculine means assigned female at birth with a masculine gender identity, including transgender men and some non-binary people; transfeminine means assigned male at birth with a feminine identity. [PEER-PH]
- Gender dysphoria (DSM-5) is distress arising from incongruence between birth sex and gender identity. Older terms such as gender identity disorder and transsexualism are no longer used. [PEER-PH]
- Gender is not binary; it is determined by chromosomes, gonads, hormonal expression, hormonal response, and societal pressures including assignment and rearing. [ROSH]
