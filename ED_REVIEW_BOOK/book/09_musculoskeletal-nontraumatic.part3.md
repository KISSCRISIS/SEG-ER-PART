## Diagnosis

### Imaging by clinical question

| Clinical question | First-line | Definitive |
|---|---|---|
| Atraumatic bone pain or suspected avascular necrosis | X-ray (may be normal early) | MRI, up to 100% sensitive [PEER-PH] |
| Osteomyelitis | X-ray — 40% to 50% bone loss needed before lucency; periosteal elevation at 14 to 21 days | MRI with and without contrast — highest combined sensitivity and specificity, 95% sensitive [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Discitis and vertebral osteomyelitis | X-ray | MRI with contrast [PEER-PH] |
| Epidural or psoas abscess | — | MRI spine [PEER-CS] |
| Cauda equina syndrome | — | MRI — gold standard [PEER-PH] |
| Suspected cord compression from cancer | X-ray | MRI with and without IV contrast [PEER-CS] |
| Occult hip fracture | X-ray — up to 10% are invisible | MRI [PEER-PH] |
| Suspected pelvic fracture | X-ray — sensitivity under 85% | CT without contrast of the bony pelvis [PEER-PH] |
| Vertebral compression fracture | X-ray is the screening test | — [PEER-PH] |

- New back pain in a patient with known cancer, or a new vertebral compression fracture in a cancer patient without neurological deficit, is staged with MRI with and without IV contrast. [PEER-CS]
- Back pain with fever, intravenous drug use, or a recent genitourinary procedure (for example cystoscopy) is imaged with MRI spine to look for discitis, vertebral osteomyelitis, epidural abscess, or psoas abscess. [PEER-CS]

### The limping child

| Condition | Clues | Confirmatory step |
|---|---|---|
| Transient synovitis | Well-appearing, afebrile, recent viral illness, tolerates passive range of motion | Normal hip x-ray and WBC; ESR and CRP to exclude sepsis [ROSH] |
| Septic arthritis | Fever, refuses weight-bearing, or cannot walk | Ultrasound for effusion, then aspiration; empiric antibiotics [PEER-CS] |
| Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease | Boy aged 5 to 8, limp, limited hip abduction and internal rotation, normal WBC and ESR | X-ray: sclerosis, irregularity, flattening of the femoral head [PEER-CS] |
| Slipped capital femoral epiphysis | Obese child aged 10 to 15, knee or thigh pain, hip held in external rotation, limited internal rotation | Widened physis on x-ray; frog-leg view of the pelvis [ROSH] |
| Developmental dysplasia of the hip | Under 6 months; clunk with abduction | Ortolani and Barlow manoeuvres; ultrasound [ROSH] |
| Juvenile idiopathic arthritis | Morning limp improving through the day, joint swelling, uveitis | Clinical; rheumatology referral |
| Apophysitis | Adolescent athlete, point tenderness at the tibial tuberosity, full range of motion and normal strength | Clinical; symptoms worse with running and relieved by rest [PEER-CS] |

### Bedside tests

| Test | How it is done | Indicates |
|---|---|---|
| Straight leg raise | Raise the extended leg | Sciatica — most sensitive test [ROSH] |
| Crossed straight leg raise | Raise the asymptomatic leg, reproducing contralateral pain | Herniated disc — most specific test [ROSH] |
| Spurling manoeuvre | Neck extended, head tilted, axial pressure applied | Cervical radiculopathy [PEER-PH] |
| Lhermitte phenomenon | Neck flexion produces shock-like paresthesias | Cervical myelopathy or radiculopathy [ROSH] |
| Finkelstein test | Thumb grasped in the palm, wrist ulnar deviated | De Quervain tenosynovitis [ROSH] |
| Empty can test | Arm elevated, thumb down, resisted | Supraspinatus impingement [PEER-PH] |
| Phalen manoeuvre | Wrist flexed and held for 1 minute | Carpal tunnel syndrome — most sensitive [PEER-CS] |
| Tinel sign | Percussion over the volar wrist crease | Carpal tunnel syndrome [ROSH] |
| Durkan test | Pressure over the transverse carpal ligament for 30 seconds, 64% sensitive and 83% specific | Carpal tunnel syndrome [ROSH] |
| Costochondritis manoeuvre | Shoulders extended and abducted, hands behind head, neck extended, posterior traction applied to the arms | Costochondritis [PEER-PH] |

- Medial epicondylitis produces a normal-appearing elbow with pain medially on resisted pronation; lateral epicondylitis inflames the extensor carpi radialis brevis attachment. [ROSH]

### Synovial fluid interpretation

| Cell count | Differential | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 5,000 to 20,000 WBC/µL, neutrophilic, no crystals | Inflammatory | Inflammatory or rheumatoid arthritis [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| 15,000 WBC/µL, 50% PMNs, negatively birefringent needle-shaped crystals, negative Gram stain | Crystal | Gout [PEER-PH] |
| 10,000 WBC/µL, 60% PMNs, positively birefringent rhomboid crystals | Crystal | Pseudogout [ROSH] |
| 75,000 to 90,000 WBC/µL, neutrophilic predominance, no crystals | Septic | Septic arthritis [ROSH] |
| More than 50,000 WBC/µL and more than 90% PMNs, low glucose | Septic | Septic arthritis; counts below 50,000 do not exclude it [PEER-CS][ROSH] |

- Every hot, swollen joint is imaged with x-ray and aspirated; fluid analysis is decisive and also therapeutic. Fever and chills in a patient on warfarin still require aspiration rather than a presumption of haemarthrosis. [ROSH][PEER-CS]

### Back pain: when to image

- Image when there is fever of 38.5°C or higher, intravenous drug use, a recent bacteraemia or procedure, a history of cancer, age over 50 with new pain, neurological deficit, urinary retention, or decreased perineal sensation. [PEER-CS]
- No imaging is required for acute mechanical back pain with a normal neurological examination, no red flags, and no midline bony tenderness — including radicular pain in a young patient with normal strength and sensation. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Decreased perineal sensation, bilateral leg symptoms, or difficulty voiding in a patient with back pain mandates emergent advanced imaging. [PEER-CS]

### Muscle, nerve, and laboratory patterns

- Suspected inflammatory myopathy is screened with creatine kinase; proximal and limb-girdle weakness with muscle tenderness and normal reflexes supports myositis. [ROSH]
- Guillain-Barré syndrome requires decreased reflexes for diagnosis, and lumbar puncture shows elevated protein with a normal white cell count. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Rhabdomyolysis: high creatine kinase, low calcium, high potassium, and high phosphorus in the acute phase; urinalysis is positive for blood with zero or rare red blood cells on microscopy. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- A rapid rise in serum creatinine marks acute kidney injury in rhabdomyolysis. [PEER-PH]

## Management

### Cauda equina syndrome

1. Suspect it with urinary retention, decreased anal or perineal sensation, bilateral leg symptoms, lower extremity hyporeflexia, or saddle anaesthesia. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
2. Measure postvoid residual at the bedside — a residual of 75 mL argues against a denervated bladder, while a value above 100 mL does not. [PEER-PH]
3. Obtain MRI, the gold standard. [PEER-PH]
4. Arrange emergent transfer to a tertiary centre for surgical decompression, which is the essential step in preventing long-term neurological sequelae, including when the cause is a spinal abscess. [PEER-CS][PEER-PH]
5. Give analgesia and treat the underlying cause; when compression comes from metastatic disease, give intravenous dexamethasone and consult neurosurgery. [ROSH]

### Acute compartment syndrome

1. Recognise the tense, firm compartment with pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch of the compartment muscles. [PEER-PH]
2. Measure compartment pressure: the delta pressure — diastolic blood pressure minus compartment pressure — below 30 mmHg is concerning and warrants orthopaedic consultation regardless of the absolute pressure. An anterior delta pressure of 15 mmHg is an abnormal result. [PEER-CS]
3. Consult surgery immediately for fasciotomy; do not wait for pulselessness. [PEER-PH]
4. Treat coexisting rhabdomyolysis with intravenous fluids and electrolyte monitoring. [PEER-PH]

### Rhabdomyolysis

1. Start intravenous normal saline — the primary treatment that reduces the risk of acute renal failure. [ROSH]
2. Target a urine output of 3 mL/kg/hr. [ROSH]
3. Monitor urine output and serum electrolytes closely, including potassium, calcium, phosphorus, and creatinine. [PEER-PH]
4. Treat hyperkalaemia and other electrolyte derangements; watch for acute tubular necrosis. [ROSH]

### Septic arthritis

1. Obtain x-rays and perform arthrocentesis. [PEER-CS]
2. Send fluid for cell count, crystals, Gram stain, and culture.
3. Start empiric intravenous vancomycin plus ceftriaxone, and involve orthopaedics for drainage. [PEER-CS]

### Necrotising soft-tissue infection

1. Suspect it with pain out of proportion, crepitus, bullae, or rapid progression in a toxic patient. [ROSH]
2. Start fluids and broad-spectrum parenteral antibiotics immediately. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
3. Consult surgery for emergent operative debridement — the definitive treatment; antibiotics alone do not suffice. [ROSH][PEER-CS]

### Antimicrobial choices by syndrome

| Syndrome | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Osteomyelitis in a child | 4 to 6 weeks of intravenous antibiotics; surgery for abscess or failure [PEER-PH] |
| Diabetic foot osteomyelitis with fever or toxicity | Vancomycin plus meropenem [PEER-PH] |
| Osteomyelitis after ankle surgery | Vancomycin plus piperacillin-tazobactam [ROSH] |
| Discitis | Vancomycin [PEER-PH] |
| Psoas or epidural abscess | Vancomycin plus cefepime [PEER-CS] |
| Septic arthritis | Vancomycin plus ceftriaxone [PEER-CS] |
| Flexor tenosynovitis or felon in an immunocompromised patient | Ampicillin-sulbactam plus vancomycin [ROSH] |
| Felon, outpatient | Incision and drainage plus trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and amoxicillin-clavulanate [ROSH] |
| Skin abscess with advanced kidney disease | Doxycycline [PEER-CS] |
| Dog bite with deep punctures | Ceftriaxone plus metronidazole [PEER-CS] |
| Wound after seawater or shellfish exposure | Doxycycline plus ceftazidime [PEER-CS] |
| Early Lyme disease | Doxycycline by mouth [PEER-CS] |
| Late disseminated Lyme arthritis | Oral antibiotics for 28 days [PEER-CS] |
| Herpetic whitlow | Acyclovir 800 mg by mouth twice daily for 7 days [ROSH] |

### Non-infectious musculoskeletal pain

- Acute radiculopathy without red flags: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs such as ibuprofen; continue normal activity as limited by pain. [PEER-PH]
- Reactive arthritis: indomethacin, with cultures of the pharynx and vagina to identify Chlamydia trachomatis. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Rheumatoid arthritis flare while already on disease-modifying therapy: prednisone. [PEER-PH]
- Inflammatory myopathy: glucocorticoids. [PEER-CS]
- Fibromyalgia with musculoskeletal chest pain: cyclobenzaprine. [PEER-PH]
- Costochondritis: topical lidocaine when nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs are unsafe, as in chronic kidney disease. [PEER-PH]
- Aseptic bursitis: ice, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and a compression dressing; septic bursitis requires antibiotics. [ROSH]
- Tendinopathy: rest, ice, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and activity modification. De Quervain tendinopathy is treated with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and a thumb spica splint. [ROSH]
- Apophysitis: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs with return to play as tolerated; restrict weight-bearing with crutches and arrange outpatient follow-up when the pain limits walking. [PEER-CS]
- Inflammatory back pain from spondyloarthritis: refer to physical therapy. [ROSH]

### Hand infections

- Paronychia: elevate the cuticle margin or separate the nail from the skin with a scalpel; drain if fluctuant. [ROSH]
- Felon: digital block with incision and drainage through a longitudinal incision on the lateral aspect of the distal finger; a high lateral approach is used for pulp infection. [ROSH]
- Flexor tenosynovitis: intravenous antibiotics plus incision and drainage by a hand surgeon. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

### Disposition and referral

- Transient synovitis: discharge with limb rest and oral analgesia; weight-bearing as tolerated. [ROSH]
- Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease: symptomatic management with urgent orthopaedic referral. [PEER-CS]
- Slipped capital femoral epiphysis: non-weight-bearing status, hip joint aspiration, and surgical pinning. [PEER-CS]
- Developmental dysplasia of the hip: urgent outpatient orthopaedic consultation. [ROSH]
- Suspected bone tumour: orthopaedic consultation for expedited follow-up. [PEER-PH]
- Pathological fracture: pain control, then orthopaedic surgery consultation. [PEER-PH]
- Osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture without neurological deficit: pain control. [PEER-PH]
- Athletic cervical disc herniation: return to play only when all symptoms have resolved. [PEER-PH]
- Adolescent radicular back pain provoked by sport: activity restriction. [PEER-CS]
