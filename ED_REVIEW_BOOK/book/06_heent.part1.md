## High-Yield Exam Review

- Sudden, painless, monocular vision loss with a pale edematous retina and a cherry-red macula is central retinal artery occlusion (CRAO). [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The embolus in CRAO usually originates in the internal carotid artery, whose ophthalmic branch gives rise to the central retinal artery; the same source can also cause a cerebral infarct. [PEER-PH]
- CRAO is an ischemic stroke of the eye. Transfer to a center staffed by both neurology and ophthalmology, because permanent loss can occur in as little as 4 hours. [PEER-PH]
- Painful monocular vision loss with pain on eye movement, loss of red color vision and an afferent pupillary defect indicates optic neuritis; oral prednisone is contraindicated at first presentation. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- A mid-dilated, minimally reactive pupil with a hazy cornea, halos around lights, headache and vomiting after entering a dark room is acute angle-closure glaucoma; intraocular pressure is typically above 40 mm Hg. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Treat acute angle-closure glaucoma with topical timolol first, then acetazolamide 500 mg IV or PO, plus topical apraclonidine and pilocarpine 1–2% drops, with the head of the bed elevated. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Cells and flare on slit-lamp examination are required to diagnose iritis (anterior uveitis); cycloplegics relieve the ciliary spasm and pilocarpine must be avoided. [PEER-PH]
- Proptosis, pain with eye movement, ophthalmoplegia, diplopia and decreased visual acuity separate orbital from preseptal cellulitis; contrast-enhanced CT of the orbits confirms it. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- An abnormal head impulse test with corrective saccades, together with unidirectional purely horizontal nystagmus, indicates a peripheral cause of acute vestibular syndrome. [PEER-PH]
- Hearing loss separates labyrinthitis from vestibular neuritis; both cause vertigo, nausea, vomiting and nystagmus. [PEER-PH]
- Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is the most common cause of vertigo; episodes last seconds and respond to the Epley maneuver. [ROSH]
- Ménière disease is episodic vertigo lasting 20 minutes to hours with tinnitus, aural fullness and sensorineural hearing loss from endolymphatic hydrops. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Impaired mobility of the tympanic membrane carries the greatest likelihood ratio for acute otitis media — 31 when distinctly impaired, and 4 when slightly impaired. [ROSH]
- Mastoiditis is the most common suppurative complication of acute otitis media; postauricular erythema and bony tenderness mandate admission for intravenous antibiotics and ENT consultation. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Parenteral vancomycin plus an antipseudomonal agent such as cefepime is the regimen for mastoiditis in a patient with recurrent otitis media or recent antibiotic use. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Malignant (necrotizing) otitis externa: diabetes, granulation tissue on the floor of the external canal, profuse otorrhea failing topical drops, and cranial nerve VII palsy as the most common complication. [PEER-PH]
- Suppurative perichondritis is usually Pseudomonas aeruginosa after ear piercing; the lobule is spared and cartilage necrosis is the feared complication. [PEER-PH]
- A button battery in the nose is an emergency: electrical current causes alkaline septal necrosis in as little as 4 hours, and oxymetazoline is contraindicated. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Unilateral purulent nasal discharge in a toddler should be treated as a nasal foreign body until proven otherwise — examine inside the nose. [PEER-PH]
- Posterior epistaxis arises from the sphenopalatine artery and needs posterior packing or a balloon catheter, not anterior pressure alone. [ROSH]
- Ludwig angina is a polymicrobial submandibular, sublingual and submental infection, most often odontogenic; it threatens the airway and requires awake fiberoptic intubation and intensive care admission. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Tripod positioning, drooling and stridor in a febrile child indicate epiglottitis: leave the child in the position of comfort, defer vascular access, and secure the airway in the operating room. [ROSH]
- Fever, stridor, hoarse voice and tracheal tenderness after a viral prodrome suggest bacterial tracheitis rather than croup. [ROSH]
- In traumatic hyphema, raise the head of the bed to 45 degrees; rebleeding at 3–5 days and a rise in intraocular pressure are the leading complications. [PEER-PH]
- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors are contraindicated in sickle cell disease because lowering aqueous pH promotes sickling and further raises intraocular pressure. [PEER-PH]
- Chemical eye injury: irrigate with normal saline before any other assessment, and continue until the ocular pH is 7.0–7.4. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- A hypopyon indicates endophthalmitis or severe infectious keratitis, both sight-threatening; a wood splinter in the eye requires the most urgent removal of any ocular foreign body. [ROSH][PEER-CS]
- Admit for parenteral antimicrobial therapy when periorbital swelling is accompanied by proptosis, ophthalmoplegia or visual change. [PEER-CS]
- Acute dystonia caused by a dopamine-receptor-blocking drug resolves with diphenhydramine or benztropine within 10–30 minutes. [PEER-CS]

## Core Concepts

### The painful red eye — pattern recognition

| Presentation | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Sudden painful red eye, mid-dilated poorly reactive pupil, hazy cornea, halos, vomiting after dark exposure | Acute angle-closure glaucoma |
| Painful red eye with photophobia, consensual pain, ciliary flush, cells and flare | Iritis (anterior uveitis) |
| Watery discharge, spreads from one eye to the other after a viral upper respiratory illness | Viral conjunctivitis |
| Purulent discharge at the lid margins and corners | Bacterial conjunctivitis |
| Severe pruritus with minimal pain | Allergic conjunctivitis |
| Contact lens wearer with pain, gritty sensation, photophobia and decreased acuity | Infectious keratitis |
| Bilateral severe eye pain hours after ultraviolet exposure (tanning bed, welding arc, snow) | Radiation keratitis |
| Dendritic ulcer or corneal vesicles on fluorescein staining | Herpes keratitis |
| Painless red eye with a whitish wedge growing from the medial conjunctiva onto the cornea | Pterygium |
| Tender pustule at the eyelid margin at a lash follicle | Hordeolum |
| Painless, nontender rubbery nodule on the upper lid margin | Chalazion |
| Painless red eye with unchanged vision, no pain, no trauma | Reassurance and discharge |

The triage question in any red eye is whether the eye is sight-threatening. Pain out of proportion, photophobia, reduced visual acuity, a fixed or mid-dilated pupil, corneal haze and a hypopyon all point away from simple conjunctivitis and toward angle-closure glaucoma, iritis, keratitis or endophthalmitis.

### Peripheral versus central vertigo

- Peripheral causes are BPPV, vestibular neuritis, labyrinthitis and Ménière disease.
- Central causes include ischemic stroke, vertebrobasilar insufficiency and infectious processes such as meningitis, mastoiditis and syphilis. [ROSH]
- The HINTS (Head Impulse, Nystagmus, Test of Skew) examination is the bedside discriminator in persistent vertigo with nystagmus.
- Peripheral pattern: abnormal head impulse with corrective saccades, unidirectional purely horizontal fatigable nystagmus, and no skew deviation. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Central pattern: absent corrective saccades, nystagmus that is bidirectional or direction-changing, vertical or nonfatigable. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The purpose of the examination is to decide who needs stroke imaging: about one-quarter of cases of acute vestibular syndrome are central in origin. [PEER-PH]
- Hearing loss or tinnitus with vertigo moves the diagnosis toward labyrinthitis or Ménière disease, because vestibular neuritis spares the cochlea. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Ménière disease is defined by recurrence: two or more episodes of vertigo lasting 20 minutes or longer but under 24 hours, with audiometric hearing loss and tinnitus or aural fullness. [ROSH]

### The painful or draining ear

- Simple otitis externa begins with itching and progresses to pain, canal erythema and edema, and pain on moving the pinna; Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Staphylococcus aureus are the usual organisms. [ROSH]
- Malignant otitis externa is the same disease at the far end of the severity scale: an older, diabetic or immunocompromised patient with severe pain, profuse otorrhea, granulation tissue in the canal and failure of topical drops. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Cranial nerve VII palsy is the most common complication of necrotizing otitis externa; untreated infection progresses to temporal bone and skull base osteomyelitis, meningitis and intracranial abscess. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Acute otitis media is diagnosed on pneumatic otoscopy. Impaired mobility is the single most useful sign; many other findings, such as erythema, are nonspecific and can reflect crying or chronic scarring. [ROSH]
- Bullous myringitis is an acute otitis media variant that shares its pathogens; Streptococcus pneumoniae is the most likely organism. [ROSH]
- Mastoiditis presents with postauricular tenderness, erythema and protrusion of the auricle on top of a picture of acute otitis media. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- The dividing line between a red eardrum and mastoiditis is bony tenderness and postauricular swelling; the second mandates admission for intravenous therapy and ENT review. [PEER-PH]

### Airway-threatening infection of the head and neck

- Epiglottitis: abrupt fever, odynophagia, muffled voice, drooling and stridor. Leave the patient in the position of comfort; a lateral neck film, if obtained, is taken at the bedside and classically shows a thumbprint sign. [ROSH]
- Bacterial tracheitis: an ill child, usually 5–8 years old, with high fever, hoarse voice, cough, stridor and tracheal tenderness following a viral prodrome. Patients point to the trachea as the source of pain. [ROSH]
- Ludwig angina: bilateral woody, tender induration of the submandibular and submental spaces with tongue elevation, dysphagia and drooling. The airway can obstruct abruptly, so early definitive airway management is the priority. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Peritonsillar abscess: unilateral sore throat, muffled "hot potato" voice, uvular deviation, trismus and drooling. Treatment is drainage plus antibiotics, with follow-up within 24–36 hours. [PEER-CS]
- Retropharyngeal abscess: a febrile child with neck stiffness, torticollis and drooling who cannot find a comfortable neck position; contrast-enhanced CT of the neck is the study of choice. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

### Orbital versus preseptal cellulitis

- The orbital septum is the dividing line. Preseptal cellulitis involves only structures anterior to it — the eyelid and periorbital soft tissue.
- Posterior findings — proptosis, pain with eye movement, ophthalmoplegia, diplopia, chemosis and decreased visual acuity — define orbital cellulitis. Periorbital cellulitis causes none of these. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Preseptal cellulitis accounts for 90–95% of periocular infections in children and is often a complication of upper respiratory infection or eyelid infection. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Contrast-enhanced CT of the orbits and sinuses is the investigation that separates them; orbital cellulitis shows fat stranding of the orbital contents, proptosis and extraocular muscle edema. [PEER-PH]
- Both entities can present with eye pain, eyelid edema and fever, which is why imaging rather than examination alone settles the diagnosis in equivocal cases. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]

### Dental and deep-space odontogenic infection

- A periapical abscess presents with exquisite tenderness to percussion, swelling of the surrounding gingiva and mild tooth elevation. [ROSH]
- Spread is named by the space involved: buccal space (cheek swelling), masticator space (trismus), and the submandibular, sublingual and submental spaces (Ludwig angina). [PEER-CS]
- Trismus, fever or malaise in a dental infection means the infection has escaped the alveolus; contrast-enhanced CT defines the extent and parenteral antibiotics plus urgent dental or surgical consultation follow. [PEER-CS]
- Dry socket (postextraction alveolar osteitis) follows roughly 30% of impacted third molar extractions and is treated by irrigating the socket. [PEER-PH]
- Acute necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis is an invasive periodontal infection marked by blunted, ulcerated interdental papillae and a gray pseudomembrane that bleeds when removed. [ROSH]
