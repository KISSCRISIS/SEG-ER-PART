## High-Yield Exam Review

- Obstructive kidney failure from benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH): place a Foley catheter first; there is no contraindication and relief is emergent [PEER-PH].
- Emergent renal replacement therapy: hyperkalemia > 6.5 mmol/L or any potassium-related conduction abnormality on ECG. AEIOU - Acidemia, Electrolytes, Ingestion, Overload, Uremia [PEER-PH].
- Renal ultrasound is the preferred initial study in new-onset asymptomatic kidney failure; it detects hydronephrosis with 90% sensitivity and specificity, and severe hydronephrosis gives the "bear paw" sign [PEER-PH].
- Hypotension is the most frequent complication of hemodialysis, occurring in about 50% of treatments; the commonest cause is excessive ultrafiltration [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome: headache, nausea, malaise and disorientation during or shortly after hemodialysis, from rapid urea removal with transient cerebral edema [ROSH].
- Cloudy peritoneal dialysis effluent means peritonitis; Staphylococcus epidermidis is the commonest organism, and empiric therapy covers gram-positive and gram-negative organisms [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Bleeding from a dialysis arteriovenous fistula: hold firm finger pressure for 5-10 minutes, then confirm a thrill is present; a lost thrill needs immediate vascular surgery consultation [ROSH].
- Poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis (PSGN) follows streptococcal pharyngitis by 1-2 weeks (skin infection 3-6 weeks) with oedema, dark urine, and hypertension; antistreptolysin O confirms it and decreased serum C3 is most specific [ROSH].
- Nephrotic syndrome is hypercoagulable with risk of thromboembolic events in renal, deep, and pulmonary veins. Minimal change disease is the commonest paediatric cause, at 2-6 years [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- The CMS 3-hour sepsis bundle is 30 mL/kg crystalloid, broad-spectrum antibiotics, blood cultures, and serum lactate. Urine culture is not part of the bundle [PEER-PH].
- Pyelonephritis is the most common cause of septic shock in pregnancy [PEER-PH].
- Urinary tract infection (UTI) in pregnancy: cephalexin, nitrofurantoin, or amoxicillin for 10-14 days; ciprofloxacin is contraindicated. Asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnancy needs antibiotics plus a urine culture [PEER-PH].
- Neonatal UTI: IV ampicillin and gentamicin; ceftriaxone is avoided because of hyperbilirubinemia and nephrotoxicity [PEER-PH].
- Escherichia coli causes more than 80% of UTIs. Uncomplicated cystitis: nitrofurantoin or oral trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for 5 days [ROSH].
- Urethritis in a young man: ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once plus doxycycline 100 mg PO twice daily for 7 days [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Primary syphilis: a single 1-2 cm painless ulcer with a raised border and clean base. Treat with benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Chancroid (Haemophilus ducreyi): multiple painful, sharply demarcated ulcers with purulent bases plus a tender inguinal bubo; single oral dose of azithromycin [ROSH].
- Lymphogranuloma venereum (Chlamydia trachomatis serovars L1, L2, L3): a painless ulcer that heals in 2-3 days, then painful inguinal lymphadenopathy weeks later [ROSH].
- An absent cremasteric reflex is the most specific sign of testicular torsion; its presence is the most useful finding to exclude torsion. Salvage falls from 96% at 4-6 hours to 20% after 12 hours [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Priapism is an erection lasting > 4 hours; the most common sequela is impotence, in up to 35%. Trazodone and other antidepressants are common causes [PEER-PH].
- Fournier gangrene: diabetes mellitus is the strongest risk factor and gas in the scrotal wall is the hallmark; broad-spectrum antibiotics plus emergency surgical debridement [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Calcium is the most common stone solute; tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily aids expulsion of ureteral stones > 5 mm [PEER-PH].
- Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) carries a fourfold increased risk of cerebral aneurysm; hypertension is the commonest finding and cardiac causes the commonest cause of death [PEER-PH][ROSH].

## Core Concepts

### Acute Kidney Injury

- AKI is a decline in kidney function over hours to days producing azotemia, the accumulation of creatinine and urea in the blood. Diagnostic thresholds: a rise in serum creatinine >= 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, a rise to >= 1.5 times baseline within 7 days, or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 hours [ROSH].
- Acute tubular necrosis is the commonest cause of in-hospital AKI and follows nephrotoxins (aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, cisplatin, foscarnet, tenofovir, mannitol), sepsis, or prolonged ischaemia [ROSH].
- Acute interstitial nephritis affects the tubules and interstitium but spares the glomeruli; most cases are a hypersensitivity drug reaction, most often to antibiotics, NSAIDs, diuretics, or cimetidine. It is not dose dependent and recurs on re-exposure [ROSH].
- Contrast-associated AKI: kidney perfusion falls for up to 20 hours after radiocontrast; creatinine rises > 25% above baseline within 3 days and resolves within 10-14 days [ROSH].

### Obstructive Uropathy and Urinary Retention

- BPH is the most common cause of obstructive AKI in older adults and the commonest cause of acute urinary retention in men; about 50% of men at age 50 and 80% at age 80 have symptoms attributed to BPH, and untreated men carry a 2.5% annual risk of acute retention [ROSH].
- Other causes of retention: neoplasm, haematuria with clots, infection, trauma, extraurinary compression, medications, and neurogenic bladder. Retention in young children may reflect voluntary holding, UTI-related pain, or constipation.

### Urinary Tract Infection and Pyelonephritis

- Cystitis is lower urinary tract infection; pyelonephritis is upper tract infection (ureter and kidney), with a clinical continuum between them based on systemic signs.
- Uncomplicated cystitis is bladder inflammation without pregnancy, comorbidity, or toxic appearance. Women are affected more often because of a shorter urethra and ascending gastrointestinal pathogens.
- E. coli is the most common organism and converts nitrate to nitrite; the urine nitrite reaction has > 90% specificity [ROSH].
- Urinalysis shows leukocyte esterase and nitrites with at least 10 WBC/hpf; in male patients even 1 WBC/hpf can be significant pyuria when bacteria are present.
- Pregnancy predisposes to pyelonephritis via smooth muscle relaxation, ureteral dilation, and pressure from the enlarging uterus; common organisms include E. coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Enterobacter cloacae, Proteus mirabilis, and group B Streptococcus [ROSH].
- Paediatric UTI: infants and young children often present with fever without an obvious source; verbal children report frequency, hesitancy, urgency, and dysuria.
- Asymptomatic bacteriuria is generally not treated, except in pregnant patients, kidney transplant recipients, and patients undergoing urologic instrumentation. Pregnant patients are screened with urine culture and treated if growth exceeds 100,000 CFU/mL [ROSH].
- Older, comorbid, or immunocompromised patients may present non-specifically with fever, rigors, altered mental status, or lethargy [ROSH].

### Glomerular Disease

- Nephrotic syndrome is heavy proteinuria causing hypoalbuminemia, oedema, and hyperlipidemia; nephrotic-range proteinuria is > 3.5 g per day in adults, > 50 mg/kg in children, or 3+ to 4+ on dipstick [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Minimal change disease is the commonest paediatric cause at 2-6 years and is treated with corticosteroids under pediatric nephrology guidance; complement levels are normal and kidney failure is uncommon [ROSH].
- PSGN is the commonest childhood glomerulonephritis and a type III hypersensitivity: IgM-containing immune complexes deposit in membranes and fix complement. It may follow streptococcal pharyngitis or impetigo, which appears as a honey-crusted rash [ROSH].
- Alport syndrome is hereditary kidney injury with sensorineural hearing loss, transmitted mainly X-linked through a collagen and basement membrane gene [ROSH].
- Goodpasture syndrome: IgG targets type IV collagen in kidney and lung basement membrane, causing haemoptysis and kidney impairment; treat with plasmapheresis, prednisone, and cyclophosphamide [ROSH].

### Nephrolithiasis

- Four stone types exist: calcium, uric acid, struvite, and cystine. Calcium oxalate and phosphate dominate; uric acid is about 8%, struvite and cystine about 1% each [PEER-PH].
- Struvite (magnesium-ammonium-phosphate) stones are 10-15% of stones and occur almost exclusively with UTI from urea-splitting bacteria (Proteus, Klebsiella, Providencia, Pseudomonas, Staphylococcus); they are the commonest cause of staghorn calculi [ROSH].
- Risk factors: Caucasian race, male sex, obesity, high meat diet, diabetes, gout, hyperparathyroidism, immobilization, dehydration, and excess salt [PEER-PH].
- Indinavir, an HIV protease inhibitor, crystallizes in urine and causes ureteral stones in about 10% of treated patients; other culprits are ceftriaxone, ciprofloxacin, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, and triamterene [PEER-PH].
- Classic presentation is abrupt colicky flank pain radiating to the groin (testicles in men, labia in women) with nausea and vomiting; only one-third have gross haematuria. Proximal stones cause flank pain, midureteral stones lower quadrant pain [ROSH].

### Scrotal, Testicular and Genital Emergencies

- Epididymitis is the leading cause of acute scrotal pain in adults; infection ascends retrogradely from the vas deferens, and age, sexual activity, and urologic risk factors determine the pathogen: sexually active men have Chlamydia trachomatis and Neisseria gonorrhoeae, while others have urinary pathogens such as E. coli, especially with BPH or instrumentation [ROSH].
- Prehn sign - relief of pain with elevation of the testicle - supports epididymitis; urinalysis shows pyuria in about 50% of cases [ROSH].
- Testicular torsion results from inadequate fixation of the testicle to the tunica vaginalis; peak incidence is age 13 years, a necrotic testicle can result within 4-6 hours, and most patients vomit unless the testis is torsing and detorsing [ROSH].
- Appendageal torsion of the appendix testis occurs most commonly in boys aged 4-10 years; symptoms resemble torsion but are less severe, more gradual, and more localized [ROSH].
- Hydrocele is fluid in the tunica vaginalis and the commonest cause of painless scrotal swelling in children; communicating hydroceles have a patent processus vaginalis, and most resolve spontaneously by 18 months [ROSH].
- Varicocele is a "bag of worms" of dilated pampiniform plexus veins, far commoner on the left because of the length of the left spermatic vein and its perpendicular entry into the renal vein [ROSH].
- Testicular cancer is the commonest solid malignancy in men aged 15-35 years but only 1% of male cancers; about 95% are germ cell tumours and cryptorchidism is the highest risk factor [ROSH].
- Fournier gangrene is necrotizing fasciitis of the perineal, genital, or perianal anatomy, usually mixed aerobic and anaerobic; early pain may later improve or resolve from nerve damage [ROSH].

### Sexually Transmitted Genital Ulcers and Urethritis

- Urethritis in men causes urethral discharge, dysuria, urgency, and haematuria; nongonococcal urethritis is most often from C. trachomatis and Mycoplasma genitalium [ROSH].
- Chancroid begins 3-10 days after exposure as a painful papule or pustule that rapidly ulcerates; half of patients develop a unilateral, painful, fluctuant bubo, and drainage is not usually recommended [ROSH].
- Genital herpes causes painful genital ulcers, dysuria, fever, tender inguinal lymphadenopathy, and headache; most cases are HSV-2, though HSV-1 is increasingly recognized [ROSH].
- Lymphogranuloma venereum progresses in three stages: a small painless genital or rectal ulcer at 3-21 days that heals in 2-3 days, then lymphadenitis that can become suppurative [ROSH].

### Hereditary and Neoplastic Kidney Disease

- ADPKD is the commonest genetic cause of chronic kidney disease, up to 10% of end-stage renal disease in the United States and Europe; 85-90% of cases relate to PKD1 mutations, and it features hepatic and pancreatic cysts, cerebral aneurysms, and cardiac valve abnormalities [PEER-PH].
- Wilms tumour (nephroblastoma) is the commonest primary childhood kidney malignancy and the second commonest childhood abdominal malignancy after neuroblastoma; median age is 3.5 years, with mutations such as WT1 and WT2 on chromosome 11 [ROSH].
- Renal cell carcinoma is about 85% of renal-derived neoplasms; symptoms include haematuria, cachexia, flank pain, anemia, and hypercalcemia [ROSH].
- Bladder cancer is the commonest urinary tract malignancy; urothelial cell carcinoma is 90% of cases, most occur over age 65 years, incidence is four times higher in men, and cigarette smoking is the leading environmental risk factor [ROSH].
