## Diagnosis

### Vaginitis and vulvovaginitis

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| pH 4.0–4.5, thick white odorless discharge, pruritus, pseudohyphae | Candida vaginitis [ROSH] |
| pH 5.5, thin gray fishy discharge, clue cells | Bacterial vaginosis [ROSH] |
| Frothy green-yellow discharge, punctate cervical hemorrhages, motile protozoa | Trichomoniasis [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Swollen erythematous vulva after an irritant, normal wet prep | Contact vulvovaginitis [PEER-PH] |
| Foul-smelling brown discharge, retained tampon visible | Foreign body vaginitis [ROSH] |
| Grouped vesicles and shallow ulcers on an erythematous base | Genital herpes [PEER-PH][ROSH] |
| Painless cauliflower-like papules | Condyloma acuminata — HPV 6 and 11 [ROSH] |
| Single ectocervical ulcer with mucosanguineous discharge | Cervical cancer [ROSH] |

- Normal vaginal pH is 3.8–4.5; pruritus is the most specific symptom of Candida vaginitis [ROSH].
- Cervicitis: purulent endocervical discharge and bleeding after swabbing; examine the cervix to exclude it [ROSH].

### Pelvic inflammatory disease
- Lower abdominal or pelvic pain with lower abdominal, cervical motion, or adnexal tenderness — the presumptive diagnosis in a sexually active patient when no other etiology is found, including transgender men with natal organs [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Also cervical friability, dyspareunia, abnormal bleeding, or discharge [ROSH].
- Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome: right upper quadrant pain worsened by inspiration and radiating to the right shoulder, with a negative Murphy sign. Liver function tests and right upper quadrant ultrasound are normal; laparoscopy shows hepatic fibrinous exudates [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Tubo-ovarian abscess: fever with a complex, fluid-filled or septated adnexal mass and a negative pregnancy test [PEER-PH].
- Nucleic acid amplification confirms C. trachomatis; when bacterial vaginosis is found, offer testing for gonorrhoea, chlamydia, HIV, and syphilis [ROSH].

### Early pregnancy: ultrasound and ectopic pregnancy

| Finding | Gestational age | beta-hCG |
|---|---|---|
| Gestational sac — anechoic centre, thick echogenic ring | ~5 weeks | 1,500–2,000 IU/L [ROSH] |
| Yolk sac | 5–6 weeks | 2,000–2,500 IU/L [ROSH] |
| Fetal pole | ~6 weeks | ~5,000 IU/L [ROSH] |

- The gestational sac is the earliest finding — transvaginal at 4–5 weeks, transabdominal at 6 weeks. Only an intrauterine fetal pole with a yolk sac confirms intrauterine pregnancy; the double decidual sign at 5.5–6 weeks does not confirm [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- The discriminatory zone is 1,500–2,000 IU/L; use pelvic ultrasound when beta-hCG is below 3,500 IU/L [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Ectopic pregnancy: abdominal pain (90%), amenorrhea, and vaginal bleeding. Unexplained syncope and hypotension in a woman of reproductive age is presumed ectopic until proven otherwise [ROSH].
- Transvaginal ultrasound is the best modality; free fluid in Morison pouch strongly predicts the need for operative intervention [PEER-PH].
- Pregnancy of unknown location: repeat beta-hCG in 48 hours; a normal early pregnancy rises at least 50–66% over 48 hours [PEER-PH][ROSH].

### Classification of first-trimester abortion

| Type | Os | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Threatened | Closed | Intrauterine pregnancy with cardiac activity; bleeding, no passed tissue [ROSH] |
| Inevitable | Open | Bleeding with cervical dilation before 20 weeks [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Incomplete | Open | Fetal tissue in the os or vaginal vault [ROSH] |
| Complete | Closed | Products passed; empty contracted uterus [ROSH] |
| Missed | Closed | Intrauterine pregnancy without cardiac activity [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Septic | — | Infection complicating pregnancy loss [ROSH] |

- Loss before 20 weeks or of a fetus under 500 g; about 75% occur before 8 weeks, and about 25% of pregnant patients bleed in early pregnancy with half of those miscarrying [ROSH].
- Risk factors: prior spontaneous abortion, advanced maternal age, lupus, infection, anatomic abnormalities, obesity, diabetes, thyroid disease, tobacco, alcohol, cocaine [ROSH][PEER-PH].

### Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy

| Parameter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Hypertension | SBP ≥140 or DBP ≥90 mmHg twice at least 4 hours apart, or SBP ≥160 or DBP ≥110 mmHg once [ROSH] |
| Proteinuria | Protein to creatinine ratio ≥0.3, or ≥300 mg per 24 hours [ROSH] |
| Severe features | SBP ≥160 or DBP ≥110 mmHg, thrombocytopenia, elevated transaminases, kidney dysfunction, pulmonary edema, neurologic involvement [ROSH] |
| Eclampsia | Seizures with preeclampsia [PEER-PH][ROSH] |

- Onset is after 20 weeks and up to 6 weeks postpartum, more than 2 days after delivery; proteinuria is not required when end-organ damage is present [ROSH].
- Eclampsia most often presents with headache and visual disturbances; neurologic severe features include binocular diplopia, new-onset headache, and blurred vision [ROSH].
- HELLP syndrome presents between 20 weeks and 4 weeks postpartum with elevated AST, LDH, and bilirubin, thrombocytopenia, elevated creatinine, low hemoglobin, and proteinuria, while fibrinogen, PT, and PTT stay normal [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Risk factors: nulliparity, multifetal gestation, obesity, diabetes mellitus, lower socioeconomic status, age over 40 [PEER-PH][ROSH].

### Third-trimester bleeding

| Feature | Placenta previa | Abruption | Uterine rupture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bleeding | Painless | Painful | Painful |
| Uterus | Non-tender | Tender, contracting | Flaccid, oddly shaped |
| Fetus | — | Abnormal heart tones | Part palpable outside the uterus; bradycardia |
| Key risk | Prior cesarean | Prior abruption, cocaine [ROSH] | Prior cesarean, TOLAC |
| Imaging | Transvaginal ultrasound localises the placenta | Ultrasound sensitivity poor; clinical | Free fluid on right upper quadrant POCUS |

- Cardiotocography detects the early fetal signs of abruption that pelvic and ultrasound examination miss [PEER-PH].
- Sudden fetal bradycardia is the single predictable tracing finding of uterine rupture [ROSH].
- Defer pelvic examination when previa or rupture is suspected — instrumentation can provoke severe exsanguination [ROSH].

### Postpartum complications

| Presentation | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Fever, tachycardia, foul-smelling lochia, uterine tenderness on day 2–3 | Endometritis [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Heavy bleeding 1 hour postpartum, soft boggy fundus | Uterine atony [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Bleeding 1 week postpartum, mild tachycardia | Retained placenta [PEER-PH] |
| Unilateral red painful swollen breast with fever and malaise | Lactational mastitis [ROSH][PEER-PH] |
| Fluctuance within mastitis | Breast abscess [PEER-PH] |
| Recurrent mastitis in a postmenopausal smoker | Periductal mastitis [PEER-PH] |
| Instability, hypoxia, oozing from IV sites after delivery | Amniotic fluid embolism with DIC [PEER-PH] |
| Postpartum hemorrhage then weakness, bradycardia, hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, low TSH | Sheehan syndrome [ROSH][PEER-PH] |

- Endometritis affects about 1 in 20 vaginal deliveries and 1 in 10 cesarean sections [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Amniotic fluid embolism with DIC: elevated D-dimer, thrombocytopenia, low fibrinogen, prolonged PT and aPTT [PEER-PH].

### Adnexal and prolapse emergencies
- Ovarian torsion: sudden severe pain often with vomiting; the most common ultrasound finding is an enlarged ovary on the affected side, and normal Doppler flow does not exclude torsion [ROSH].
- Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome: distention, fluid wave, hypotension, tachycardia, hemoconcentration, creatinine 2.0 mg/dL, sodium 130 mmol/L, potassium 5.5 mmol/L, and bilateral pleural effusions [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Uterine prolapse: pelvic pressure worsened by standing and relieved by lying down. First-degree lowers the cervix into the lower third of the vagina, second-degree protrudes it through the introitus, and third-degree (procidentia) externalises the uterus completely [PEER-PH].
- Endometriosis causes dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia, infertility, rectovaginal nodularity, and adnexal masses; thoracic involvement causes pneumothorax or hemothorax with chest pain, dyspnea, and reduced breath sounds coinciding with menstruation [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Uterine perforation with IUD translocation: severe pelvic pain and bleeding after placement, absent IUD string, and instability — obtain emergent pelvic ultrasound [PEER-PH].

## Management

### Pelvic inflammatory disease and tubo-ovarian abscess

| Scenario | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Outpatient PID (2021 CDC) | Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once, plus doxycycline 100 mg PO, plus metronidazole 500 mg PO twice daily — 14 days [PEER-PH] |
| Severe PID requiring admission | Cefotetan plus doxycycline — cefotetan covers N. gonorrhoeae and anaerobes, doxycycline covers C. trachomatis [PEER-PH] |
| Tubo-ovarian abscess | Ceftriaxone plus doxycycline plus metronidazole, at least 14 days [PEER-PH] |
| Alternative inpatient | Cefoxitin IV for 3 days with doxycycline PO for 14 days [ROSH] |

1. Admit if pregnant, severely ill with fever or vomiting, abscess or sepsis, unable to tolerate oral therapy, likely non-compliance, or no improvement within 72 hours [PEER-PH].
2. Admit tubo-ovarian abscess for at least 24 hours of IV antibiotics, and consult gynecology [PEER-PH][ROSH].
3. Leave an intrauterine device in place — insufficient evidence supports removal, and it does not change the regimen [PEER-PH].

### Cervicitis
- Treat empirically for C. trachomatis and N. gonorrhoeae without waiting for confirmation: ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once plus doxycycline 100 mg PO twice daily for 7 days; azithromycin replaces doxycycline in pregnancy [PEER-PH][ROSH].

### Vaginitis, herpes, and warts

| Diagnosis | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Bacterial vaginosis | Metronidazole 500 mg PO twice daily for 7 days; or clindamycin 2% cream nightly for 7 days; or metronidazole 0.75% gel daily for 5 days [PEER-PH] |
| Trichomoniasis | Metronidazole 500 mg PO twice daily for 7 days [ROSH] |
| Candidiasis | Clotrimazole 1% vaginal cream, vaginal tablets, or oral therapy [PEER-PH] |
| Genital herpes | Acyclovir; for recurrences, famciclovir 1,000 mg twice daily for 1 day [ROSH] |
| Herpetic urinary symptoms | Sitz baths to dilute urinary acidity, plus topical analgesia [PEER-PH] |
| Contact vulvovaginitis or retained tampon | Remove the irritant or the tampon [PEER-PH][ROSH] |

### Ectopic pregnancy and early pregnancy bleeding
1. Confirm pregnancy with a quantitative beta-hCG and pelvic ultrasound [ROSH].
2. Beta-hCG above the discriminatory zone with no intrauterine pregnancy is treated as ectopic until proven otherwise [PEER-PH].
3. Pregnancy of unknown location: repeat beta-hCG in 48 hours and document that the patient can comply with close follow-up [PEER-PH].
4. Unruptured ectopic with beta-hCG below 5,000 mIU/mL, no fetal heart activity, no rupture, and no kidney insufficiency: single-dose intramuscular methotrexate with follow-up hCG levels [ROSH].
5. Abdominal pain after methotrexate requires ultrasound to exclude rupture, with obstetrics consultation for likely treatment failure [PEER-PH].
6. Rupture, instability, or fetal heart activity mandates surgical management; free fluid in Morison pouch warrants immediate gynecology consultation [ROSH][PEER-PH].
7. Threatened abortion: pelvic rest and follow-up, with Rh D immune globulin for Rh-negative patients. Do not repeat Rh immune globulin when an anti-D Coombs titer is detectable or a recent dose is still circulating — its half-life is 24 days [ROSH].
8. An unstable patient in hemorrhagic shock receives transfusion and surgery under implied consent, documenting the severity, the inability to consent, and the inability to contact family [PEER-PH].

### Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy
1. Magnesium sulfate 4–6 g IV over 20 minutes, then 1–2 g/hr (2–3 g/hr in some protocols) [PEER-PH][ROSH].
2. Confirm the blood pressure, then treat severe hypertension with labetalol 20 mg IV; hydralazine, nifedipine, and nicardipine are also appropriate [ROSH].
3. Monitor for hypermagnesemia — drowsiness, bradycardia, hypotension, lost deep tendon reflexes, respiratory depression. Stop the infusion; intravenous calcium reverses the neuromuscular and cardiac effects [ROSH].
4. Admit for obstetric evaluation and consult obstetrics for induction of labour or delivery [ROSH].

### Third-trimester bleeding and trauma
1. Establish IV access and begin volume resuscitation for instability — circulating blood volume rises 40% to 50% in pregnancy, so instability represents considerable loss [PEER-PH].
2. Obtain pelvic ultrasound to localise the placenta before any digital examination; transvaginal ultrasound is superior and considered safe [PEER-PH][ROSH].
3. Apply cardiotocography, and withhold speculum and digital pelvic examination when placenta previa is suspected [PEER-PH][ROSH].
4. Consult obstetrics for delivery planning in previa and for suspected abruption, which is usually a clinical diagnosis [ROSH].
5. Give Rh(D) immune globulin empirically to any Rh-negative pregnant patient after blunt abdominal trauma — it prevents isoimmunization from fetomaternal hemorrhage, which can lead to fetal hydrops [PEER-PH].

### Intrapartum emergencies
1. Umbilical cord prolapse: manually elevate the presenting fetal part and leave the hand in the vagina, even during transport to the operating room, with emergent obstetric consultation. Cesarean section is the delivery method of choice for a viable infant; if it cannot be achieved in time, preserve umbilical circulation and prepare for rapid vaginal delivery [PEER-PH][ROSH].
2. Shoulder dystocia: McRoberts maneuver first — hyperflex and abduct the maternal hips, knees to chest — with downward suprapubic pressure, to slide the anterior shoulder beneath the pubic symphysis [PEER-PH].
3. If the head remains retracted against the perineum, identify the fetal posterior shoulder, flex the elbow, and sweep the arm across the chest [ROSH].
4. Nuchal cord: loop a loosely wrapped cord over the fetal head; if reduction fails, clamp and cut the cord and deliver expeditiously, since the infant no longer receives oxygen-rich blood from the placenta [ROSH][PEER-PH].
5. For a non-reassuring fetal heart tracing, use the lateral recumbent position. After delivery, wipe the infant's nose and mouth, dry promptly to reduce heat loss, and stimulate; routine suctioning is not performed unless there is airway obstruction [PEER-PH][ROSH].

### Prelabor rupture of membranes and intraamniotic infection
1. Sterile speculum examination: fluid pooling in the vaginal vault, a positive nitrazine test, and ferning on microscopy confirm rupture, with ferning the most specific [PEER-PH][ROSH].
2. Over 34 weeks, delivery is preferred; between 24 and 34 weeks, consider corticosteroids. Admit to obstetrics for delivery [ROSH][PEER-PH].
3. Intraamniotic infection — fever, maternal and fetal tachycardia, uterine tenderness — requires antibiotics and urgent induction of labour or delivery [ROSH].

### Postpartum hemorrhage
1. Cumulative loss of 1,000 mL or more, or bleeding with hypovolemia, within 24 hours of delivery [ROSH].
2. Two-handed uterine massage and aggressive resuscitation with OB/GYN consultation; a soft, boggy fundus indicates atony [PEER-PH][ROSH].
3. Give oxytocin IV. Exclude the other causes: uterine rupture, retained products, uterine inversion, lacerations, coagulopathy, and expanding hematoma; for uterine inversion, apply manual pressure on the mass toward the cervix with a clenched fist [ROSH].
4. For delayed hemorrhage from 24 hours to 6 weeks, obtain ultrasonography to detect retained tissue; curettage is indicated for removal [PEER-PH].

### Postpartum infection, mastitis, and thrombosis
- Endometritis: admit for broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics — clindamycin and gentamicin — and exclude retained placenta; mild outpatient disease may be treated with amoxicillin-clavulanate [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Lactational mastitis: NSAIDs and cool compresses with complete breast emptying, and continue breastfeeding from both breasts. Start antibiotics if symptoms do not improve within 12–24 hours or if systemic symptoms are present; first-line agents are cephalexin or dicloxacillin, clindamycin for beta-lactam sensitivity, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or clindamycin when MRSA is a concern [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Breast abscess: breast ultrasound, then aspiration under ultrasound guidance; add IV fluids and IV vancomycin for MRSA coverage when sepsis is present. Recurrent mastitis or nonlactating periductal mastitis needs breast surgeon follow-up within 1 month to exclude malignancy [PEER-PH].
- Postpartum pulmonary embolism while breastfeeding: enoxaparin 1 mg/kg subcutaneously or unfractionated heparin, avoiding dabigatran and rivaroxaban. Ergotamine is contraindicated while breastfeeding because it causes vomiting, diarrhea, and seizures in infants [PEER-PH].

### Gynaecologic procedures and other management
- Bartholin abscess: incision and drainage with a Word catheter, which stays 4–6 weeks to allow a tract to form, or marsupialisation; incise on the mucosal surface of the vestibule. Needle aspiration has a 38% recurrence rate. Send gonorrhoea and chlamydia testing [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- A painless Bartholin cyst smaller than 3 cm is managed expectantly; sitz baths and warm compresses may encourage drainage [PEER-PH].
- Ruptured hemorrhagic ovarian cyst with hemoperitoneum, stable vital signs, and no significant hemoglobin drop: admit for observation and serial hemoglobin and hematocrit. Abnormal vital signs or a falling hemoglobin suggest operative hemorrhage control. Ovarian torsion requires surgical intervention [PEER-PH].
- Avoid bimanual examination in ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome [ROSH].
- Abnormal uterine bleeding: base treatment on hemodynamic stability. Options include oral medroxyprogesterone three times daily for 7 days, or every 6 hours in adolescents, and conjugated estrogen for acute heavy bleeding; transfuse O Rh-negative blood when indicated [ROSH].
- Endometriosis: NSAIDs and hormonal contraceptives, with an estrogen-progestin oral contraceptive first-line for pelvic pain [ROSH][PEER-PH].
- Uterine prolapse: manual reduction and referral for pessary placement [ROSH].
- Hyperemesis: small protein-rich meals and ginger; pyridoxine is first-line and ondansetron is used when it fails. For dehydration give intravenous 5% dextrose in lactated Ringer solution or normal saline, plus thiamine to prevent Wernicke encephalopathy [PEER-PH][ROSH].
- Emergency contraception: a copper intrauterine device is the most effective, with a pregnancy risk of 0.1%, effective up to 5 days after unprotected intercourse, at any cycle point, and regardless of body mass index. Oral options include levonorgestrel, mifepristone, combined estrogen-progestin, and ulipristal acetate 30 mg with metoclopramide 10 mg [ROSH].
- After sexual assault, offer STI prophylaxis, postexposure hepatitis B vaccination, and postexposure HIV prophylaxis; obtain a pregnancy test on all female patients of reproductive age unless they have had a hysterectomy [ROSH][PEER-PH].
