## High-Yield Exam Review

- Septic shock is sepsis with persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors despite adequate fluid resuscitation, plus serum lactate greater than 2 mmol/L. [ROSH]
- Systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) requires two or more of: temperature greater than 38°C or less than 36°C; heart rate greater than 90 bpm; respiratory rate greater than 20/min or PaCO2 less than 32 mm Hg; white cell count greater than 12,000/mcL, less than 4,000/mcL, or greater than 10% immature forms (bandemia). [PEER-PH]
- SIRS is not specific to infection — heat stroke, pulmonary embolism, and recent surgery all produce it. [PEER-PH]
- qSOFA has three components: altered mental status (Glasgow Coma Scale [GCS] ≤14), tachypnea (respiratory rate ≥22/min), and hypotension (systolic blood pressure ≤100 mm Hg). Two or more points should prompt consideration of sepsis. [PEER-PH]
- Resuscitate sepsis with decreased end-organ perfusion using 30 mL/kg of crystalloid within the first 3 hours. [PEER-PH]
- Balanced crystalloid such as lactated Ringer is preferred over normal saline for patients needing multiple liters. [PEER-PH]
- Norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor; start within 6 hours if hypotension persists after adequate fluid resuscitation. Mortality increases by 5.3% for every hour norepinephrine is delayed. [PEER-PH]
- Titrate vasopressors to a mean arterial pressure (MAP) greater than 65 mm Hg. [PEER-PH]
- Stress-dose glucocorticoids are adjunctive, reserved for septic shock that persists despite fluids and vasopressors. [PEER-PH]
- Transfuse packed red blood cells in an otherwise healthy septic adult at a hemoglobin less than 7 g/dL. [PEER-PH]
- Broad-spectrum sepsis coverage pairs an antipseudomonal beta-lactam (piperacillin-tazobactam, cefepime, or meropenem) with vancomycin when Pseudomonas or multidrug-resistant organisms are a concern; ceftriaxone plus vancomycin is adequate otherwise. [PEER-PH]
- Carbapenems such as imipenem and meropenem retain activity against extended-spectrum beta-lactamase (ESBL) organisms. [PEER-PH]
- Hand hygiene adherence among health care workers is the most effective single measure to prevent transmission of multidrug-resistant organisms. [ROSH]
- Full-term febrile infants up to 21 days of age require a lumbar puncture; empiric therapy is ampicillin plus gentamicin or ceftazidime. [PEER-PH]
- Chorioamnionitis is a major risk factor for early neonatal sepsis, which occurs within 7 days of birth. [PEER-PH]
- Empiric therapy for bacterial meningitis in an adult is ceftriaxone plus vancomycin; dexamethasone is added to reduce hearing loss and other neurologic sequelae. [ROSH]
- Close contacts of meningococcal meningitis receive prophylaxis with ciprofloxacin, rifampin, or ceftriaxone. [PEER-PH]
- Both staphylococcal and streptococcal toxic shock syndrome are caused by toxin-producing gram-positive organisms; clindamycin is given to inhibit exotoxin production. [PEER-PH]
- Necrotizing soft tissue infection requires broad-spectrum antibiotics plus emergent operative debridement — antibiotics alone are never sufficient. [PEER-PH]
- Botulism presents with bilateral cranial neuropathies and symmetric descending paralysis with normal mental status and sensation. [PEER-PH]
- Botulinum antitoxin is given on clinical suspicion alone, without waiting for confirmatory testing. [PEER-PH]
- Tetanus immune globulin neutralizes only unbound circulating toxin; it cannot reverse toxin already bound to tissue. [PEER-PH]
- Rocky Mountain spotted fever (RMSF) is treated with doxycycline in all age groups, started within 5 days of symptom onset without waiting for confirmatory testing. [PEER-PH]
- Malaria is confirmed with thick and thin blood smears; three negative sets 12 hours apart are required to exclude it. [PEER-PH]
- Tuberculin skin test induration of 10 mm or greater is positive in a patient with diabetes; 5 mm or greater is positive in a patient with HIV. [PEER-PH]
- Pregnant patients with varicella pneumonia are admitted and treated with intravenous acyclovir. [PEER-PH]

## Core Concepts

### Sepsis as a Syndrome, Not a Diagnosis

- Sepsis is a syndrome of dysregulated host response to an infectious agent, producing end-organ dysfunction; it is the most common cause of death in medical and traumatic intensive care units worldwide. [ROSH]
- There is no gold standard definition; the SIRS criteria and the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score are the tools used in practice. [ROSH]
- SIRS plus a known infectious source classically identifies sepsis, but the same criteria are met by non-infectious insults. [PEER-PH]
- Pediatric septic shock is defined as suspected infection with at least 1 point on the Phoenix Sepsis Score indicating cardiovascular dysfunction. [ROSH]
- Hypotension and lethargy are late findings in pediatric sepsis, which is why early recognition is difficult and resuscitation must not wait for them. [ROSH]

### Fluid and Vasoactive Strategy

- Crystalloid is the resuscitative fluid of choice over albumin; balanced solutions are associated with a slightly lower likelihood of kidney injury and possibly a mortality advantage. [PEER-PH]
- Vasopressors are indicated only after fluid resuscitation has failed — refractory hypotension after a rapid 30 mL/kg bolus. [ROSH]
- The vasopressor sequence in septic shock is norepinephrine, then epinephrine, then vasopressin, with phenylephrine as a last-line agent. [ROSH]
- Higher MAP targets than 65 mm Hg do not improve mortality and increase adverse events such as tachyarrhythmias; patients 75 years and older may have increased mortality with higher targets. [PEER-PH]

### Empiric Antimicrobial Selection

- Broad-spectrum means activity against common gram-positive and gram-negative organisms, including methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA). [PEER-PH]
- Risk factors for Pseudomonas or multidrug-resistant organisms should push the beta-lactam choice toward an antipseudomonal agent. [PEER-PH]
- For ESBL-producing organisms, carbapenems outperform piperacillin-tazobactam and cefepime for sepsis and bacteremia. [PEER-PH]
- Procalcitonin is more sensitive and specific for bacterial infection than C-reactive protein (CRP) or leukocyte count, but it is elevated by malignancy, trauma, surgery, pancreatitis, and non-septic shock. [PEER-PH]
- Coagulase-negative Staphylococcus growing in blood culture is most likely a contaminant. [ROSH]

### Fever in the Young Infant

- The 2021 American Academy of Pediatrics guidance covers febrile infants 8–60 days old with a rectal temperature of 38°C or greater, and splits them into 8–21 days, 22–28 days, and 29–60 days because the risk of serious bacterial infection falls with age. [PEER-PH]
- Infants 8–21 days old with no evident source need a full septic workup including urinalysis, blood culture, and lumbar puncture. [ROSH]
- Escherichia coli, group B Streptococcus, Staphylococcus aureus, Listeria monocytogenes (rare), and enterovirus are the main pathogens in this age group. [ROSH]
- Inflammatory markers used in risk stratification include procalcitonin greater than 0.5 ng/mL, CRP greater than 20 mg/L, and absolute neutrophil count thresholds of greater than 4,000/mcL or greater than 5,200/mcL depending on procalcitonin availability. [ROSH]
- Well-appearing febrile infants 29–90 days old with no high-risk features need only urinalysis and urine culture, because urinary tract infection is the most common bacterial infection at that age. [PEER-PH]
- Rectal measurement is the most reliable route; axillary, temporal, and tympanic readings are positive only above 38°C, and bundling should never be accepted as the cause of a fever. [ROSH]

### Meningitis and Meningococcemia

- Streptococcus pneumoniae, Neisseria meningitidis, and Haemophilus influenzae are the most common causes of bacterial meningitis in adults; N. meningitidis is the most common in young adults and children. [ROSH]
- The classic triad of fever, nuchal rigidity, and altered mental status is uncommon as a complete set, though each element is common. [PEER-PH]
- Risk factors for meningococcal disease include communal living (college dormitories, military barracks), extremes of age, young adults 16–21 years, complement deficiency, asplenia, and close contact with an infected person. [PEER-PH]
- Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome — adrenal infarction causing adrenal insufficiency — is a feared complication of meningococcemia. [ROSH]

### Toxin-Mediated Systemic Illness

- Staphylococcal toxic shock syndrome (TSS) is mediated by toxic shock syndrome toxin-1 (TSST-1) and enterotoxin type B; streptococcal TSS is mediated by streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins. [PEER-PH]
- These superantigens cause fever, massive systemic inflammation, increased vascular permeability, hemodynamic instability, coagulopathy, and multiorgan failure. [PEER-PH]
- Staphylococcal TSS progresses rapidly, usually within 48 hours of bacteria entering the bloodstream, and the rash may involve the palms and soles. [PEER-PH]
- Retained vaginal foreign bodies, nasal packing, and recent sinus surgery are recognised triggers. [ROSH]

### Tuberculosis

- One-third of the world's population is infected; tuberculosis is the leading infectious cause of death worldwide. [ROSH]
- Transmission is airborne via respiratory particles, and the organism can remain dormant for years in granulomatous form. [ROSH]
- HIV infection is the single most significant risk factor; HIV-positive patients are 20% to 30% more likely to develop tuberculosis, and latent infection reactivates far more readily. [PEER-PH]
- Other high-risk groups are recent immigrants from high-incidence countries, people who inject drugs, and those living or working in congregate settings such as prisons, drug treatment facilities, nursing homes, and homeless shelters. [PEER-PH]
- Painless lymphadenitis (scrofula) is the most common extrapulmonary manifestation. [ROSH]

### HIV and Opportunistic Infection

- Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) is a retrovirus with an RNA genome whose hallmark effect is destruction of CD4+ T cells, primarily impairing cell-mediated immunity. [ROSH]
- Acute retroviral syndrome occurs 2–4 weeks after exposure and presents with fever, myalgias, fatigue, nonexudative pharyngitis, diarrhoea, headache, and diffuse adenopathy, plus painful mucosal ulcers and a maculopapular rash that can involve the palms and soles. [PEER-PH]
- Fourth-generation HIV antigen/antibody tests detect p24 antigen or HIV antibodies beginning 2 weeks after exposure. [PEER-PH]
- With medication compliance, normal CD4 counts, and undetectable viral loads, life expectancy is near normal. [PEER-PH]
- Several infections are effectively markers of advanced immunosuppression: cryptococcal meningitis and neurotoxoplasmosis below a CD4 count of 100 cells/mm3, disseminated Mycobacterium avium complex below 50 cells/µL, and Pneumocystis pneumonia. [PEER-PH]

### Vaccine-Preventable Exanthems

- Measles (rubeola) is an extremely contagious RNA virus with an incubation period of 7–21 days; the prodrome lasts about 3 days and features fever, malaise, and the 3 Cs — cough, coryza, and conjunctivitis. [PEER-PH]
- Koplik spots — white spots on the buccal mucosa, usually opposite the first and second upper molars — are pathognomonic and appear 2–3 days after symptom onset. [PEER-PH]
- Rubella is a single-stranded RNA togavirus with an incubation period of about 14–18 days; illness is generally mild, and polyarticular joint pain is a common accompaniment in young children. [PEER-PH]
- Roseola infantum (sixth disease) is caused by human herpesvirus 6 and affects children 6 months to 3 years old; a high fever lasting 3–5 days resolves abruptly and the rash appears as the fever breaks. [ROSH]
- Erythema infectiosum (fifth disease) is caused by parvovirus B19; the slapped-cheek rash appears as the viremic phase resolves, followed 1–4 days later by a lacy reticular rash on the trunk and extremities. [ROSH]
- Varicella vesicles appear in different stages of development, while smallpox lesions are uniform and appear at the same stage — the single most useful discriminator. [PEER-PH]
