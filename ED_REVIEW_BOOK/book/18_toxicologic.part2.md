## Pathophysiology

### Ethanol and alcoholic ketoacidosis

- Chronic users saturate alcohol dehydrogenase, so ethanol follows zero-order kinetics — a fixed amount per hour — and tolerance lets them appear sober at higher levels. [PEER-PH]
- Alcoholic ketoacidosis occurs in undernourished alcoholics after a binge, with abdominal pain and vomiting; a normal mental status despite severe acidosis distinguishes it from toxic alcohol ingestion. [PEER-PH]
- In young children ethanol causes coma and hypoglycemia 2–10 hours after ingestion, with hypothermia and hypoventilation, through decreased gluconeogenesis. [ROSH]

### Ethylene glycol

- The parent alcohol is minimally toxic; the metabolites cause damage. Glycolic acid is the major metabolite, with glyoxylic and oxalic acid also formed. [ROSH]
- Oxalic acid binds calcium to form calcium oxalate crystals in the renal tubules, causing tubular necrosis and kidney failure. [ROSH]
- It produces a profound anion gap metabolic acidosis; the osmolar gap is highest early, before metabolism, and the anion gap rises as toxic acids accumulate. A lactate gap is characteristic, because glycolate is falsely read as lactate by blood gas analyzers using lactate oxidase while serum lactate is unaffected. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Fomepizole inhibits alcohol dehydrogenase; thiamine and pyridoxine divert intermediates into less harmful compounds. [ROSH]

### Methanol

- Methanol is metabolized by alcohol dehydrogenase to formaldehyde and then by aldehyde dehydrogenase to formic acid, which is toxic to the optic nerve; optic disc hyperemia and retinal edema are specific findings. [ROSH]
- It raises the osmolar gap while its metabolites produce an anion gap metabolic acidosis. Symptoms may be delayed 12–24 hours, longer if ethanol is coingested because ethanol competitively inhibits methanol metabolism. Fomepizole prevents further metabolism; hemodialysis removes formic acid and corrects acidemia. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Isopropyl alcohol

- It is the second most commonly ingested alcohol after ethanol, twice as potent with two to four times the duration; peak concentration is at 30–120 minutes and symptoms begin within 1 hour. [ROSH]
- Alcohol dehydrogenase converts most of it to acetone. The signature is ketosis without acidosis: a normal anion gap with ketonemia and an osmolar gap. Fomepizole has no role. [PEER-PH][ROSH]

### Acetaminophen

- At therapeutic levels acetaminophen forms harmless urinary metabolites. After large or repeated supratherapeutic ingestions, N-acetyl-p-benzoquinoneimine (NAPQI) overwhelms glutathione stores and causes hepatocellular death. [ROSH]
- N-acetylcysteine regenerates hepatic glutathione and acts as a sulfhydryl donor. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Nausea and vomiting with unremarkable vital signs occur in the first 24 hours; right upper quadrant pain with abnormal hepatic enzymes, bilirubin and coagulation factors follow after 24 hours; hepatic failure with coagulopathy, hypoglycemia, jaundice, cerebral edema, anuria and acidosis develops at 48–96 hours, with death typically at 72–96 hours. [ROSH]
- The toxic dose is > 150 mg/kg in children or > 10 g in adults; > 250 mg/kg or > 12 g in 24 hours is likely toxic. The Rumack-Matthew nomogram is valid only for acute single ingestion; in subacute or chronic overdose give N-acetylcysteine guided by the level, liver function tests and clinical status. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Salicylates

- Salicylates act on the medullary respiratory centers, causing hyperventilation and a respiratory alkalosis — the earliest acid-base abnormality — followed by an anion gap metabolic acidosis. [ROSH]
- Uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation causes anaerobic metabolism and lactic acid generation; the same uncoupling with heat release produces the hyperthermia. Acidemia increases the unionized fraction, which crosses cell membranes freely and worsens toxicity. [ROSH]

### Carbon monoxide

- Formed by incomplete combustion of carbonaceous fuel, it is the leading cause of poisoning morbidity and mortality, binding hemoglobin with an affinity more than 200 times that of oxygen. [ROSH]
- Carboxyhemoglobin cannot carry oxygen and shifts the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve to the left; carbon monoxide also inhibits cytochrome oxidase and causes endothelial dysfunction. Cherry-red skin is often not seen in living patients, and bilateral basal ganglia hypodensities are associated with poisoning. [ROSH]
- Methylene chloride is metabolized to carbon monoxide, delayed 4–8 hours, with a half-life of about 13 hours. [PEER-PH]

### Cyanide

- Cyanide binds mitochondrial cytochrome oxidase, disrupting the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation; tissues with high oxygen consumption — heart, lungs and brain — are most susceptible. It causes profound lactic acidosis, and venous oxygen content is high because cells cannot extract oxygen. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Sources include combustion of plastics, polymers, wool, nylon and silk; cassava root; stone fruit pits containing amygdalin; nitroprusside; and acetonitrile. Aerosolized hydrogen cyanide has an odor of almonds. [ROSH]
- Hydroxocobalamin binds cyanide to cyanocobalamin; the reddish plasma discoloration comes from hydroxocobalamin, not cyanide. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Methemoglobinemia

- Iron in hemoglobin is oxidized from the ferrous (Fe2+) to the ferric (Fe3+) state, preventing oxygen binding. Congenital methemoglobinemia causes lifelong cyanosis in otherwise asymptomatic patients. [ROSH]
- Methylene blue reduces methemoglobin to hemoglobin through the glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase pathway. Oxygen raises the arterial partial pressure of oxygen but does not correct the cyanosis or the pulse oximetry reading. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Cardiac glycosides

- Digoxin inhibits the sodium-potassium-ATPase, raising intracellular sodium, which impairs the sodium-calcium exchanger and raises intracellular calcium, increasing inotropy and automaticity. It also increases vagal tone, causing sinus bradycardia, atrioventricular nodal dysfunction and high-degree atrioventricular block, and can cause hyperkalemia. [ROSH]
- Nerium oleander and Thevetia peruviana (yellow oleander) contain cardiac glycosides that inhibit the same pump. [ROSH]

### Beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers

- Beta-blockers reduce inotropy, dromotropy and chronotropy and, through beta-2 effects, cause vasodilation; peak effect is at 1–6 hours. Bradycardia is the most common overdose sign, followed by hypotension and altered consciousness. [ROSH]
- Beta-2 blockade prevents gluconeogenesis, so hypoglycemia marks beta-blocker overdose; calcium channel blockers cause hyperglycemia by blocking L-type calcium channels on pancreatic beta cells. [PEER-PH]
- Sotalol adds potassium channel blockade (class III), prolonging the QT and risking torsades de pointes. Propranolol is unique in causing sodium channel blockade with seizures, QRS widening, hypotension and bradycardia. [PEER-PH]
- Calcium channel blockers antagonize slow calcium channels in myocardium and smooth muscle, causing vasodilation, reduced contractility, sinoatrial node depression and slowed atrioventricular conduction; verapamil is the most deadly. Dihydropyridines such as amlodipine cause vasoplegic shock with compensatory tachycardia. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Sulfonylureas

- Sulfonylureas promote insulin release from beta-pancreatic cells; the effect lasts up to 24 hours, allowing once-daily dosing, and hypoglycemia can occur without overdose. Risk factors include poor oral intake, exercise, alcohol, overdose, kidney impairment and longer-acting agents such as glyburide. [ROSH]
- Octreotide, a somatostatin analog, antagonizes insulin release and is first-line for sulfonylurea poisoning; diazoxide is an alternative and glucagon is less effective. [PEER-PH]

### Tricyclic antidepressants

- These agents antagonize sodium channels, acetylcholine receptors, peripheral alpha-1 receptors, histamine receptors and gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) receptors. Sodium channel blockade widens the QRS, alpha-1 blockade causes hypotension, antimuscarinic effects cause tachycardia and dry skin, and potassium blockade prolongs the QT. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Seizures arise from increased synaptic monoamines, sodium channel inhibition and GABA antagonism. The three Cs are coma, convulsions and cardiac dysrhythmia. [ROSH]

### Serotonin syndrome

- It follows excess serotonin at the synaptic cleft, thought to involve excessive serotonin type 2A receptor stimulation with type 1A receptors contributing. It occurs with selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants and monoamine oxidase inhibitors. [ROSH]
- The triad is autonomic instability, altered mental status and increased neuromuscular activity, developing within minutes to hours of exposure. [ROSH]
- Fluoxetine has a long half-life and active metabolites, so a 6-week washout precedes a monoamine oxidase inhibitor. [ROSH]

### Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

- NMS is hyperthermia, muscular rigidity and autonomic dysregulation with antipsychotics, through dopamine depletion from phenothiazines or typical antipsychotics, or withdrawal of dopaminergic drugs such as levodopa; abrupt cessation of antiparkinsonian agents can also trigger it. [ROSH]
- High-potency first-generation agents such as haloperidol and fluphenazine are most often implicated, but risperidone, clozapine, olanzapine and antiemetics such as metoclopramide and promethazine are all described. NMS affects 0.02–3% of treated patients, usually within the first 2 weeks. [ROSH]
- Creatine phosphokinase elevation is typical. Hyporeflexia and lead-pipe rigidity with absent clonus separate NMS from serotonin syndrome. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Cholinergic crisis

- Organophosphates bind acetylcholinesterase and block acetylcholine breakdown, producing nicotinic and muscarinic effects; both ganglia types are stimulated, so the heart rate may be fast or slow, and nicotinic stimulation causes fasciculations, weakness and paralysis. [ROSH]
- Carbamates cause the same toxidrome; pralidoxime prevents aging of the inhibited cholinesterase. Rivastigmine, a transdermal acetylcholinesterase inhibitor used in Parkinson and Alzheimer dementia, causes cholinergic toxicity at excessive levels. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Nicotine mimics acetylcholine at nicotinic receptors, altering brain and spinal cord sodium and calcium channels. Ingestion is biphasic, stimulation then depression, with onset delayed about an hour by poor gastric absorption. [ROSH]

### Metals

- Iron is corrosive to the gastrointestinal mucosa. Stage I (30 minutes to 6 hours) is vomiting and diarrhea; stage II, the latent stage at 6–24 hours, brings transient resolution of gastrointestinal symptoms while organ damage continues; stage III is shock, acidosis and hepatic failure; stage IV is pyloric stenosis. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Lead causes anemia, basophilic stippling and lead lines, confirmed by a blood lead level > 5 mcg/dL. [ROSH]

### Hydrocarbons, marine and fungal toxins

- Hydrocarbons are aspirated because of low viscosity, low surface tension and volatility; ingestions greater than 30 mL carry high risk through regurgitation. In the lung they cause aspiration pneumonitis by destroying alveolar lipid membranes, and they sensitize the myocardium to catecholamines, underlying sudden sniffing death. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Ciguatoxin is a neurotoxin concentrated in larger fish such as barracuda, grouper and red snapper; it is odorless, colorless and tasteless, not deactivated by heat, and causes symptoms 2–30 hours after ingestion. [ROSH]
- Amanita phalloides contains amatoxin, which inhibits RNA polymerase I and II in the liver; its first latent stage lasts up to 24 hours with no signs or symptoms. [ROSH]

### Toxicities of therapy

- Propylene glycol, the diluent in parenteral diazepam and lorazepam, causes anion gap acidosis and lactic acidosis with large or continuous infusions. [PEER-PH]
- Amiodarone is 37% organic iodine by weight and can raise iodine levels 10–20 times the normal dietary requirement, precipitating hyperthyroidism and thyroid storm; it blocks the delayed rectifier potassium current and enhances warfarin anticoagulation through P-glycoprotein. [ROSH]
- Ethambutol causes dose-dependent optic neuropathy with decreased visual acuity, scotomata, red-green color blindness or visual field defects. Linezolid is a nonspecific monoamine oxidase inhibitor, causing serotonin toxicity with selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and hypertensive crisis with sympathomimetics or tyramine-rich foods. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Tramadol inhibits serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake and has an active metabolite, O-desmethyltramadol, a mu-opioid receptor agonist; seizures occur at therapeutic and supratherapeutic doses and seem to be precipitated by naloxone. [ROSH]
