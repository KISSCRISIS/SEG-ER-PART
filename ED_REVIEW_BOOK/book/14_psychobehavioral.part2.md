## Pathophysiology

### Alcohol Withdrawal

- Chronic ethanol enhances inhibitory GABA-A transmission and inhibits excitatory NMDA glutamate receptors. Abrupt cessation leaves GABA-A downregulated and NMDA upregulated, producing central nervous system hyperexcitability.
- The hyperexcitable state is expressed as tremor, autonomic hyperactivity, hallucinations, withdrawal seizures and delirium tremens. Symptoms begin when the sedative effect of the last drink wears off.

### Thiamine Deficiency and Alcohol-Related Organ Injury

- Thiamine (vitamin B1) is a cofactor for enzymes of carbohydrate metabolism. Deficiency is common in chronic alcohol use because of poor intake, reduced absorption and impaired hepatic storage.
- Wernicke encephalopathy is the acute neurologic syndrome of deficiency; untreated or undertreated it progresses to Korsakoff syndrome with irreversible memory impairment.
- Hypomagnesaemia commonly accompanies alcohol-related thiamine deficiency and makes the deficiency resistant to replacement until the magnesium is corrected. [PEER-PH]
- High-output cardiac failure with peripheral vasodilatation (wet beriberi) is the cardiovascular expression of the same deficiency.
- Sustained heavy intake — on the order of more than 80 g of ethanol daily for 5 years or longer — produces a dilated cardiomyopathy with reduced ejection fraction. [PEER-PH]

### Sedative and Antidote Pharmacology

- Benzodiazepines increase the frequency of GABA-A chloride channel opening; barbiturates such as phenobarbital increase the duration of opening, giving a different and longer-lasting effect at the same receptor. [PEER-CS]
- Because barbiturates act at a different site on the same channel complex from benzodiazepines, their depressant effect is not fully reversed by a benzodiazepine antagonist.

### Opioid Agonists, Partial Agonists and Antagonists

- Buprenorphine is a partial mu-opioid agonist with a ceiling effect on sedation and respiratory depression. Its receptor affinity is roughly 1,000 times that of full agonists, so it displaces methadone and other full agonists and can precipitate withdrawal if given too early. [PEER-CS]
- Methadone is a full mu-opioid agonist used for maintenance; it prolongs the QT interval by interfering with extracellular potassium handling, so QT must be monitored. [PEER-CS]
- Loss of mu-opioid agonism in withdrawal releases noradrenergic activity in the brainstem, driving mydriasis, lacrimation, rhinorrhoea, piloerection, diaphoresis, myalgias, cramping and diarrhoea.

### Medications for Alcohol Use Disorder

- Naltrexone is a mu-opioid antagonist. It blocks the dopamine-mediated reward produced by drinking, which is the basis of its effect on craving and heavy drinking. [PEER-PH]
- Acamprosate acts on glutamate transmission as an NMDA receptor antagonist and supports abstinence maintenance. [PEER-PH]
- Disulfiram inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase, so acetaldehyde accumulates after any alcohol exposure and produces flushing, nausea, vomiting, tachycardia and hypotension.

### Sympathomimetic and Hallucinogen Toxicity

- Cocaine and amphetamines increase synaptic catecholamines; the resulting excess produces hypertension, tachycardia, hyperthermia, mydriasis, agitation and, in predisposed users, psychosis and seizures.
- Methamphetamine causes a greater central serotonin excess than other stimulants, and it is this serotonin effect that is linked to the characteristic psychotic picture.
- Phencyclidine is an NMDA receptor antagonist; its disinhibition and hallucinations arise from blockade of that receptor, and it is associated with seizures and violent agitation. [PEER-PH]
- Sedation with a benzodiazepine is the treatment for sympathomimetic and phencyclidine agitation because it lowers the excess sympathetic drive without lowering the seizure threshold.

### Toxic Alcohols and Carbon Monoxide

- Methanol is metabolised to formic acid; ethylene glycol to glycolic acid and then oxalic acid, which binds calcium to form calcium oxalate crystals in the kidney and other tissues; isopropanol to acetone. Toxic alcohol poisoning therefore produces a high anion gap metabolic acidosis with an osmolar gap that closes as the parent alcohol is metabolised.
- Carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin with roughly 200 times the affinity of oxygen and shifts the oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve to the left. Tissue oxygen delivery falls even when the partial pressure of oxygen on a blood gas is normal. [PEER-CS]

### Salicylate and Iron

- Salicylate directly stimulates the respiratory centre early, producing respiratory alkalosis; it then disrupts the citric acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation, producing a high anion gap metabolic acidosis with lactic acid accumulation, plus increased capillary permeability. Mixed respiratory alkalosis with metabolic acidosis is the classic pattern.
- Free circulating iron is directly corrosive and disrupts mitochondrial function. Deferoxamine chelates ferric iron to form ferrioxamine, which is excreted in urine. [PEER-CS]

### Psychosis

- Schizophrenia is attributed to dysregulation of dopamine transmission: excess dopaminergic activity in mesolimbic pathways generates positive symptoms, while cortical dopaminergic deficit contributes to negative symptoms.
- Antipsychotic effect follows dopamine D2 receptor blockade in mesolimbic pathways. Blockade in the nigrostriatal pathway produces extrapyramidal symptoms; blockade elsewhere in the brain underlies the other adverse effects of the class. [PEER-CS]
- Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis is an autoimmune disorder in which antibodies target the NMDA receptor; the resulting glutamatergic dysfunction produces psychosis and seizures in a young patient, frequently a woman, after a viral-like prodrome. [PEER-CS]
- Isoniazid inhibits the enzyme that converts pyridoxine to its active form, producing a functional GABA deficiency that predisposes to seizures. [PEER-CS]

### Mood, Anxiety and Stress

- Major depressive disorder is understood as a disorder of neurotransmitter balance in limbic circuits, and antidepressant response is delayed because downstream adaptive changes take weeks to develop. That delay, rather than drug absorption, explains the 4–6 weeks needed for sertraline and similar agents to work. [ROSH]
- Anxiety disorders are attributed to neurotransmitter imbalance combined with increased sympathetic nervous system activity, which explains the autonomic features of a panic attack. [PEER-PH]
- Panic attacks are self-limited because the sympathetic surge is not sustained; this is why an attack peaks within about 10 minutes and resolves within an hour. [PEER-PH]
- Steroid-induced psychosis is a direct central effect of exogenous corticosteroid on limbic and dopaminergic systems; it appears early in treatment.
- Discontinuation of a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor causes an acute fall in synaptic serotonin, producing the discontinuation syndrome. [PEER-CS]

### Eating Disorders

- Self-induced vomiting and laxative abuse remove gastric hydrochloric acid and hydrogen ions, producing a hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis. [PEER-CS]
- Starvation in anorexia nervosa causes loss of cardiac muscle mass together with increased vagal tone, producing bradycardia, hypotension and a prolonged QT interval that predisposes to arrhythmia.
- Refeeding syndrome is the consequence of restarting carbohydrate too quickly: the resulting insulin surge drives phosphate, potassium and magnesium into cells, and the pre-existing total body depletion becomes an acute, severe hypophosphataemia through increased phosphate use for adenosine triphosphate production. [ROSH]

### Delirium and Dementia

- Delirium reflects an acute, reversible disturbance of cerebral function in a patient with little reserve, triggered by systemic illness, drugs or environmental disruption. It fluctuates and is worse at night because arousal and sensory input vary.
- Alzheimer disease reflects progressive loss of cholinergic innervation, which is why anticholinergic drugs worsen cognition in these patients.
- Lewy body dementia is defined pathologically by alpha-synuclein deposits and clinically by visual hallucinations, parkinsonian features and fluctuating cognition.
- Frontotemporal dementia affects the frontal and temporal lobes, producing disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy and hyperorality rather than an early memory deficit.

### Opioid and Sedative Intoxication

- Mu-opioid receptor agonism produces miosis, respiratory depression and depressed mental status; the respiratory depression, not the sedation, is what kills.
- Tolerance to respiratory depression develops more slowly than tolerance to euphoria, so a user who returns to a previously tolerated dose after a period of abstinence is at high risk of fatal respiratory depression.
- Alcohol and benzodiazepines act additively with opioids at the level of central respiratory control, so co-ingestion converts a survivable dose into a fatal one.

### Mechanism of Injury in Abuse and Neglect

- Inflicted injury patterns follow the mechanism applied: forceful pulling or twisting of a limb produces metaphyseal corner fractures, and anteroposterior compression of the chest produces posterior rib fractures. [ROSH]
- Bruising in a child who is not yet mobile requires a mechanism to explain it, which is why bruising in a child under 6 months carries a very high likelihood of intentional trauma. [ROSH]
- Neglect, rather than a single violent act, is the most common form of child maltreatment, and it produces harm through omission — unmet food, medical, supervisory and emotional needs. [ROSH]
- Elder mistreatment is driven by caregiver burden and dependency, occurs most often in the home, and psychological and emotional abuse is its most prevalent form. [ROSH]

### Grief and Occupational Stress

- Grief is a normal adaptive process that resolves over months; it becomes complicated grief when it prevents return to normal life beyond roughly 6 to 12 months. [PEER-PH]
- Acute stress disorder and PTSD share the same traumatic origin but differ in duration: acute stress disorder resolves within 1 month, and PTSD persists beyond it. [PEER-PH]
- Fearful, replaying nightmares are a feature of PTSD because the memory is stored with the fear response attached, whereas grief produces sad dreams that trigger sadness rather than fear. [PEER-PH]
