## High-Yield Exam Review

- Avascular necrosis (AVN) is most common in the femoral head; in sickle cell disease the lifetime prevalence of femoral head AVN reaches 50%, and up to 20% of these patients also develop humeral head AVN. [PEER-PH]
- MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) is the gold standard for AVN and is up to 100% sensitive; plain films may be normal early in the disease. [PEER-PH]
- AVN risk factors: femoral neck fracture, hip dislocation, sickle cell disease, Gaucher disease, decompression disease, radiation, heavy alcohol use, and glucocorticoid therapy — including prednisone and even short courses. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Proximal pole scaphoid fractures carry the greatest AVN risk because the arterial supply enters the distal scaphoid; a suspected scaphoid injury is immobilised in a thumb spica splint even when imaging is negative. [PEER-PH]
- Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease is idiopathic AVN of the paediatric femoral head; radiographs show sclerosis, irregularity, and flattening of the femoral head.
- Osteomyelitis in children is almost always from haematogenous spread, involves the metaphysis of long bones, and is treated with 4 to 6 weeks of intravenous antibiotics; surgery is reserved for abscess or antibiotic failure. [PEER-PH]
- Staphylococcus aureus is the most common cause of osteomyelitis overall; Salmonella predominates in adults with sickle cell disease; Pseudomonas aeruginosa causes about 90% of osteomyelitis after a plantar puncture wound through the sole of a shoe; Fusobacterium with Eikenella corrodens indicates a human bite. [PEER-PH]
- MRI with and without contrast is the most accurate test for osteomyelitis (95% sensitive); plain film is the least sensitive — 40% to 50% bone loss is needed before lucency appears, and periosteal elevation takes 14 to 21 days. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Suspected diabetic foot osteomyelitis in a febrile patient is polymicrobial and is treated with vancomycin plus meropenem. [PEER-PH]
- Discitis and vertebral osteomyelitis are usually caused by S. aureus (over 50%), spread haematogenously — endocarditis is a common source — and the erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) is elevated in 90% of cases while the white blood cell (WBC) count is often normal. [PEER-PH]
- MRI with contrast is the imaging modality of choice for discitis, and empiric antimicrobial coverage is vancomycin-based.
- Fever with low back pain is a red flag for epidural abscess, osteomyelitis, or discitis — fever is present in only one-third of epidural abscesses and neurological deficit in only half. [PEER-CS]
- Disc herniation is the most common cause of sciatica and the most common cause of cauda equina syndrome (CES); the L5 nerve root is the most commonly affected root, and L5-S1 the most common herniation level. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Urinary retention is the most common and most sensitive finding in CES (sensitivity about 90%, specificity 95%). Postvoid residual is the bedside test: normal is under 30 mL, and above 100 mL indicates a denervated bladder. [PEER-PH]
- CES is imaged with MRI and treated with surgical decompression; absence of upper motor neuron signs distinguishes it from conus medullaris syndrome. [PEER-PH]
- Septic arthritis arises from haematogenous seeding in about 70% of cases; S. aureus is the most common organism overall, and Neisseria gonorrhoeae is the most common cause in sexually active adolescents and young adults. Staphylococci and streptococci account for up to 91% of cases. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- Synovial fluid with more than 50,000 WBC/mm³ and more than 90% polymorphonuclear cells (PMNs) indicates septic arthritis, and low synovial glucose supports it; septic arthritis can still be present with counts below 50,000. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Gout is confirmed by negatively birefringent, needle-shaped monosodium urate crystals; the first metatarsophalangeal joint is affected in 75% to 90% of cases (podagra). [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Pseudogout shows positively birefringent, rhomboid calcium pyrophosphate crystals with chondrocalcinosis on x-ray, and most commonly affects the knee. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Acute gout is treated with a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) such as ibuprofen, naproxen with topical ice, or indomethacin; prednisone 30 to 40 mg daily for 5 days is used when chronic kidney disease precludes NSAIDs. Allopurinol is not started during an acute attack. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Advanced age confers the highest risk of osteoporotic fracture; vertebral compression fractures are the most common osteoporotic fracture (about 45%) and the most common manifestation of osteoporosis. [PEER-PH][PEER-CS]
- MRI is the test of choice for suspected occult hip fracture — up to 10% of hip fractures are invisible on plain x-ray; for suspected pelvic fracture, computed tomography (CT) is required because pelvic x-ray sensitivity is under 85%. [PEER-PH]
- Pathological fracture most commonly involves the femur; breast, lung, and prostate cancer account for most cases, while liver cancer is least likely to cause one; lytic lesions release calcium and produce hypercalcaemia. [PEER-PH]
- Osteosarcoma is the most common primary bone malignancy of adolescence, favours the metaphysis of the distal femur, and produces a sunburst periosteal reaction with a raised alkaline phosphatase; Ewing sarcoma arises in the diaphysis with an onion-peel periosteal reaction and a median age of 15 years. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Pain is the most common first symptom of a malignant bone tumour; pain that worsens over time or wakes the patient from sleep is a red flag.
- Slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) affects obese boys aged 10 to 15 years, shows a widened physis on x-ray, is evaluated with a frog-leg pelvis view, and is managed with non-weight-bearing status and surgical pinning; 20% to 40% are bilateral at presentation. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Fluoroquinolones — levofloxacin, ciprofloxacin, ofloxacin — cause tendinopathy and spontaneous tendon rupture, affecting the Achilles in 90% of cases; risk rises with age over 60, corticosteroid use, renal dysfunction, and transplantation. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Carpal tunnel syndrome is median nerve compression; first-line treatment is a removable neutral wrist splint. Phalen (wrist flexed for 1 minute), Tinel (percussion over the volar wrist), and Durkan (pressure over the transverse carpal ligament for 30 seconds, 64% sensitive and 83% specific) are the bedside tests. [PEER-CS][ROSH]
- Flexor tenosynovitis is diagnosed by the Kanavel signs — fusiform swelling, tenderness along the flexor tendon sheath, finger held in flexion, and pain with passive extension — and requires intravenous antibiotics and hand surgeon incision and drainage. [PEER-CS][ROSH]

## Core Concepts

### Bone death: avascular necrosis and osteonecrosis
- AVN is disruption of the blood supply to bone causing cell death and collapse; the femoral head has a precarious supply and is the most commonly affected site.
- Traumatic causes: femoral neck fracture and hip dislocation — hence prompt reduction of a dislocated hip in the emergency department (ED). [PEER-PH]
- Non-traumatic causes: sickle cell disease, Gaucher disease, glucocorticoids, heavy alcohol use, kidney disease, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), radiation, and decompression disease; idiopathic in children as Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Presentation is insidious hip, groin, thigh, or buttock pain without infectious symptoms; early disease may be asymptomatic. Management principle: make the patient non-weight-bearing and refer urgently for orthopaedic follow-up.

### Bone and joint infection
- Osteomyelitis is an inflammatory process of bone caused by an organism; it occurs by haematogenous spread or by direct extension from a contiguous site, the latter accounting for about 80% of adult cases.
- Children: haematogenous spread to the metaphysis of long bones. A thin periosteum, low oxygen tension near the growth plate, and transphyseal vessels favour bacterial growth and spread. [PEER-PH]
- Adults: compromised hosts — immunosuppression, intravenous drug use, diabetes, vascular disease, prostheses, and recent instrumentation.
- Discitis is infection of the vertebral disc space, usually with vertebral osteomyelitis; abscess may form in the epidural space or paravertebral tissues.
- Septic arthritis is a true orthopaedic emergency; the synovial membrane lacks a protective basement membrane, so bacteria enter the joint space readily. Hand infections form a spectrum from superficial to deep: paronychia, felon, then flexor tenosynovitis, which can spread to bone. [PEER-PH]

### Spinal pain: four patterns to separate
- Mechanical or nonspecific back pain: no radicular or neurological deficit, and most cases resolve within a few weeks.
- Radicular pain (sciatica): shooting or burning pain along a dermatome, most often from disc herniation compressing a nerve root.
- Neurogenic claudication from spinal stenosis: bilateral leg symptoms worse with walking, standing, and lumbar extension, relieved by rest, sitting, lying, or flexion at the waist.
- Compression syndromes: CES, conus medullaris syndrome, and spinal epidural abscess — all neurological emergencies requiring MRI and urgent surgical or medical treatment.
- Inflammatory back pain: gradual onset before age 45, morning stiffness lasting over 30 minutes, night pain, and improvement with activity — the spondyloarthropathy pattern. [ROSH]

### The acutely swollen joint
- Monoarticular arthritis must be treated as a septic joint until proven otherwise; delay in diagnosis increases morbidity and complication rates. [ROSH]
- Arthrocentesis with synovial fluid analysis is the decisive step in every undifferentiated hot joint, and it is also therapeutic. [ROSH]
- Four categories matter: septic, crystal-induced, inflammatory, and haemorrhagic. Gout and pseudogout are separated by crystal type and birefringence; rheumatoid arthritis and Lyme disease give inflammatory fluid without crystals.

### Muscle: injury, inflammation, and rhabdomyolysis
- A strain is injury to the musculotendinous unit from excessive force, graded from mild swelling and pain (first degree) to complete separation of muscle or tendon from bone (third degree). [ROSH]
- Inflammatory myopathies — polymyositis and dermatomyositis — cause symmetric proximal weakness of the hips, shoulders, and neck with sparing of sensation, reflexes, and ocular muscles; dermatomyositis adds Gottron papules and heliotrope rash. [ROSH]
- Rhabdomyolysis is skeletal muscle breakdown with release of myoglobin and intracellular contents, causing third-spacing, hyperkalaemia, and acute kidney injury (AKI). [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Acute compartment syndrome is a pressure crisis within a fascial compartment, most often after a tibial diaphyseal fracture.

### Fragility and pathological fracture
- Osteoporosis is low bone mass with microarchitectural disruption; advanced age, female sex, low body weight, previous fracture, glucocorticoids, alcohol, and tobacco raise fracture risk. [PEER-PH]
- Fragility fractures occur from minimal trauma; the distal radius follows a fall onto an outstretched arm, while vertebral compression fractures are the most common osteoporotic fracture overall. Pathological fracture occurs through bone weakened by metastatic disease or myeloma, with minor or inconsistent trauma.

### Bone and soft tissue tumours
- Osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma are the aggressive primary bone tumours of adolescents and young adults; pain is the commonest presenting symptom.
- Rhabdomyosarcoma is the most common childhood soft-tissue sarcoma, usually before age 7. Benign lesions — osteochondroma and unicameral bone cyst — matter chiefly because they cause mechanical symptoms or pathological fracture. [PEER-PH]

### Paediatric hip and congenital musculoskeletal disease
- Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) is diagnosed clinically before 6 months with the Ortolani and Barlow manoeuvres and imaged with ultrasound. [ROSH]
- SCFE, Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease, transient synovitis, and septic arthritis of the hip are the four causes of the limping child that must be separated.
- Osteogenesis imperfecta is a genetic bone fragility disorder with extraskeletal features including hearing loss and dentinogenesis imperfecta; low-impact fractures are its hallmark. [PEER-PH]
- Craniosynostosis is premature closure of skull sutures and can cause raised intracranial pressure; Marfan syndrome carries a risk of aortic dilatation and dissection.

### Overuse, entrapment, and the hand
- Tendinopathy follows repetitive eccentric contraction; fluoroquinolone exposure is the drug cause. [PEER-CS]
- Bursitis affects superficial bursae — olecranon, prepatellar, subacromial, anserine — and is septic when erythema and warmth accompany the swelling.
- Entrapment neuropathies: median nerve at the carpal tunnel, ulnar nerve at the cubital tunnel, radial nerve with prolonged pressure, peroneal nerve at the fibular head.
