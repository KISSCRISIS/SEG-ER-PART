## High-Yield Exam Review

- Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is the most common skin cancer; melanoma accounts for only 4–5% of skin cancers but causes the most deaths of any skin cancer. [ROSH]
- BCC: slow-growing, translucent-to-pearly papule or nodule with raised, rolled borders and telangiectasias, on sun-exposed head, neck, and scalp; may ulcerate centrally and bleed with minor trauma. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- BCC risk factors: ultraviolet (UV) exposure (particularly UVB), pale skin, advanced age, smoking, immunosuppression, ionizing radiation, albinism, xeroderma pigmentosum, and outdoor occupations such as farming. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is a malignancy of epidermal keratinocytes; risk factors are UV light, human papillomavirus (HPV), chronic arsenic exposure, ionizing radiation, and immunosuppression after solid-organ transplant. Actinic keratoses are a precursor. [ROSH]
- In older adults, 75% of neck masses are neoplastic; the most common cause of a unilateral neck mass is SCC of the upper aerodigestive tract metastatic to cervical lymph nodes. [PEER-PH]
- Melanoma mnemonic ABCDE: Asymmetry, irregular Border, Color variation, Diameter greater than 6 mm, Evolution. Any new mole appearing after age 35 needs dermatologic evaluation. [ROSH]
- For localized (stage I/II) cutaneous melanoma, Breslow thickness (depth of the lesion) is the single most important prognostic factor for survival; for stage III disease the amount of regional lymph node involvement is the main predictor. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- UV exposure is thought to contribute to 90% of melanoma cases in the United States; tanning-bed UV can be fifteen times stronger than midday sun. [PEER-PH]
- Acral lentiginous melanoma arises on palmar, plantar, and subungual surfaces; Hutchinson sign (extension of pigment to the proximal or lateral nail fold) is pathognomonic. [PEER-PH]
- Kaposi sarcoma is an AIDS-related vascular tumor caused by HHV-8 and associated with CD4 counts below 200; lesions are firm, painless, elliptical purple macules along skin tension lines on the face, genitalia, and lower extremities, and biopsy is required for definitive diagnosis. [PEER-PH]
- Cellulitis involves the deep dermis and subcutaneous fat; erysipelas involves the upper dermis and superficial lymphatics and shows well-demarcated borders and a peau d'orange appearance. [ROSH]
- Group A *Streptococcus* is the most common cause of cellulitis; *Staphylococcus aureus* is implicated less commonly. [ROSH]
- Uncomplicated cellulitis in a patient without significant comorbidity is treated with 5–6 days of oral antibiotics directed against streptococci, such as a first-generation cephalosporin (cephalexin). [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Ultrasound separates cellulitis from abscess: cobblestoning indicates cellulitis; a well-circumscribed hypoechoic centrally avascular fluid collection indicates abscess and warrants incision and drainage. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Simple abscesses in otherwise healthy patients are treated with incision and drainage alone. Systemic antibiotics are indicated for immunosuppression, failure to resolve after drainage, signs of systemic infection (systemic inflammatory response syndrome criteria), extremes of age, or recurrent abscesses. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Necrotizing soft tissue infection: pain out of proportion to examination, crepitus, haemorrhagic bullae, dusky skin, and spread over hours. The gold standard for diagnosis is direct visualization of the fascial planes in the operating room. [ROSH]
- Type 1 necrotizing soft tissue infection is polymicrobial in approximately 55–75% of cases (gram-positive cocci, gram-negative rods, anaerobes); type 2 is monomicrobial in 20–30%, most often group A *Streptococcus* or methicillin-resistant *S. aureus* (MRSA). [ROSH]
- Empiric therapy for necrotizing fasciitis is intravenous piperacillin-tazobactam plus vancomycin plus clindamycin, given alongside immediate surgical consultation. [ROSH]
- Clindamycin is added for its antitoxin effect: it mitigates exotoxin production and limits tissue destruction even in organisms resistant to its antimicrobial action. [PEER-PH]
- Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome (SSSS) results from hematogenous spread of exfoliative toxins A and B from a distant *S. aureus* focus; the toxins cleave desmoglein 1 in the upper epidermis. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- SSSS: tender erythroderma beginning around the mouth, positive Nikolsky sign, and sparing of the intraoral mucous membranes; a preceding upper respiratory infection is common. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Pemphigus vulgaris causes flaccid bullae, mucosal involvement (present in almost all patients, often preceding skin lesions), and a positive Nikolsky sign; it is caused by IgG autoantibodies against desmoglein-3 (± desmoglein-1) with acantholysis. [ROSH]
- Bullous pemphigoid causes tense bullae in patients over 60 years, preceded by a pruritic urticarial or papular prodrome, with a negative Nikolsky sign and mucosal involvement that is uncommon. [ROSH]
- Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) involves less than 10% of body surface area (BSA), SJS/TEN overlap 10–30%, and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) more than 30%; all show a positive Nikolsky sign and mucocutaneous involvement. [ROSH]
- Immunoglobulin A vasculitis (IgAV, formerly Henoch-Schönlein purpura) is a small-vessel vasculitis; classic triad is palpable purpura, arthritis, and abdominal pain. Urinalysis and creatinine are the key screening tests. [ROSH]
- Erythema nodosum is a delayed hypersensitivity panniculitis of the dermis and subcutaneous fat; the most common associated infection is streptococcal, and the most common bacterial organism is *Streptococcus pyogenes*. [ROSH]
- Scabies is treated with topical permethrin 5% cream left on for 8–14 hours and washed off, repeated in 1 week if symptoms persist. [PEER-PH]
- Venous stasis ulcers sit proximal to the medial or lateral malleolus; treatment is compression and elevation, and topical antibiotic creams are avoided because they cause allergic contact dermatitis. [PEER-PH]
- Pressure ulcers are staged 1–4: stage 2 is partial-thickness skin loss with a pink or red wound bed and no exposed adipose tissue; stage 3 is full-thickness skin loss with visible subcutaneous fat but no exposed bone, muscle, or tendon. [ROSH]
- In a pressure ulcer with systemic symptoms, MRI is the imaging modality of choice to exclude osteomyelitis, and blood cultures should be obtained when sepsis physiology is present. [PEER-PH]

## Core Concepts

### Skin cancer

- BCC arises from malignant transformation of epithelial cells, is slow-growing, and occurs on sun-exposed skin; the morpheaform (sclerosing) subtype presents as a flat, scar-like plaque with ill-defined borders, and pigmented variants may mimic melanoma. Diagnosis is by punch or shave biopsy. [ROSH]
- SCC arises from epidermal keratinocytes and most commonly affects the head, face, neck, lip, and ear, presenting as a well-defined red or brown thickened patch that may crust or ooze. [ROSH]
- Melanoma arises de novo in 70% of cases and from dysplastic nevi in 30%; it metastasises to lymph nodes, bone, liver, lung, and brain. It peaks between ages 20 and 45 and favours the lower extremities in women and the head, neck, and trunk in men. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Bacterial skin and soft tissue infection

- The clinical spectrum runs from impetigo and abscess through cellulitis and erysipelas to necrotizing fasciitis and toxic shock syndrome. Most infections begin at a break in the skin. [ROSH]
- Impetigo is a superficial, highly contagious infection of preschool and school-age children, most often caused by *S. aureus* with group A streptococci as a less common cause. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Necrotizing fasciitis involves the deeper soft tissues, destroying fascia and subcutaneous fat while the underlying muscle is often spared because of its robust vascular supply. It may be cryptogenic, with no evident portal of entry. [ROSH]
- Fournier gangrene is a necrotizing fasciitis of the perineum that includes female labial and perineal disease; diabetes and excessive alcohol use are the most common predisposing factors. [ROSH]
- Omphalitis is cellulitis of the umbilical stump and surrounding tissue, usually presenting before 14 days of life, most often caused by *S. aureus* and group A *Streptococcus*. [ROSH]
- Bite wounds are polymicrobial. Human bites implicate *S. aureus*, viridans streptococci, *Eikenella corrodens*, *Bacteroides*, and *Peptostreptococcus*; dog and cat bites implicate *Pasteurella multocida* plus skin flora and anaerobes. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Blistering diseases

- Autoimmune blistering disease is classified by cleavage plane. Pemphigus vulgaris splits intraepidermally (acantholysis) and produces flaccid, fragile bullae; bullous pemphigoid splits at the basement membrane zone (subepidermal) and produces tense bullae.
- Drug-induced severe cutaneous adverse reactions form a second group: SJS/TEN, and drug-induced hypersensitivity syndrome (DIHS, also called DRESS). DIHS is a type IV hypersensitivity reaction with T cell–mediated attack on native tissue and mortality above 10%. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- SSSS is a toxin-mediated, not autoimmune, blistering disorder: it is distinguished from SJS/TEN chiefly by the absence of mucosal (other than lip) involvement and by its epidemiology in young children. [ROSH]

### Eczematous and papulosquamous disease

- Atopic dermatitis (eczema) is a chronic relapsing pruritic disorder with epidermal barrier dysfunction in the stratum corneum; it belongs to the atopic triad with asthma and allergic rhinitis. [PEER-PH][ROSH]
- Contact dermatitis is either irritant (nonimmunologic, more common, confined to the exposure site) or allergic (type IV hypersensitivity, may spread beyond it). [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Psoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated disease with well-demarcated erythematous plaques bearing a thick silvery scale, symmetrically on scalp, extensor elbows, knees, and gluteal cleft. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Pityriasis rosea is a benign, self-limited papulosquamous eruption with a herald patch followed by smaller lesions along skin cleavage lines in a fir-tree pattern. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Seborrheic dermatitis produces salmon-coloured greasy patches with yellow scale in sebaceous-gland-rich areas (scalp, diaper area, axillae); *Pityrosporum* has been associated. [ROSH][PEER-PH]

### Vasculitis and purpura

- Purpura describes any skin eruption resulting from extravasated blood; petechiae are purpuric lesions smaller than 3 mm. Purpura may reflect minor trauma or life-threatening illness such as purpura fulminans. [PEER-PH]
- IgAV is the most common systemic vasculitis of childhood, arising from IgA-dominant immune complex deposition in small vessels with leukocytoclastic vasculitis. [ROSH]
- Hypersensitivity vasculitis (serum sickness and serum sickness–like reaction) is a small-vessel inflammation that follows antigen exposure by 7–10 days and resolves over days to weeks after the drug is stopped. [PEER-PH]
- Schistocytes on the peripheral blood smear are the feature that best distinguishes thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura from immune thrombocytopenia. [PEER-PH]

### Ulceration and pressure injury

- Lower-extremity ulcers are classified by location and mechanism: venous stasis (superior to the malleoli), neuropathic (plantar pressure points, punched out within a thick callus), and arterial insufficiency (distal, from poor perfusion). [PEER-PH]
- Pressure injury results from external forces plus host factors: immobility, malnutrition, decreased skin perfusion, and neurological disease causing sensory loss. [ROSH]
- A pressure ulcer is colonised with bacteria, but only wounds with evidence of active infection should be cultured and treated with antibiotics. [ROSH]

### Benign and vascular cutaneous lesions

- Lipoma is the most common benign skin tumour: a rubbery, mobile, painless subcutaneous fatty mass on trunk, neck, arms, or axillae that grows to a size then persists. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Epidermoid cyst is a keratin-filled, dermis-like sac with a central punctum, most often on face, neck, scalp, upper trunk, and back. [ROSH]
- Pyogenic granuloma (lobular capillary haemangioma) is a benign capillary proliferation following minor trauma in about one third of patients; it is common in children, pregnant women, and younger adults. [PEER-PH]
- Infantile haemangioma is the most common tumour of infancy; cutaneous lesions in a beard distribution (chin and anterior neck) increase the risk of a concomitant airway haemangioma. [PEER-PH]

### Sexually transmitted and viral cutaneous disease

- HPV is the most common sexually transmitted infection in the United States; types 6 and 11 cause condylomata acuminata, and about 1% of infections produce clinically apparent warts. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
- Herpes simplex virus (HSV)-1 causes most oral herpes and most herpetic whitlow in children; HSV-2 typically causes genital herpes. [ROSH][PEER-PH]
